Edit by hand: options

Owner, voice, 11 October 2026: Locked the earlier rule that only the assistant may author is reversed for editing. The assistant is still how a recipe is started; everything in a version can now be edited by hand in Kitchie: name, ingredients, steps, notes, tags and other fields, and photos (add, replace, remove, make banner, crop, rotate). The data model does not change: the same version, revision and change-set model, the same Recipe service methods. This is a flow and layout mockup.

Rules carried over, not reopened: a try edits in place; a keeper asks at save (fix this one, keep both, or a different dish; V2 C); every save is one revision with per-change undo; nothing is hard-deleted (undo only); an assistant edit and a hand edit land in the same History with who did it shown; one 4,000 character scrolling note box; one banner per version, one photo per step (at most 10), gallery 3; no originals kept; a pending photo shows "Photo is being prepared".

Each option below is for you to say yay, nay or combine. The Recommended one is marked, and it is the one the built screens use. Open the edit screen on a phone, or at 1280 wide for the desktop layout. The Mockup button on that screen switches the options that can be switched.

E-O1 · Where you edit

1One edit screen, opened at the section you tappedRecommended

A. One edit screen for the version. Details, Ingredients, Method, Notes and Photos on one scrolling screen with a docked jump row, one Save. A pencil in the top row (key E) opens it at the top; a pencil beside each section on the recipe page opens it at that section. One change set, one revision, one keeper question. Costs: a long screen on a phone; the jump row and the section pencils take the edge off.
B. Edit in place, field by field. Tap any text on the recipe page and it becomes a box; it saves when you leave it. Fastest for a typo. Costs: every field is its own revision (History fills with typo fixes) or the page needs a hidden "pending" state; a keeper would ask its question on every field; easy to edit by a stray tap while cooking.
C. A sheet per section. The pencil beside Ingredients opens an Ingredients sheet with its own Save. Costs: a change that touches an ingredient and its step is two saves, two revisions and two keeper questions; five save flows to build.
Combine. A is the default. B could be added later for notes only, where one field per revision is natural.

E-O2 · Ingredients and steps on a phone: typing and reordering

2Three fields per ingredient; Reorder mode with move arrowsRecommended

Typing: every ingredient is a name, an amount and a unit (a plain list, or "(count)"), plus an "Amount?" switch for when the source did not say. Nothing is parsed from a sentence and no amount is guessed. Each row has its own remove button; a saved row you remove stays on screen, struck through, with Put back, and is a change in the revision (History can undo it). A row you added and never saved is just dropped.

A. Reorder mode, move arrows. A "Reorder" button above the list turns every row into one short line with up and down arrows; "Done" goes back to editing. Big targets, nothing to drag, no accidental moves while you scroll. A step's photo moves with the step. Costs: two taps to move a row a long way; fine for the 5 to 12 rows a recipe has.
B. A row menu. A ⋯ button on each row: Move up, Move down, Remove. Leaves rows tall and tidy. Costs: a sheet for every move.
C. Drag handle. Press and hold a handle, drag the row. Quickest for a long list. Costs: fights with scrolling on a touch screen, needs arrows as the keyboard and switch fallback anyway, and is the hardest to get right; not drawn.
D. One typed line per ingredient ("300 g rice, cooked and cooled") that is parsed. Nay by default: needs a parser, and parsing is the "inventing amounts" the model rules out. Not drawn.
Desktop has room: one line per row, arrows and remove always visible, no Reorder mode.

E-O3 · Crop and rotate a photo

3Rotate, and a fixed frame you move and zoomRecommended

The honest part, shown on the screen itself: Recipe keeps no original. The banner is stored at large (1600 px wide) and every other photo at medium (960 px) plus a square. So crop and rotate act on the stored copy: what you crop away is gone for good once the 5 minutes of undo end, a rotate re-encodes the file, and zooming in leaves fewer pixels. The screen shows the size it will save at, in plain words ("Sharp", "a little soft on a big screen", "small for a banner: it will look soft"). Like any photo change it applies at once and is not part of Save.

A. Rotate only (two 90 degree buttons). Cannot lose pixels except by one re-encode. Covers the most common mistake, a sideways phone photo. Cannot fix a badly framed shot.
B. Rotate plus a fixed-shape crop. The frame is the shape the photo is stored in (4 to 3); you drag the photo under it and zoom with a slider; the square tile is made from the middle of what you keep. Works the same by touch, mouse and keyboard (arrow keys move, the slider zooms). Costs: one more screen to build, and zoom can make a photo soft (hence the size line).
C. Free crop (drag the corners to any shape). Stored photos are all 4 to 3, so an odd crop would have to be padded or stretched. Nay by default; not drawn.
Combine. B contains A. If you want the smaller build, take A first and add the frame later.

E-O4 · How the keeper question appears

4A sheet when you tap SaveRecommended

A. A sheet at Save. Save lists what you changed (up to 4 rows, "and N more") above three cards: Fix With peas (revision N, everyone cooking it gets the fix), Keep both (a new version that starts as Trying; you name it) and It is a different dish now (its own recipe, linked back). Nothing is preselected; one optional line in your own words (stored as the revision's why, or user_intent for a new version). On a try there is no question: Save goes straight in as the next revision, with an Undo. Costs: one extra layer on a keeper.
B. Inline above the Save bar. Save first expands the three choices in the bar; pick one; Save again. Fewer layers. Costs: cramped at 390 wide, and no room for the change list or the optional words.
C. Choose first, before editing. "Editing: fix this one / make a copy". Not "asks at save" (V2 C), and a wrong early pick means a lot to redo. Not drawn.
Desktop: the question is already in the changes lane once there is a change, so no sheet at all; Save names the choice ("Save as revision 4").

E-O5 · The desktop layout (1280)

5The form with a changes lane beside itRecommended

A. Form on the left, a lane on the right that lists each change as you make it, says who is saving ("Sam, by hand"), holds the keeper question and Save. Same markup and classes as the phone; one extra file, fetched only at 1024 px and up. Sheets become centred dialogs; crop shows the photo on the left and its controls on the right. The Recipes tab is not drawn on desktop yet (handoff X13), so this is a plain wide window, not the Pantry's rail layout.
B. The phone column, centred. Least to build, lots of empty screen, the change list hidden behind Save.
C. A nested flyout over the recipe page, like the Pantry item edit. The best fit once desktop Recipes exists (X13); then the lane's content moves into the flyout. Not drawn yet.

Smaller choices I made where you were silent
  1. E-D1A hand edit on a keeper needs no "To review". A person made it with the change list in front of them; only assistant edits open a review (V4 B). Wrong guess: a review step on every hand edit, which is noisy. Proposal
  2. E-D2The banner can be replaced or swapped for another photo, not removed (the photos slice's existing rule: one banner per version, always). Alternative: allow remove, falling back to the parent's banner or the stock picture. Proposal
  3. E-D3Photos apply at once and are not part of Save; undo for 5 minutes (P6, P10). An inherited photo is the parent's: Replace or crop gives this version its own copy and the parent keeps its photo; there is no Remove for it. Proposal
  4. E-D4Save does nothing until something changed; leaving with changes asks (Keep editing, Discard). Ctrl or Cmd plus Enter closes the top layer as everywhere (T3); on this screen it asks the same question and never saves silently. Proposal
  5. E-D5Amounts are typed for the recipe's own serving count; Recipe stores them per serving (the build converts). A batch recipe is typed for the whole pot. Proposal
  6. E-D6Editing a note that was written higher up rewords it for this version and below (the versions slice's update op); the parent keeps its own. Taking a note off a version is a change in the revision, with Put back before Save and undo after. Proposal
  7. E-D7Two people editing at once: Save carries the revision you started from (patch_version's base revision). If someone saved first the screen says so ("Jane saved revision 5 while you were editing") and offers Apply mine on top or Start again. Not drawn; a build rule. Proposal
  8. E-D8One History, who is shown: a hand edit reads "Edited by Sam" with a "by hand" mark; an assistant edit keeps its "assistant" mark and "on behalf of" name. See History Proposal
  9. E-D9Keys: E opens Edit from the recipe and the version page, comma opens History (a pencil and a clock in the top row carry the hints), Ctrl or Cmd plus Enter closes. No Recipe-specific keys (T3). Proposal
