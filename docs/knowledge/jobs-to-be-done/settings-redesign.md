## Job

Make Kitchie look and behave the way this person wants, once, without hunting. Today Settings tries to be five jobs at once: how it looks (theme, text, emoji, motion), what a Pantry row shows, how pushy the assistant is, the household's structure, and account actions. The person's real jobs are fewer: (1) pick a look, (2) tell the app who and what the household is, (3) connect an assistant, (4) decide how often the app asks questions, (5) get my data out. Text size, motion and dark at night are the phone's jobs.

## How we know the person is in this job

They tap the avatar (then Looks, Preferences, Household) or the desktop window's gutter. It is almost always a one-time visit after sign-up or after a specific annoyance. **I:** nobody visits Settings daily; no usage data exists for any knob (platform telemetry counts household-active days only).

## What leads

On the phone: the avatar menu is the real front door (11 rows). The Settings overview page adds nothing the menu does not already have except Sample items (F: `profile-menu.ts` and `app.ts renderSettingsPage` both call `appSharedRowsHtml`). Looks is the only screen with display knobs.

## What this job does not need

Per-person row-detail switches, a second reduce-motion switch, a size control that moves one list, a Reset button for them, a bell switch, a Settings overview page that repeats the menu. DESIGN.md section 1: "A job does not have to show everything we know about an item", the job decides, not a preference. Precedents for deleting a knob: the desktop shortcut-cue switch (12 Oct), the Light/Dark/Follow choice (9 to 10 Oct), the old time-zone setting (D18, inferred instead).

## Owner decisions, 23 Oct 2026 (voice review of A to D; recorded in Kitchie #1090)

Closely paraphrased from his review; these override the earlier recommendation of A.

1. **The default is a hybrid.** The normal, usual-flow Settings is small, opinionated and curated (A's short list is the front door). **Search reaches everything else**, including advanced settings and the knobs A would have removed. Removed from the front does not mean deleted: some stay search-only. He picked from A, B, C and D for the default; the combined shape is E. A to D stay in the page for comparison; **E is the recommended default**.
2. **Search is the product** and has to be "super, super good". It must cover the many ways people look for things (synonyms, plain-language phrases, typos: "too small", "bigger text", "font", "dark", "night", "colour", "preference") and say what each setting affects, where it lives and its current value, and let the simple ones be changed inline. What is **searchable** (everything, including advanced) is distinct from what is **shown in the usual flow** (curated only).
3. **One pinnable area on the front of Settings, made of several pinnable segments.** The owner decides which settings are pinnable; not everything is. For now: (a) **Looks** = themes + light/dark mode + emoji together (he loved the Looks design in the earlier options), (b) **text size**. Pinning adds a shortcut only; the setting stays in its normal place and in search; unpinning loses nothing. It is a widget-like panel on the front. Unpinned, Looks lives in search and the profile menu. How pinned and unpinned look, how to pin, the empty state and phone versus desktop were left to the designer (Proposals SR-P13 to SR-P14).
4. **Text size comes back as a real control, whole-app, not Pantry only** (that is the Kitchie #1089 bug; not repeated). It must be shown on sample screens (Home, Pantry, Shopping) changing together. He liked, from B, "Match my phone" for text size with "Standard", and the Rows choice "Names only / Standard" for row detail. How these relate to the old item-name-size setting is below.
5. **He asked where Reset to defaults ends up.** Answered explicitly: in E, search only, and it never touches pins or household data. In A: gone. B: "Reset looks" under More. C: a search result. D: "Back to default" in the Aa popover, for that list only.
6. **The mockup must be broader**: he wants to search for anything and "get a feel for how various things look when I search them". Hence a 52-entry index (all 35 audited settings plus household name, members, invites, categories, locations, install, sign out, assistant connections, the bell, stock and minimum settings, time zone, units, motion, reset, food rules and avoids, light/dark, theme names, emoji), a real input with example chips, and a 17-state gallery.

## Owner decisions, 24 Oct 2026 (phone polish after his live review of UAT 0.167.0; Kitchie #1110, built in kitchie PR #1114, 0.171.0)

Voice review plus a screenshot of the pinned Looks and Text size cards. **All five are decided**; the build went to UAT first and this mockup follows it (`fragments/settings-redesign`, option E; phone and the desktop frame draw the same controls, the build is phone only).

1. **The pin is only an icon.** No "Pin" or "Pinned" words on the cards, the Looks and Text size pages or the search results. Filled icon = pinned, outline = not; the accessible name says "Pin Looks to Shortcuts" / "Unpin Looks from Shortcuts"; 44px. The dashed add chip shows the segment name beside an outline pin (**Proposal**: an icon alone would not say which setting it adds).
2. **The Emoji setting is removed.** "If it has one, it shows; if no emoji is set, nothing shows. Take it off." Out of Looks, the pin set, Reset and the index (a search for "emoji" now finds a "Not a setting" explanation, K04 stays covered). In the build the server stops reading `kitchie_emoji` everywhere, so Pantry, Home (phone and desktop, with their searches), Shopping and the shopper link, Plan, Recipe ideas, Locations and Categories show an emoji exactly when the item or area has one; stored emoji data is untouched; an old cookie or stored `showEmoji` is ignored.
3. **Theme swatches show the theme's real look**: one tile, a mini preview (background, card, accent, text) with the theme's name underneath on one line. Ten different looks, light and dark, checked in `tools/settings-redesign-check.mjs`.
4. **The pinned Looks card stays one row for themes**: all of them when they fit, otherwise a sideways scroll-snap row ("rotary"; the next tile peeks in, a soft fade on the side that has more, labelled radiogroup so arrow keys work). System, Light and Dark stay in the card (their label is dropped there to keep it compact). The Looks page keeps the full grid.
5. **Text size is a one-line stepper** (replaces the four-option row of decision 4 above; "Standard" is dropped): `A-` | "Match my phone" with seven dots | `A+`. Middle = the phone's own size (step 0); three taps smaller, three larger, disabled at the ends. **Proposal** (defaults): larger +10%, +20%, +30%; smaller -8%, -16%, -24% of the phone's own size. The label is drawn at the applied size. Old stored values migrate (match 0, standard 0, larger +2, largest +3). Search still finds Text size from "bigger text", "too small", "hard to see".

## Settings shake-up, 24 Oct 2026 (owner's verdict on UAT 0.171.0; Kitchie #1110, #1090)

Mockup: `fragments/settings-shakeup/` (live: <https://basement-bridge.github.io/mockup/fragments/settings-shakeup/>; `?o=A|B|C`, `&bare=1` for the phone full screen). Phone only. Check: `tools/settings-shakeup-check.mjs`. Nothing built, nothing approved for build.

**Owner, voice, on the live UAT build 0.171.0 of option E.** "The whole settings feel, I think it looks like it's going worse than what it is now." "It seems very Java ME like" (dense old-school menu lists drilling through levels). "Don't know why you've got stuff" (elements with no clear reason). "I thought the intent of the shortcuts is that it's readily available, I don't think you're getting the intent right": shortcuts are the **control itself**, small and one tap (current theme with a tap to change, a text-size stepper), **not** a full copy of the Looks page stacked at the top. "You have to look at a way to shake up the settings; nothing changes": stop patching the same structure, go back to what Settings is for. Principles: "User experience is paramount. Less is more." Screen space is precious; every element earns its place; he has vision issues (strong contrast, 44px targets, scalable text). Earlier verdict on search results (also bad): tall blocks with pills and AFFECTS / LIVES IN lines.

**Job.** Settings is opened for three things. (1) Make the app comfortable to read: theme, light or dark, bigger text. (2) Look after the household: who is in, the invite link, the shape of the kitchen. (3) Rarely, tune the assistant. Everything else is something the person knows the name of and searches for. So the comfort controls are **controls, not a place**; there are three or four plain areas; search is excellent and everywhere.

**What stands (earlier owner decisions, unchanged).** Search must be excellent (synonyms, plain language, typos) and reach every setting from anywhere in Settings, current page first ("On this page", then "Everywhere else"); pin is an icon only, no pin words; no Emoji setting; text size is the seven-step stepper centred on Match my phone (3 smaller -8/-16/-24%, 3 larger +10/+20/+30%); themes are visibly distinct named swatches; per-device client-side storage; one canonical name per setting.

### Recommendation: B, with the trade-offs (**chosen by the owner, 24 Oct 2026; see "Settings, the chosen design"**; SR-Q36; questions SR-Q36 to SR-Q41 are in the list below)

Smallest front (11 controls against 24); the three comfort controls are exactly where he said the intent of shortcuts is (small, one tap, the control itself, on the front); it leads with search, which he called the product; no page exists to hold nothing. **Risks:** the controls live only on the front (two taps from the app); a big search field is 56px that earns nothing if few people search (no usage data, none collected, SR-Q14); Kitchen setup folds into Household, two rows longer; deleting the Looks page and pins removes something just shipped to UAT (per-device pins vanish quietly, nothing lost); Preferences is untouched and is now the heaviest screen left; taps do not improve, so this is a judgement about clarity, not speed; not tried on a real phone or with a screen reader. **Fallback:** A is deletion only and can grow B's big search later; C is a small step from B if he wants controls on every page.

## Search index design

Search is a product feature here, not a filter. Everything in `search-index.js` is plain data that the app would generate from its setting definitions, so a setting cannot exist without an entry (SR-P22).

**Fields (one row per thing a person can look for).** `id`; `label` (what the result is called); `synonyms` (what people type: single words, plain-language phrases, old names); `aka` (old audit labels this replaces); `affects` (one sentence: what changing it changes); `scope` (device, member, household, account, or "not a setting"); `where` (path in the app); `value` (key of the current value, resolved at draw time); `advanced` flag (search-only: on no page); `control` (inline: theme, mode, emoji, text, rows, motion, bell, checks, reset, a theme name) or `destination` (the screen it opens) or `info` (explains, no control); `pin` (the segment it can be pinned as); `k` (audit ids covered); `pri` (tie-break).

**Size.** 52 entries (42 settings and screens, 10 theme names), 663 synonym phrases, 10 flagged Advanced. `search-index.js` is 20.5 KB raw, about 7.1 KB gzipped (F, measured). Loaded on first use of search, not on page load (DESIGN.md section 6).

**How a query is read.** Lower-cased; apostrophes dropped; small words ignored (the, my, to, how, make, change, turn, off, ...); plurals folded. A word nobody has used anywhere in the index is replaced by the nearest word someone has (one edit up to six letters, two for longer; a swapped pair counts as one), and the result says so ("Showing results for dark mode") with a way back to the exact text.

**How it ranks.** Exact name 100; an old name (`aka`) or a name containing every word 92; an exact synonym or phrase 90; a phrase inside a longer sentence 72; then word by word (name 30, synonym 22, prefix a little less, one-letter-off a little less again, description and place 6). A result needs at least half of the words to hit. Advanced results sit 4 points lower so an everyday answer wins a tie. A theme named like a mode ("Kitchie Night") is capped so "night" leads with Light or dark. Then the curated order breaks ties. Six results at a time, then "Show more".

**What a result row says.** Name; why it matched ("Exact match", "Also called 'bigger text'", "Was 'Item name size', now part of Text size"); an **Advanced · search only** badge when it is on no page; scope (This device, You, Whole household, Your account, Not a setting); **Affects** (what it changes); **Lives in** (where) and **Now** (current value); then the control. Simple settings are changed in the row (switch, segmented choice, theme swatches, theme name, Reset). Larger ones say where they live and open it. Things that are not settings (time zone, units, notifications) explain instead of leaving a dead end. **Phone:** stacked in that order. **Desktop:** two columns, text left, control right, sample screens beside.

**States drawn (17, in the gallery, and live in the phone and desktop box).** Exact (`text size`); synonym (`bigger text`); typo (`drak mode`); vague phrase (`too small`); vague with several answers (`hard to see`: Text size, Light or dark, Theme); advanced, search only (`animations`); a colour word (`orange`: two themes); jumps to a screen (`food rules`); many results (`household`); an old name (`item name size`); a merged switch (`use-by date`); Reset (`undo changes`); not a setting (`time zone`); no match (`flux capacitor`: closest ideas, browse by group, tell us); empty box (suggestions); empty box with recent searches; the front with nothing pinned. Twelve example chips above the phone fill the phone and the desktop box.

**Keyboard.** `/` focuses search (from anywhere in E); Up and Down move a highlight (the best match starts highlighted); Enter uses it (toggles a switch, moves into a choice, opens a screen); Esc clears the box, a second Esc leaves it. The box is a combobox with `aria-activedescendant`. Open build question: results contain controls, so a listbox is a compromise (SR-Q29).

**Cost to build and keep true (SR-Q26).** Client-side, no server, no new data store, no telemetry. Engine about 150 lines (tokenise, fold, correct, score), plus an index generated from setting definitions, plus tests: every setting has an entry, each of the 17 states returns its expected first result, no console errors. I: about 3 to 5 engineer-days for a first version; the recurring cost is keeping synonyms true as settings change, which the generated index and one test per setting make a review item, not a hunt. Risks: a synonym that sends someone to the wrong setting (mitigated by the test list and "Tell us" on a miss); search too slow (not an issue at 52 entries: under 5 ms in the mockup).

## Code audit

Settings in the app today is four surfaces: the **Looks** screen (device display knobs), the **Settings** overview (phone: `/settings`; desktop: the window's Settings pane), **Preferences** (Mine and Household: stock-check dials, food, notes, proposals; about 8,800 lines in `server/src/preferences/` plus 12 assets, owner-designed and out of scope except placement) and the **people pages** (Me, Household). UAT and production run in platform mode: household name and members are Platform's, and My assistant is on Profile > AI assistants (`assistantOnProfile = platformOn`); standalone mode keeps the Settings-page versions (F).

Facts that frame the table:
- **Age (F, git log).** The repo's history starts 29 Sep 2026. The display knobs K03 to K11 were added 1 Oct 2026 for issue #73 ("browser-only settings", stage 1 and 2), for a single list view. `settings.js` was last touched 10 Oct (themes). Since 13 Oct the repo has 558 commits; none changed what these knobs reach.
- **Where it is stored (F).** Display knobs: one localStorage key `kitchie.settings` (per device, per browser, per environment since #1056 on 22 Oct, so UAT started empty then). Two cookies leave the device so the server can paint first: `kitchie_theme`, `kitchie_emoji`. Member prefs and the assistant choice are in `stock_prefs`; `kitchie_assistant` and `kitchie_zone` are cookie mirrors. The Looks note says "Only your theme and the emoji setting also travel with you"; in code that means "sent to the server for first paint", not "follows you to another device" (I: the copy reads as cross-device; it is not).
- **Effect of Item name size, measured (F).** Test app (harness `test/web-harness.ts`) in headless Chromium at 390 px and 1366 px, four Pantry items. With `size: large`: Pantry list item names 16.96 px to 20.8 px (small 15.2 px), 11 text nodes change; Home 0 of 22, Shopping 0 of 14, Plan 0 of 157, Cooking 0 of 10, Preferences 0 of 53, menu 0 of 26 (phone). Desktop: Pantry 11 of 44, Home 0 of 37, Shopping 0 of 16. History had no rows in the fixture (I: its rows are `li .name` so the rule would reach them). The browser test asserts `data-size="large"` is set (`browser/settings-screens.browser.ts`), never a font size, so it cannot fail when the setting does nothing. The segment is labelled Normal where the report says Medium (F: `settings-mockup.test.ts`). Whether the Android report is this, or a stale UAT store after #1056, is #1089's to say.
- **Other effects measured (F).** Show the spot: Pantry meta "1 · Shelf · Meat" becomes "1"; Show the amount: becomes "Shelf"; use-by: visible `.due` 2 to 0 on the Pantry list; Compact rows: row height 70.4 px to 59.2 px, no font change. None of them reaches Home or Shopping.
