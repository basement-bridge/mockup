Recipe versions and lifecycle

Draft for feedback, 10 October 2026. One slice of the Recipe tab (the end-to-end flow is in recipe/). This one answers: what is "the recipe" to a person, when a change fixes the version you have and when it makes a new one, how a try becomes a keeper, what stays as an audit trail, and what the assistant may change on its own. Phone-width screens; sample data only; open Mockup (bottom right on every screen) to flip options or who is using it.

The model in one picture

Three layers. Only the bottom one is frozen; the two above it are allowed to move. That is the relaxation.

Recipe (a family). What a person means by "egg fried rice": one row in the list. It is named by, and opens, the household's usual version.
Version. One way of making it: "With peas", "Less soy, more garlic". Has a parent, a state (Suggested, Trying, Kept, Put away) and a current revision. Its name and state can change.
Revision. One saved edit of a version: the whole recipe as it stood, who saved it (and whether through an assistant), their words, when. Never changed, never deleted. Fixing a version adds a revision; going back adds one too.

A Ask at save: edit freely, then choose Fix this one, Keep both, or Different dish. B Ask first: choose before editing. C By state: a Trying version takes edits straight in (it is a try; nobody relies on it yet); a Kept one asks at save. Recommended: C. 4The assistant and a kept version

A Suggest only: an assistant may create and change Trying versions with the person's words; a change to a Kept version or the usual waits as a suggestion for a person. B Edit, review after: it saves straight in; the change shows once to check or undo. C Trusted per member: each member chooses A or B for their own assistant. Recommended: A; the usual is what everyone cooks.

ABC

5How far immutability is relaxed (backend)

A Frozen revisions: versions move, every revision is frozen. B Mutable rows with the event journal as the only trail. C Keep today's rule, add only states and the usual pointer. Recommended: A. Details on the spec page.

6What a revision stores (backend)

A The whole recipe per revision; the diff is worked out when shown. B A sparse change set against the parent's pinned revision (today's model, pinned). Recommended: A; editing in place makes inherited content fragile. Details on the spec page.

Decisions in this slice (all Proposal until the owner says)

Numbered V-D so they do not clash with R-D (flow slice) or other slices. The same list is in docs/knowledge/jobs-to-be-done/recipe-tab.md.

  1. V-D1Three layers: recipe (family), version, revision. Immutability moves down from the version to the revision. A version can be fixed in place; what it said before is never lost. Proposal
  2. V-D2Four states for a version: Suggested, Trying, Kept, Put away. Suggested = made by an assistant and nobody has said yes (out of lists, plans, ideas and search). Trying = in use, not yet judged. Kept = a keeper. Put away = hidden, kept in full. "Favourite" is not a state: it is each member's star (existing set_preference, made per member). Proposal
  3. V-D3Transitions. Suggested → Trying (a person says yes) or dismissed. Trying → Kept (Keep it), → Put away (Not for us). Kept → Trying (Back to trying), → Put away. Put away → Kept (Bring back). Making a Trying version the usual also keeps it. A spin-off ends a version's life here with a pointer to its new recipe. Proposal
  4. V-D4The state says itself in one slim band at the top of a version, with the one action that moves it on (Keep it, Bring back, Open). Kept and the usual need no band: the "Our usual" pill under the title says it. Same slim component as R-D12. Proposal
  5. V-D5"The recipe" is the family, named by and opening its usual. The list shows one row per family; Plan, Cook and Ideas open the usual unless someone picks another version. Every family has exactly one usual at all times; the first version is it until someone changes it. This is gap G4, answered. Proposal
  6. V-D6Stars are per member and never change what the household opens. Only the usual does. Proposal
  7. V-D7A version row reads: name, what it changed from its parent, who and when, cooked count and revision, then usual and state pills. An assistant's work shows a dashed ✦ avatar and "Alex's assistant". Proposal
  8. V-D8Versions is a screen, not a sheet. The R-D20 chip ("With peas · 4 versions") opens it. The flow slice's sheet stays fine for a quick switch; the screen holds states, put-away versions, suggestions and compare. Proposal
  9. V-D9Every lifecycle action is in the version's ⋯ sheet, and only the ones that apply show: Make it our usual, New version from this, Make it its own recipe, History, Compare with our usual, Keep it, Back to trying, Put away. Proposal
  10. V-D10Three kinds of change, in the person's words: Fix this one (next revision of the same version), Keep both (new version, parent = this one, starts Trying), It is a different dish now (spin off: a new recipe, linked back). Proposal
  11. V-D11Where a change goes is option 2 (recommended C: a try takes edits in place, a keeper asks at save). Either way the top band says where the change will land before you touch anything. Supersedes R-D16 Proposal
  12. V-D12A child version is pinned to the revision of its parent it was made from. A later fix to the parent is offered once on the child ("Original was fixed on 21 Sep · Bring it in"), never applied silently. Proposal
  13. V-D13History lists revisions newest first: what kind (Made, Fixed, Adjusted, Went back, Spun off), who (assistant marked), when, their words, and the diff in the R-D20 tint. Each older one has See it as it was and Go back to this. Going back saves a new revision; nothing is rewound. Proposal
  14. V-D14Compare is two columns with only what differs, the rest folded into one line ("5 ingredients the same, method the same"). Left is always the usual. Proposal
  15. V-D15Kept as an audit trail, never changed or deleted: revisions, the cook log (each cook points to a version and revision), the event journal, nutrition observations (attached to a revision), suggestions and their outcome, and spin-off links. Proposal
  16. V-D16Relaxed: a version's name, state, current revision and parent label; the family's name and usual; per-member stars. Every one of these changes is a journal event with who and when. Proposal
  17. V-D17A person's own words stay required where an assistant acts (create a version, change a try, accept nothing on a person's behalf). In the app, tapping Save is the intent; "why" is optional. Each revision stores which it was. Proposal
  18. V-D18Promoting a try is option 3 (recommended A: ask after every cook of a Trying version: Keep it, Needs work, Not for us). Keep it offers "Make it our usual" as a separate tick. Needs work can open Change on the try. Not for us puts it away with the date. It sits after R-D19's pantry and freezer questions, in the same single end-of-cook ask. Proposal
  19. V-D19Any member who can cook can answer, and the first answer counts. A later cook can change it. Followers (R-D8) are not asked; their cook is logged. Proposal
  20. V-D20Every revision and state change carries an author (a member) and a channel (app or assistant). An assistant is never an author on its own: it acts for one member and shows as "Alex's assistant". Answers gap G5 (added by). Proposal
  21. V-D21The assistant and kept versions is option 4 (recommended A: a Kept version or the usual is changed by an assistant only as a suggestion). An assistant may freely create Trying versions and fix Trying versions when it passes the person's words. Proposal
  22. V-D22Suggestions wait quietly: one slim line on the version and on Versions, and a Suggestions screen. Never a pop-up, never on Home (R-D12). Four answers: Apply (next revision), Keep both (new Trying version), Not now, No thanks. A whole new recipe from an assistant is a family whose only version is Suggested: this is R-D12's draft and answers gap G3 (drafts live in Recipe). Proposal
  23. V-D23Who may do what: any member who is not only following may make, fix, keep and put away versions. Changing the usual, and putting away a version others cooked, follow the household-admin rule where the household has admins (preferences D25 to D30); otherwise any member. Putting away the usual is blocked until another one is the usual. Proposal
  24. V-D24Two people saving at once never overwrite each other. A save names the revision it started from; if someone else saved first, the screen says so ("Jane saved a change 2 minutes ago") and shows both, and the person picks Save on top or Keep both. Proposal
  25. V-D25Spin off makes a new recipe whose first version is Trying, with "From Egg fried rice" on it; the old version stays, read-only, as "Spun off" with a link across. Proposal
  26. V-D26Word on screen is "version", never "variation". The API moves to "version" too, with the old tool names kept as aliases for a release (spec). Proposal
  27. V-D27Existing data migrates without loss: each recipe becomes a family with its "Original" version (Kept, usual) at revision 1; each variation becomes a version (Kept if active, Put away if disabled) whose revision 1 is its resolved content, pinned to its parent's revision 1; old ids keep working. Authors are "before members". Detail on the spec page. Proposal
  28. V-D28The cook log records version and revision (extends gap G2), so "what did we cook on the 8th" shows the recipe exactly as it was then, even after fixes. Proposal
  29. V-D29Nutrition belongs to a revision. A fix that changes ingredients marks the figures "for an earlier revision" until someone records new ones; nothing is recomputed by Recipe. Proposal
  30. V-D30Loading: the version page loads the version's head only. Versions, History, Compare and Suggestions are separate routes, fetched on tap. The ⋯ sheet and the Keep sheet build on tap. One small stylesheet (versions.css) and one small script on top of the flow slice's. Proposal
How this changes the flow slice's decisions
  1. R-D16"Changing a recipe always makes a new version" becomes V-D10 and V-D11: a try is fixed in place; a keeper asks.
  2. R-D17Stands. Naming from the change and the "why keep it" line are used for Keep both and spin off; a fix asks "what did you fix?" instead.
  3. R-D19Stands, with V-D18's three answers added for a Trying version; "Keep it as a version" for tonight's swap makes a Trying version.
  4. R-D11, R-D20"Make this version the default" is worded "Make it our usual"; the chip opens the Versions screen (V-D8).
  5. G3, G4, G5Answered by V-D22, V-D5 and V-D20. G2 is extended by V-D28.
What loads (DESIGN.md section 6)
  1. Default path (a version page): theme, the flow slice's recipe.css and recipe.js, then versions.css (about 7 KB) and versions.js (sample data and helpers). In the build: one get_version call for the head revision.
  2. Deferred: the family, history, compare and suggestion screens are routes, loaded on tap; history pages older revisions in on scroll. Sheets are built on tap.
  3. Stays close to the build: .st, .vr, .band, .rv, .df, .ch, .sug, .verd, .cmp are meant to be lifted beside the flow slice's classes. Sample names live only in versions.js.
Open questions
  • Options 1 to 6 above. A wrong guess on 2 or 4 changes what people trust; on 5 or 6 it costs a second migration.
  • Should "Not for us" after the first cook of a try put it away at once, or wait for a second opinion in a household of several cooks? Mockup: at once, undo in the toast.
  • Does a version page need its own photo and notes, or do they stay on the family (R-D24)? Mockup: notes on the family; cook photos on the cook log entry, so they show which version.
  • Is there ever a real delete (for example a try made by mistake a minute ago)? Mockup: no; Put away only. A hard delete for a version with no cooks and one revision is cheap to add later.
  • How long do dismissed suggestions stay visible to the member whose assistant made them? Mockup: in their assistant's history only.
