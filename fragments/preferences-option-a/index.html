Preferences, option A: one list

Fragment. Every preference in one list. Each row shows the value in force for you and where it comes from (built-in default, the household, or you). Open a row and a You / Household switch shows the other level. Fake data, tap everything. Option B is drawn separately.

The owner believes preferences are recorded per person (member) as well as per household. The model was read first (below), and this option is drawn from what it really holds.Owner belief, relayed 10 October 2026. A design-options request, not a build. Awaiting the owner’s approval; nothing here is decided.
Data

Some set shows every state: built-in default, from household, your choice over the household (quiet hours can be set to none), both read, household only. Any member can change the Household side. Nothing set is the first-time state. No Recipe hides the Recipes group, because the household is not entitled to it.

Did the model hold what the owner believes?
  • RightIn Kitchie: stock-check settings, notes, stops and food likes and avoids are all kept for the household and for each member.
  • Not soIn Recipe: stars and votes are kept for the household only. There is no member column, and the signed-in person (sub) is not stored with them.
  • NoneIn platform: no preference is stored. Its session token carries sub, household, products and the times, no role and no owner.
  • UnclearThe two levels do not behave the same everywhere: stock-check settings override (member beats household), notes and food claims are added together, and Recipe has one level only. Option A needs a row style for each, not one rule.
What the model holds (read from the code, 10 October 2026: Kitchie main at 9d1049c, Recipe at 5cec532, platform at 409f44f)

Stock-check settings Both levels, override

What
Ask to check amounts (enabled, on or off), How proactive (proactivity, quiet, normal or helpful), Quiet hours (quiet_start and quiet_end, HH:MM), Most suggestions per day (daily_cap), Time zone (tz_offset_minutes, -720 to 840).
Kept
stock_prefs (scope, scope_id, key, value). Scope is household (the household id) or member (the member id). One row per key.
Combine
Built-in default, then the household’s value, then the member’s own, key by key. The member wins. Clearing a key falls back one level. The store reports where each value came from (dial_sources: default, household or member).
Who changes
A member changes their own. Today a household value needs the household owner (the longest-standing member); anyone else is refused: “Only the household owner can change a household-wide setting. Nothing was changed.” Decided (D5, 10 Oct): any member may change a household value; that check goes. No tenure gate for now, deliberately deferred.
Surfaces
Settings > Stock checks (one switch, then “Your settings” and “Household settings” forms; today a non-owner sees the household values read-only). Chat: stock_settings (get, set_dial, set_note, stop, remove_stop, list_stops), personal link only, own settings only. Household values are changed in the app only.

Notes Both levels, both read

What
One free-text note for the household and one per member, up to 600 characters, kept as data and read as a preference, never as an instruction.
Kept
notes (scope, scope_id, text).
Combine
No override. Both are returned. A member’s note is read for that member only; another member’s is never read.
Who changes / surfaces
Same rule and same two forms as the settings above, and stock_settings set_note for the member’s own.

Not asking about (stops and snoozes) Person

What
Stop or snooze (a week) stock checks for one item, an area, a category, or everything. A stop gets a check-back later, with growing gaps.
Kept
stock_stops (household_id, member_id, target, target_key, mode). member_id may be empty in the table and reads count a household-wide stop, but every write today carries the member. A stop on one item covers every member.
Who changes / surfaces
The member, in the Not asking about list, on a check (Stop asking), or in chat. Not drawn here: it is a list of things, not a value.

Food likes, dislikes and avoids Both levels, added together

What
Claims the person stated: likes, dislikes, avoids, usually, fact, about an item, ingredient, recipe or cuisine. An avoid carries a severity (hard or soft) and a reason (safety or other). Optional meal time and weekday or weekend.
Kept
context_claims; an empty member_id means the whole household.
Combine
No override. get_context for “me” returns the member’s own and the household’s together; for “household” only the household’s. Avoids are always returned. Another member’s own claims are never read.
Who changes
Only through the assistant (give_feedback, who is “me” or “household”). The code has no owner check for a household claim, so any member can record one; the owner decided that stays (D8).
Surfaces
Chat only (get_context, give_feedback). There is no web page for it.

My assistant Person only

What
Which AI app the person uses: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, another, or none.
Kept
stock_prefs, scope member, key assistant, mirrored in a cookie on the device. No household value exists.
Surfaces
A card in Settings. No chat tool.

Recipe stars and votes Household only

What
Starred (yes or no) and vote (-1, 0, 1) on a recipe or a variation, plus how many times it was used and when last.
Kept
Recipe preferences (target_kind, target_id, starred, vote, times_used, last_used). The household comes only from the recipe row the preference points at. No member column.
Who changes
Anyone in a household with Recipe enabled; no owner rule. Every write re-checks the entitlement live.
Surfaces
Chat only (set_preference, record_usage). Recipe’s web pages neither show nor set them.

Other person-level settings Person

Plan the week
Usual days off cooking (plan_stencil) and one-off blocked days (plan_blocks) are per member. Already drawn in fragments/plan-week-ai/, so not repeated here.
Me screen
Name, picture and kitchen role are per member.

Not in the data model This device

What
Theme (a cookie), item name size and the seven display switches (this browser). They follow the device, not the person, so they are not in this list.
How option A uses it Every line is a Proposal unless marked as a model rule or as superseded. Numbered A-1 to A-10 so they can be picked apart. The owner's decisions are D5 to D8, in the next panel.
  1. A-1 One list, grouped by what the row is about (Stock checks, Food and taste, Notes, Assistant, Recipes), not by who owns it. The switch is how you see the other level.
  2. A-2 A closed row shows the value in force for the viewer and a chip for where it comes from: Built-in default From household Your choice. A personal choice also says what it overrides (“Overrides household: Quiet”). Words carry the meaning; colour only backs it up.
  3. A-3 The You / Household switch lives inside the open row (one row open at a time), You first. Detail on demand, not a switch on every closed row.
  4. A-4 On You, the first choice is “Same as household (value)”, which clears the person’s own value. On Household, the first choice is “Built-in default (value)”. Both mirror the existing Stock checks form.
  5. A-5 A pick saves at once and says “Saved”; there is no Save button. Today’s forms have one, but the store already writes one key at a time.
  6. A-6 Superseded by D5This drew the model's one read-only state: a member who is not the household owner sees the household value and a lock line. The owner decided there is no owner and no gate, so the Household side is editable by any member and carries a line that says so. Nothing read-only is drawn.
  7. A-7 Rows that do not override get their own chip and a plain line: “Both are read” (notes), “Added together” (food), “Household only” (Recipe), and for My assistant the Household half of the switch is struck out. The switch never promises an override the model does not have.
  8. A-8 Food claims and Recipe stars are shown but not edited here. The assistant is the only way in for food; stars and votes are set on each recipe.
  9. A-9 The Recipes group is not drawn when the household has no Recipe (platform D6: no hint of a product the household does not have).
  10. A-10 One layout at every width. It is a Settings sub-screen, so on desktop it keeps the phone column like the rest of the household flow, and tablet only relaxes the page. No viewport-specific file was needed.
Locked decisions Owner, voice session, 10 October 2026. Numbered D5 to D14 across options A, B and C; the ones that apply to this option are D5 to D8 (the rest are Option C's).
  • D5 Household-level settings and edits: any household member may edit them. No owner concept, no tenure gate, no restriction. The owner explicitly deferred any earn-your-way, tenure-based gate to a later decision; it is deferred, not forgotten, and nothing here builds it in.
  • D6 Quiet hours: any member may override the household's quiet hours down to none for themselves. No restriction. (The quiet-hours editor on You has a None choice.)
  • D7 Most suggestions per day: 1 to 20, default 2. The web form (was 1 to 10) and the chat surface (was 0 to 20) both use it.
  • D8 Household-level food claims (for example “we don’t eat pork”) are open to any member, consistent with D5. Person-level food statements stay unrestricted.
Open questions and what a wrong guess costs. The questions that D5 to D8 settled are removed.
  • Quiet start and end are two keys that chat can set separately, so a person could have one from the household and one of their own. The form needs both. Treat the pair as one preference?
  • One person cannot switch off a household avoid for themselves (food is added together, with no override). Intended? (Who may record a household claim is decided, D8.)
  • Should Recipe stars and votes get a person level (who starred it)? Today shared favourites hide individual taste, and a wrong guess means a Recipe schema change later.
  • Does Preferences replace Settings > Stock checks (it holds the same data), or sit above it? A wrong guess means two screens writing the same keys.
  • Household-wide stops: the table allows them, nothing writes them. Needed?
  • Which option leads, A or B?
Performance note (DESIGN.md section 6)
  • Default path loads: this page, the shared theme and stylesheet, a.css (about 10 KB) and a.js (about 22 KB, before compression). No images and no icon files; the row icons are emoji.
  • Deferred until engaged: in the real app a closed row needs only the values (the stock-check read is two small queries, one per level). A row’s editor, the note text, the food claims and the Recipe counts load when the row is opened. Recipe counts are asked only for a household that has Recipe. Other themes are separate files fetched on choice (theme.js).
  • Why reasonable now: it is a settings screen reached on purpose, so one small read on entry is enough.
