Settings shake-upmockup only, nothing built

Three simpler directions for the phone Settings, all playable. Recommended: B. Each has working search on the real index, Household, Preferences, Kitchen setup and Assistants pages, light and dark, and text to +30%.

Direction Try

How to play. Everything works: tap a theme tile, the sun or moon, A− and A+ (seven steps, up to +30%), open Household and press Copy invite link, and search from any page with the field or the magnifier (the page you are on is listed first, then everywhere else). Open ?o=B&bare=1 on a phone for the full-screen version. In this mockup a theme is one look, so Light and Dark switch between Kitchie Day and Night; in the build every theme carries both looks and Light or dark keeps your theme.

Owner, 24 Oct 2026, on the live build 0.171.0 of option E: “I think it looks like it’s going worse than what it is now.” “It seems very Java ME like.” “Don’t know why you’ve got stuff.” Shortcuts should be “readily available”: the control itself, small, one tap, not a copy of the Looks page. “You have to look at a way to shake up the settings; nothing changes.” Principles: “User experience is paramount. Less is more.” Kitchie #1110, #1090. Phone only.

Start from what Settings is for. Almost nobody opens it to browse. They open it to make the app comfortable to read (theme, light or dark, bigger text), to look after the household (who is in, the invite link, the kitchen’s shape) and, rarely, to tune the assistant. Everything else is something they know the name of and search for. So: the comfort controls are controls, not a place; three or four plain areas; one excellent search. Below, three directions that differ in structure, not in paint, then a cut list of every element on today’s screens.

Honest reading: today’s theme is 1 tap when it is among the five tiles showing, 2 after a swipe; in C the light or dark icon cycles System, Light, Dark, so Dark from System is 2; the bell is search, type, switch in every version. No direction makes the common jobs much cheaper in taps. Today’s pinned tiles were already one tap. What drops is what has to be read and drawn, and the Looks page and the pin machinery are deleted. The invite link gets a tap cheaper because Copy invite link makes the link and copies it in one press (Proposal, SR-Q39). Taps start at the Settings front; reaching Settings is the same for every direction.

Recommendation: B, Search is the front door

Why. It is the smallest front (11 controls against 24), it puts the three comfort controls exactly where the owner said the intent of shortcuts is (small, one tap, on the front, the control itself), it leads with the thing he called the product (search), and it has no page that exists to hold nothing. A does the least and is the safest; C answers a question nobody asked (“let me change text size from the Household page”) and spends 52px on every page to do it.

Trade-offs and risks, plainly. (1) The controls live only on the front, which is two taps from the app (avatar, Settings). (2) A big search field is 56px of height that earns nothing if few people search; there is no usage data and we decided not to collect any (SR-Q14). (3) Kitchen setup moves inside Household, which is two rows longer. (4) Deleting the Looks page and the pins is a deletion of something just built and shipped to UAT (0.171.0): per-device pins vanish quietly, nothing is lost. (5) Preferences is untouched and is now the heaviest screen left; it needs its own shake-up next. (6) Taps do not improve, so this is a judgement about clarity, not speed. (7) Not tried on a phone or with a screen reader; the strip and stepper are checked at 390 and 360 px wide, to +30% text.

Fallback. A is deletion only and can grow B’s big search later. If the owner wants controls on every page, C is a small step from B.

What stays decided

  • Search must be excellent and reach every setting from anywhere, current page first (“On this page”, then “Everywhere else”): the engine and the 51-entry index are the earlier ones (../settings-redesign/search-index.js), synonyms, plain phrases and typos unchanged (try “drak mode”). Results are one grey line under the name, no tags or paragraphs.
  • No pin words, no Emoji setting, text size is the seven-step stepper centred on Match my phone (−8, −16, −24, +10, +20, +30%), themes are named tiles that show their own look, preferences stay per device and client-side, one name per setting.
  • 44px targets (and 3.2em at larger text), strong contrast, text to +30% on every screen here, light and dark.

Open questions and decision for the owner

  1. SR-Q36. Which direction: A, B or C? Recommend B. A wrong guess costs a front screen only; the sub pages are shared.
  2. SR-Q37. Delete the Looks page, the Shortcuts area and the pins (whichever direction)? Recommend yes: the controls act in place, so there is nothing left to pin. Stored pins are ignored.
  3. SR-Q38. Fold Kitchen setup into Household (B)? Recommend yes: Categories and Locations are the household’s. A and C keep the row.
  4. SR-Q39. Copy invite link makes and copies the link in one press instead of making it on one page and showing it on the next? Recommend yes (Proposal). A wrong guess costs one confirm line.
  5. SR-Q40. Leave household stays search-only, as the index files it, rather than a plain bottom row on Household? Recommend search-only, with the confirm that follows. A wrong guess is a risky action that is slightly harder to find.
  6. SR-Q41. Is Preferences next? Recommend yes: it is the heaviest page left (eight rows, each a drill-down).
