Item edit flyout

Editing an item used to open its own full page. Here it opens as a flyout nested under the item flyout you already have open, on phone, tablet and desktop. Mockup only: nothing is saved, nothing is built. Everything below is a proposal until the owner confirms it. Decisions are numbered 63 to 81 (Plan on desktop used 49 to 62).

Recommendation

Layout A, one scroll in five groups, with the key hints shown on the icons.

The flyout nests: the item flyout stays open and in front, the edit flyout comes out from beneath it (a fourth column from 1240 wide, tucked under the item's header in the same lane from 1024, a taller sheet stacked over the half sheet on a phone). Header: a pencil (edit), a turned-back clock (history), then the close x. The two tabs do not survive: a cook editing a tin of tomatoes needs quantity, minimum and use-by together, and the second tab hid them. The fix for the Shopping list block is one spaced, ruled-off group at the end of the form, not under both tabs. Add item is the same form in add mode.

Layout of the fields: the real choice

A. One scroll, five groups

Recommended.

  • Item (emoji and name), Amount (quantity, level drop, minimum), Where (location, spot, category), Use by, Shopping list; then the recipe action.
  • Every field visible without a second tap; level is a drop, never a word.
  • Cost: a long scroll on a phone (about two screens).

B. Two tabs kept

Parity with the app today.

  • Details (name, quantity, where) and Stock & more (level, minimum, use by, shopping). Shopping now sits on one tab only; the recipe action sits under both.
  • Shorter screens, familiar to people who use the current page.
  • Cost: use by is one tap further, and the owner has already moved away from hiding things on a second tab.
DesktopStock tabPhone

C. Tiles

The item flyout's own pattern, one level down.

  • Every field is a tile with its value; a tap opens that one editor with a way back.
  • Consistent with the tile grid, big touch targets.
  • Cost: it repeats the parent's tiles, adds a tap to every edit, and hides how much is editable.

Decisions 63 to 81 (proposed, none confirmed)
  1. 63.Editing is a flyout nested under the open item flyout, never a page. The item flyout stays open, in front and live.
  2. 64.One child slot: Edit and History share it. The pencil and E, and the clock and ,, toggle their child; opening one while the other is open swaps.
  3. 65.Desktop from 1240: a 360px column beside the item flyout, sliding out from beneath it. From 1024: tucked under the item's header inside the lane. It never covers the list or the item. History's slot goes from 320 to 360 so both children share one width.
  4. 66.Phone and tablet: a second sheet (84% high) stacked over the half sheet; the item collapses to a strip. Tap the strip or the dim, drag a grab handle or the title bar down, or press Esc to step back one layer.
  5. 67.Header actions in this order: pencil, turned-back clock, round x. The open child's icon is filled. The phone sheet keeps the no-x rule of 8 October.
  6. 68.Hints: a key cap on each icon (default) or on hover and focus; the shortcut line reads "E edit" and ", history"; the cue switch hides all; desktop widths only.
  7. 69.Close is Ctrl or Cmd Enter on desktop, Esc on a phone: the top layer first. Layout c steps back from a field to its tiles first.
  8. 70.A tile tap in the item flyout lands in the edit flyout on that field, highlighted. The tiles stay the glance.
  9. 71.Layout A is recommended. The two tabs do not survive. Tiles (C) are drawn, not recommended.
  10. 72.Level is a drop, never a word. Counted: worked out, not editable here. Weighed or worded: four drops. The "Level: plenty" chip and the separate select are gone.
  11. 73.Minimum stock sits under Quantity in Amount, shown only when the item has a unit or a minimum.
  12. 74.Shopping list is one spaced, ruled-off group at the end, once. The copy is the app's; the item flyout's Used up and Add to shopping stay exactly as they are (owner).
  13. 75."Use in a recipe" is a plain action row at the bottom; its label and target belong to the Recipe manifest; hidden without Recipes.
  14. 76."Mark as used up" is not repeated in the form.
  15. 77.Every change autosaves with a short "Saved". No Save button. Errors sit on the field with Try again. A name is needed; a use-by date cannot be in the past.
  16. 78.Add item is the same form in add mode, in the item flyout's place, with its own footer. New items are Unplaced.
  17. 79.History shows this item only, newest first by day: what changed, when, who, from where. The last 3 days that have entries, then 5 more. Edits made on this page appear under Today. Fetched on open, never before.
  18. 80.The emoji is a tile ("+" when none) with a small picker of foods and "No emoji". No photos.
  19. 81.Viewport code is in its own files: phone.css, tablet.css, desktop.css, desktop-1240.css, and one host script (phone.js or desktop.js).

What loads, and when (DESIGN.md section 6)

Default path: pantry.html, theme.css (two default themes), flyout.css, the size stylesheets (each is media-gated to its own width, so a browser applies only the matching ones and fetches the rest last, without blocking), flyout.js, and exactly one host script (phone.js below 1024px, desktop.js from it). No images, no fonts. Deferred: form.js on the first Edit or Add, history.js on the first History open, and the journal itself only on that open. Nothing is preloaded. Why this is reasonable: most visits open the item and leave; the form is for the minority who edit, and History is rarer still.
