Plan it flyout: how to show “more than one meal on this night”

Four visual options; cue 1 (the count badge) is chosen by the owner (SC-D1, 25 October 2026), nothing is built yet. Mockup only, nothing is saved or sent. Each is the real option F flyout on desktop Home (same rows, same keys, unchanged rows.js and rows.css); only the cue under or on the stacked night changes. Sample: Sat has 2 meals (the household stir-fry and Sam’s own), Mon has 3 (the household curry, Jo’s toastie, Sam’s rice).

Owner, 24 October 2026

The thin sliver card peeking under the day row, shown when there is more than one meal on a day, “reads as a stray border” on both the dark and the light theme. He wants a different visual language. Options first; he picks.

The options (flyout at 1440x900 and 1280x720, dark and light)

Today: the sliver

What F draws now: a 12px strip of a card peeking under the row, with a tiny uppercase label that is empty until the night is open.

Trade-off: Reads as a stray border on both themes (owner, 24 October 2026). Shown only as the baseline.

Baseline: Not an option.

Cue 1: count badge chosen by the owner (SC-D1)

A small pill at the row's top right with a two-cards glyph and the count, “2 meals” or “3 meals”. It is the open button.

Trade-off: Says plainly that there is more and how much; the cheapest to build and the most legible. It does not say whose, and it narrows the row text by about 90px (one more wrapped line on long consequence text).

Recommendation: Recommended; chosen by the owner, 25 October 2026 (SC-D1).

Cue 2: stacked cards not taken

A real pile: the front row is 12px narrower and one or two whole cards sit behind it, each lower and further right, with a visible edge and rounded corners on every side.

Trade-off: Closest to the original idea and no words to read; the pile shows the count (two cards for three meals). Shape only, so it needs the screen-reader text and the first-pick rule to carry the meaning, and the front card is narrower than its neighbours.

Recommendation: Second choice if he wants no extra text.

Cue 3: who dots not taken

Overlapping small circles with an initial and a ring, one for each person who has their own plan that night (S for Sam, J for Jo).

Trade-off: Shows whose without opening, in the app's own people language. It needs learning (an initial in a ring is not self-explaining), and a household of many would need a cap.

Recommendation: Good if “whose” matters more than “how many”.

Cue 4: a second mini row not taken

A 28px attached row under the night for each person with their own plan (two at most), with an elbow connector, the name and the meal: “Jo Cheese toastie”.

Trade-off: Nothing to decode and nothing to open to learn what it is. The cost is height: about 28px per person, so Mon (3 meals) grows by two rows; the flyout is already taller than the window at 1280x720.

Recommendation: Use if he wants the carve-outs visible without a tap.

What every option keeps
  • The cue is F’s own peek button: the first pick on the night opens it (it does not plan), the second plans; Right opens the night’s full picture, Left folds it; when open it is F’s “Hide” strip.
  • Shape plus words or letters, never colour alone; shared tokens only (--surface, --bg, --fg, --muted, --accent, --border); text 4.5:1 and cue edges 3:1 against the flyout in both themes, asserted by the check.
  • A night with one meal only (someone’s own plan on an otherwise open night: Fri and Sun in the sample) gets no cue in these options; F still opens it in two steps. Proposal, see question 3.
Decisions
  1. SC-D1 (owner, 25 October 2026): cue 1, the count badge, is chosen; cues 2 to 4 are not taken and the sliver goes. Wrong guess costs one CSS block and one small render step in rows.js.
  2. Questions 2 to 4 below are still awaiting the owner’s answer; the drawn proposals stand until then.
  3. Does the count include the household meal (“3 meals” for household plus Jo plus Sam, as drawn), or only the extra own plans (“+2”)?
  4. A night with only an own plan and no household meal: no cue (drawn), or the same cue?
  5. Cue 1 and 3 are 24 to 26px tall pills inside a 56px row that is itself the 44px-plus tap target: fine as a visual cue because the whole row opens the night, or should the badge itself be 44px?
