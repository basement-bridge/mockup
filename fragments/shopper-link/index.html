Shopper link: someone else is shopping

Fragment. The job: shopping for the household without being in the app. A member says someone else is shopping and hands over the list, in the family's own chat, with one link and no sign-in. The shopper swipes what they got and the household's list and Pantry change as they go. Kitchie issue kitchie#479. Owner review of this fragment: 10 October 2026, decisions SL-D1 to SL-D16 below.

The owner's words voice, 10 October 2026
  • “The intent of the entire thing here is to reduce the friction and not force people to be in the system for them to make use of it.” “Find people where they already naturally live.”
  • Whoever shops is not on the app: “Give me the list, I'm going to get those things in the house. And that's the extent of how engaged they're willing to be.”
  • The gap: “they bought some of them, they've gone and substituted some of those things, and there's no way for Kitchie to get all of that information back in.”
  • “At the time of sharing the shopping list, what if Kitchie could spin off a one-off link where the person who's doing the shopping can [tick it off], then that person is not forced to be in the system. That link is just unique for that person. It expires, let's say, forty-eight hours. That's good enough.”
  • “We can just get that person to tick off what they bought, and make any substitutes that they made decisions on, and if they don't tick it off, we kind of know that. Get a confirmation, of course, but we know that person has now acted on that list either fully or partially.”
  • “The call to action there needs to be two modes. One is that they just tick off what's on the list and do the substitutes. Let's start with it as an MVP.” The second mode, scanning the receipt, is phase two: drawn below, not built.
  • The shopper “may choose neither” and that is accepted.

Part A

The member, signed in, on Shopping

Four moments: where the hand-over lives, what lands in the chat, links being out, and what comes back as it happens.

1. Where does “Someone else is shopping” live? Judgement call: A

The Shopping header lost its cart button on purpose (one shared header on every screen), and the plan still asks where the hand-off goes. Tap it in any phone: it makes the link and hands the list to the phone's own share sheet (or copies it). The owner did not pick a place, so the fragment's own favourite stands as a judgement call (SL-J1) and the other two stay here for comparison.

AA hand-off slot above the list Judgement call

Used. One strip above the list is where the hand-off lives: the control, the link lines, then what came back. Seen on arrival however long the list is, and the shared header stays untouched. Costs one row of height, always.

BAn icon in the header

Smallest. But the header is the same on Home, Pantry and Shopping by the owner's decision, an icon alone does not say “with a link”, and the live state has nowhere to sit beside it.

CAfter the last line

Where you are when the list is finished, and close to the thumb on a short list. On a long list it is below the fold, and it sits where “Done shopping” appears once anything is ticked.

Why A

  • One place for the whole hand-off. The control, the link lines and the cards sit together, so there is nothing to hunt for.
  • It says what it does. A sentence and a bag, not an icon to decode.
  • It leaves settled things alone: the shared header and the “Done shopping” bar.
  • What it costs: the list starts one row lower for everyone, including households that never hand over.

2. What lands in the family's chat

Plain text, so it reads the same in any chat app: the list exactly as Kitchie already writes it (share-text.ts), then one line and the link. The link goes after the list (SL-D14): the list alone is still useful to someone who never taps.

3. Links that are out

Quiet, one line per link, with a way to end it. A link stays usable for its whole window however often it is opened, so a second trip the same day reuses it (SL-D12). The window is 48 hours from sharing, plus a fresh 48 from the first open if that is later (SL-D13), so an opened link can say “ends in 45 hours” and a link nobody has opened keeps its first window. Up to 3 links per household, including ones never sent; a link made when the share sheet was closed reads “Made, not sent?” and has Withdraw (SL-D16). Cancel is one tap with no second confirm (SL-D15). None is labelled with a person (SL-D3). Cancel and Withdraw work in the phones.

Made, not sent?

The share sheet was closed. The link still counts toward the 3 until it is withdrawn or ends.

Three out, the most

Opened, not opened, and not sent. The hand-off steps aside and says why. Cancel or withdraw one and it comes back.

4. What comes back, as it happens

The real list changes as the shopper answers (SL-D2): bought lines are crossed out and say “in the Pantry”, a swap says what the Pantry did with it (a fusilli already in the Pantry is topped up; anything else is added as new, SL-D11), and a line that could not be found stays on the list. What the shopper added as “also got” shows after the list. Nothing waits behind “Done shopping”. The card is a notification only (SL-D4): counts, and which lines, so two shoppers on two links can be told apart without labels (SL-D3).

Part B

The shopper: no sign-in, one hand, in a shop

One page, two modes. Swipe is the default: swipe right for “got it”, left for “couldn't find it” (SL-D5), and the row turns green or red as you pull (SL-D17). Every action syncs at once, with no confirm step, and an answered line leaves the list (SL-D18). A tap on the tick is “got it” too. A long-press on any line is multi-select: checkboxes, nothing syncs, one Send at the end (SL-D8).

Try it, live

Three phones that share one pretend server. Swipe in the first and watch the other two: the second shopper's list marks it “Someone else got this” and the member's Shopping screen changes by itself. Press and hold a line for multi-select.

  1. Swipe a line right, another left, and watch the colour grow. Each line you answer leaves the list. The bar at the top says what is left and what Undo will take back.
  2. Tap Got something else on one, say what you got, press Enter. It syncs as a swap (120 characters at most).
  3. Type in Also got something that wasn't on the list? and press Got it too.
  4. Won't be able to buy these closes every line still open. Undo brings them back.
  5. Press and hold a line: the list becomes checkboxes and the bottom button becomes Send, with the three counts first.

The requests, exactly for the build

Swipe mode sends one request per action and the server answers with the whole list's state. Multi-select and the no-script form send one request. All are form-encoded POSTs to <base>/list/<token>/… with no cookie and no session. A 404 is the one neutral answer for a link that has finished.

line
key (the line's id, stable while the list changes), state (got, notgot or open), swap (text, up to 120 characters; if it is not empty the line is swapped and the swap wins). One swipe, tap or saved swap is one request. Answers 409 with the current state if the line was already got or swapped on another link (SL-J4). A line answered here leaves the shopper's list; state=open (Undo) brings it back.
extra, extra/remove
text (up to 120 characters) adds an “also got” line; id removes one of this link's own.
unresolved
No fields. “Won't be able to buy these”: every line still open becomes notgot; answered lines, and lines answered on other links, are untouched.
undo
No fields. Takes back this link's own latest action (a whole unresolved or batch counts as one) and answers the state.
batch
Multi-select and the no-script form: got (repeated, one per ticked line), swap_<key>, also (repeated), confirm=1. Lines not named are notgot; lines already got on another link are skipped. Without confirm the server answers the confirm page.
GET events
The live stream (server-sent events). Each event is the whole state, so a missed one costs nothing: { rev, lines: { <key>: { state, words, by } }, extras, last, hours }. by is you, other or empty, never a name. Within about a second of any change on any link of the list.
  • GET <base>/list/<token> draws the page with data-state, data-by and the swap words already on each line, so it is right before the script runs. Every line is drawn, and each answered line carries data-sl-gone (the stylesheet hides it): a visit shows only what is left, and Undo only takes the mark off (SL-D18, SL-J28). When no line is open the server draws the end card unhidden and leaves the Send bar out. The batch request is unchanged: hidden lines' ticks and swap fields are still in the form.
  • List rows use the Shopping screen's own names (ul.rows.shop, li.shoprow[data-shop-row], .swrow, .swfg, .swbg, .tick, .rlink, .em, .smain, .sname, .want). Everything new starts with sl-.
  • One place the markup cannot match the app: the app's tick is button.tick[role=checkbox], which needs script. Here it is label.tick around a real checkbox, so the page works as a plain form. Same class, same look, one size up.
  • Pantry rules are server-side and not drawn: a got line adds its listed amount (1 where there is none, SL-J2); a swap and an also-got go through the same add-item matching as a hand-added item (SL-D11).

Decisions the owner's: 10 October 2026 (review of this fragment) SL-D1 to SL-D16, and 11 October 2026 (live test of the built page) SL-D17 to SL-D20

Numbered as this feature's own series, like the preferences D-series and the recipe tab's R-D series. “Revises” and “answers” point at the proposals (P1 to P9) and open questions (1 to 13) these replace.

  1. SL-D1. Naming. “Share” becomes a hand-off wording, for example “Someone else is shopping”. It is only for when someone else does the shopping.
  2. SL-D2. A result updates the real list at once. When a result comes back on any link, the shopping list updates immediately for members. It is not gated on a “Done shopping” tap. Revises P1; answers question 1; is question 13.
  3. SL-D3. Concurrent links share one live view. Other live links for the same list reflect an update in near real time, so two shoppers (morning and afternoon) do not double-buy. P3's frozen snapshot becomes a live, shared view. Links need no label: the result's content tells concurrent shoppers apart. Revises P3; answers question 3.
  4. SL-D4. The result card is a notification. “Got 9 of 12 · 1 swap · 2 not got” is a summary only, never a gate.
  5. SL-D5. The shopper's page defaults to swipe. Swipe right = got it, swipe left = didn't find it. Every action syncs instantly with no confirm step.
  6. SL-D6. Undo is always visible at the top of the page, because the shopper is the person least familiar with the app.
  7. SL-D7. The bottom button in swipe mode is not “Send”. It reads “Won't be able to buy these” and applies only to lines still unresolved; lines already synced are untouched.
  8. SL-D8. Long-press is multi-select. A long-press on any line turns the whole list into checkboxes. Nothing syncs live; the picks are one batch. The bottom button is the original summary send and confirm: three counts, listing only the non-“got” lines. Revises P4.
  9. SL-D9. “Got something else” is a small button on each line with an in-place text edit (120 characters) that syncs instantly as a swap. Revises P2.
  10. SL-D10. The free-text “also got” box is built now, for things bought that were not on the list. Answers question 4.
  11. SL-D11. Swaps go into the Pantry automatically with the standard add-item matching: an existing fusilli is topped up when penne was swapped for fusilli; otherwise a new item is created. No member hand-add. Answers question 2.
  12. SL-D12. One link, reused. A link stays usable for its whole validity window however many times it is opened; each open shows the current state (what is still not bought). A second trip the same day reuses it. Revises P5; answers question 5.
  13. SL-D13. The window. 48 hours from sharing; if the link is first opened later, a fresh 48 hours from first open too (effectively whichever gives more time). Answers question 6.
  14. SL-D14. The link goes after the list text in the chat message. Answers question 7.
  15. SL-D15. Cancel is one tap, with no second confirm. Answers question 8.
  16. SL-D16. At most 3 live links per household (P7). A link made but not sent (the share sheet closed without sending) still counts, unless the sender withdraws it. Links expire by themselves after their window. Answers question 9.
  17. SL-D17. The drag reads at a glance, without reading words. (Owner, voice, 11 October 2026, live test of the built page: the text tag shown while dragging toward “couldn't find” was hard to read mid-swipe.) As a row is dragged its background colour grows with the drag: green toward the right (got it), red toward the left (couldn't find), with an icon; the words stay but are secondary. The existing tick for “got it” is “actually fantastic” and is kept exactly; the existing “couldn't find” icon is fine and is kept (not replaced with a cross). Colour is not the only cue (icon, words, an outline once letting go will answer), contrast passes WCAG AA in light and dark, there is a reduced-motion path, and the keyboard and no-script paths are unchanged. Revises the plain reveal of SL-D5.
  18. SL-D18. Answered lines leave the shopper's list. (Owner, same test: “don't need that” about a trail of done items.) Once a line is got, swapped or couldn't find, it drops out of the shopper's own list, so only what is still left to do is shown, at the top. Nothing is lost: the action is already on the server, the always-visible Undo brings the last one back, and opening the link later shows only what is left. When nothing is left the page says so calmly and makes clear the household has the answers; nothing bought is not an error. In multi-select nothing syncs live, so nothing vanishes until the batch is sent. The swap, the also-got list and extras behave as before. Revises SL-J9 (a second visit drew still-to-get first, then done lines) and the locked-row drawing for resolved lines (SL-J4's drawing, not its rule); SL-D12 now reads “shows what is left” literally.
  19. SL-D19. No control suggests “delete” unless it really removes something. (Owner, same test: he saw a plus and a minus on the shopper page: “minus sounds like I'm deleting it”.) Every plus, minus, cross and similar control on the shopper page and the member's hand-off and result cards was reviewed, redrawn and relabelled where needed, each icon saying what it does. What changed, and what was kept and why, is SL-J25 and SL-J26.
  20. SL-D20. Not a change. “Won't be able to buy these” and “No sign-in needed. This link works for another 48 hours.” stay as they are.
Judgement calls ours, because the owner's decisions did not say; each is listed in the PR to review before production

Numbered SL-J1 on. Each says what was chosen, what the other way would be, and what a wrong guess costs. None is settled.

  1. SL-J1. Where the hand-off control lives: option A, the slot above the list (question 10; the fragment's favourite). Cost: the wrong one is rebuilt; A also moves the list down one row for households that never hand over.
  2. SL-J2. A got line also reaches the Pantry by itself, at the amount on the list (1 where the list gave none), the same effect as a member's “Done shopping”. SL-D2 says the list is not gated on that tap and SL-D11 names swaps only; treating gots differently from swaps would leave the Pantry half-updated. Other way: got lines wait, ticked, for a member's “Done shopping”. Cost: if gots should wait, the Pantry gains stock a shopper only claimed (undoable per item in History); if they should not, the household pays one tap per shop. This is the largest call here.
  3. SL-J3. How “live” travels: one server-sent-events stream per open page, each event the whole state, the browser reconnecting itself; a polling fallback every few seconds is the build's to add and is not drawn. Cost: a different transport (WebSocket, polling) changes server code only; the page's contract (a snapshot) stays.
  4. SL-J4. Two links, one line. Confirmed by the owner, 10 October 2026, as built. A line answered “got” or swapped on one link is locked on the others (“Someone else got this”, nobody named, P6 stands), so it cannot be bought twice or overwritten; “didn't find” does not lock. A refused write answers 409 and the page corrects itself. Changed on 11 October 2026 to match what he confirmed (this text and the pretend server in mock.js still had the first version, where a swap could be answered by the other shopper). How a locked line is drawn is now SL-J20. Cost: a wrong “got” can only be undone by the shopper who made it, or by a member.
  5. SL-J5. Swipe is not the only way. A tap on the tick is “got it” (and again is back to open), and each line has a hidden “Couldn't find” button that shows when it has keyboard focus, for people who cannot swipe. Cost: none visible.
  6. SL-J6. Undo takes back this link's own latest action, one step at a time; a whole “Won't be able to buy these” or a batch Send is one step. It shows what it would undo. In multi-select it takes back the last pick. It is disabled, not hidden, when there is nothing to take back. Cost: undoing someone else's answer is not possible from this page.
  7. SL-J7. “Won't be able to buy these” has no confirm and no count on the button (SL-D5 says no confirm step; Undo is the safety). It is a quiet outline button, not the accent one, because it closes every open line. The bar leaves when no line is open. Cost: an accidental tap closes the rest until Undo.
  8. SL-J8. Multi-select details. The long-pressed line starts ticked; the list starts from the current synced answers (revised by the owner's SL-D18: lines already answered have left the list and stay out of sight, see SL-J23); lines another link got are locked and ticked; “Back to swiping” drops the unsent picks and says so; sending returns to the list in swipe mode with the link still live. Unticked lines become “not got” at Send (the owner's original rule). Cost: a shopper who backs out loses the picks.
  9. SL-J9. Revised by the owner, 11 October 2026 (SL-D18). A second visit shows only what is left. It used to put “still to get” first (open and not-found lines) and then “bought already”, with a status of “3 to go · 2 not found”. Now every answered line, found or not, is off the list, there are no dividers and the status reads “3 to go”. What this costs, and the other way, is SL-J21.
  10. SL-J10. “Also got” is free text only (up to 120 characters, no amount field), read by the Pantry's add-item matching; the shopper can remove their own and Undo takes the last one back. It shows on the member's screen after the list. Cost: amounts in the words (“2 cans”) are only as good as the matching.
  11. SL-J11. The live list is the real list (SL-D3 retires the snapshot): lines a member adds while a link is out appear on the open page, and removed ones disappear unless already answered. Line keys are therefore stable ids, not places in a snapshot; the 200-line cap (P8) stands. Not drawn: the mock list is fixed at 12. Cost: a shopper's list can change under them; the other way is a stale list.
  12. SL-J12. The member's card says “Shopping is coming in” with a live dot until no line is open, then “The shop is back”; it shows the counts and the first three lines, one card per link, Dismiss, no link named. The hand-off control no longer steps aside while a result is waiting (it did when a result was a gate); it still steps aside at 3 links. Cost: two cards stack on a busy day.
  13. SL-J13. “Opened” means the page ran, not that the address was fetched: the first live-stream connection starts the fresh 48 hours, because chat apps fetch links to draw a preview and that must not count. The member's line shows “Not opened yet” or “Opened 3 hours ago” with the hours left. Cost: an unopened link keeps only its first window.
  14. SL-J14. Made, not sent? The page tells the server the share sheet was closed (the share answer now carries the link's id) so that line can say so and offer Withdraw. It never withdraws by itself; it still counts toward the 3 (SL-D16). Cost: a sheet closed by mistake leaves a line to withdraw.

Performance note (DESIGN.md section 6)
  • Shopper, default path loads: the page (30 KB for 12 lines, about 4.5 KB compressed; about 2.1 KB a line, answered lines included, so roughly 420 KB at the 200-line cap, which should compress to about 20 KB; both figures are estimates. The swipe reveals and the swap field are on every line), shopper.css (16 KB, 5 KB compressed) and one deferred script, shopper.js (36 KB with its comments, 11 KB compressed; about 30 KB and 9 KB compressed without the comment lines). No app stylesheet, no header, no bottom bar, no scribble script. The page is usable before the script arrives, and without it (as the multi-select form).
  • Shopper, while shopping: one small request per swipe (a few hundred bytes, answered with the state, so about 2 KB for 12 lines), plus the one open event stream. Nothing polls. Without a swipe nothing is sent.
  • Deferred until chosen (phase two): receipt.js (8 KB, 3 KB compressed) and receipt.css (2 KB) are fetched only when the person taps “Snap the receipt”. In phase one the chooser itself is not in the page.
  • Sent and “That link has finished”: the page and its stylesheet only. No script, no list rows.
  • Member, default path: the Shopping screen as today plus the hand-off slot's markup (drawn on the server), about 6 KB of CSS (2 KB compressed) and about 5 KB of script folded into shopping.js. The live feed is the Shopping screen's own, opened only while a link is out; with no link out nothing extra loads.
  • Why reasonable now: the shopper opens this on mobile data, possibly with a weak signal in a shop. Everything they need arrives in the first response, and a missed event costs nothing because every event is the whole state.
  • This fragment page is heavier than any screen it shows: it loads about 30 frames at once so every state can be compared. Mockup only. mock.js (the pretend server, its stream and a second shopper) and kitchie.css are also mockup only.
