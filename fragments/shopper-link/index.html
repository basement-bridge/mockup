Shopper link: someone else is shopping

Fragment. The job: shopping for the household without being in the app. A member says someone else is shopping and hands over the list, in the family's own chat, with one link and no sign-in. The shopper swipes what they got and the household's list and Pantry change as they go. Kitchie issue kitchie#479. Owner review of this fragment: 10 October 2026, decisions SL-D1 to SL-D16 below; his live test of 11 October 2026, SL-D17 to SL-D20; and his second voice session the same day on promising, claiming and the household's view, SL-D21 to SL-D33 (Part D).

The owner's words voice, 10 October 2026
  • “The intent of the entire thing here is to reduce the friction and not force people to be in the system for them to make use of it.” “Find people where they already naturally live.”
  • Whoever shops is not on the app: “Give me the list, I'm going to get those things in the house. And that's the extent of how engaged they're willing to be.”
  • The gap: “they bought some of them, they've gone and substituted some of those things, and there's no way for Kitchie to get all of that information back in.”
  • “At the time of sharing the shopping list, what if Kitchie could spin off a one-off link where the person who's doing the shopping can [tick it off], then that person is not forced to be in the system. That link is just unique for that person. It expires, let's say, forty-eight hours. That's good enough.”
  • “We can just get that person to tick off what they bought, and make any substitutes that they made decisions on, and if they don't tick it off, we kind of know that. Get a confirmation, of course, but we know that person has now acted on that list either fully or partially.”
  • “The call to action there needs to be two modes. One is that they just tick off what's on the list and do the substitutes. Let's start with it as an MVP.” The second mode, scanning the receipt, is phase two: drawn below, not built.
  • The shopper “may choose neither” and that is accepted.

Part A

The member, signed in, on Shopping

Four moments: where the hand-over lives, what lands in the chat, links being out, and what comes back as it happens.

1. Where does “Someone else is shopping” live? Judgement call: A

The Shopping header lost its cart button on purpose (one shared header on every screen), and the plan still asks where the hand-off goes. Tap it in any phone: it makes the link and hands the list to the phone's own share sheet (or copies it). The owner did not pick a place, so the fragment's own favourite stands as a judgement call (SL-J1) and the other two stay here for comparison.

AA hand-off slot above the list Judgement call

Used. One strip above the list is where the hand-off lives: the control, the link lines, then what came back. Seen on arrival however long the list is, and the shared header stays untouched. Costs one row of height, always.

BAn icon in the header

Smallest. But the header is the same on Home, Pantry and Shopping by the owner's decision, an icon alone does not say “with a link”, and the live state has nowhere to sit beside it.

CAfter the last line

Where you are when the list is finished, and close to the thumb on a short list. On a long list it is below the fold, and it sits where “Done shopping” appears once anything is ticked.

Why A

  • One place for the whole hand-off. The control, the link lines and the cards sit together, so there is nothing to hunt for.
  • It says what it does. A sentence and a bag, not an icon to decode.
  • It leaves settled things alone: the shared header and the “Done shopping” bar.
  • What it costs: the list starts one row lower for everyone, including households that never hand over.

2. What lands in the family's chat

Plain text, so it reads the same in any chat app: the list exactly as Kitchie already writes it (share-text.ts), then one line and the link. The link goes after the list (SL-D14): the list alone is still useful to someone who never taps.

3. Links that are out

Quiet, one line per link, with a way to end it. A link stays usable for its whole window however often it is opened, so a second trip the same day reuses it (SL-D12). The window is 48 hours from sharing, plus a fresh 48 from the first open if that is later (SL-D13), so an opened link can say “ends in 45 hours” and a link nobody has opened keeps its first window. Up to 3 links per household, including ones never sent; a link made when the share sheet was closed reads “Made, not sent?” and has Withdraw (SL-D16). Cancel is one tap with no second confirm (SL-D15). None is labelled with a person (SL-D3). Cancel and Withdraw work in the phones.

Three out, the most

4. What comes back, as it happens

The real list changes as the shopper answers (SL-D2): bought lines are crossed out and say “in the Pantry”, a swap says what the Pantry did with it (a fusilli already in the Pantry is topped up; anything else is added as new, SL-D11), and a line that could not be found stays on the list. What the shopper added as “also got” shows after the list. Nothing waits behind “Done shopping”. The card is a notification only (SL-D4): counts, and which lines, so two shoppers on two links can be told apart without labels (SL-D3).

Part B

The shopper: no sign-in, one hand, in a shop

One page, two modes. Swipe is the default: swipe right for “got it”, left for “couldn't find it” (SL-D5), and the row turns green or red as you pull (SL-D17). Every action syncs at once, with no confirm step, and an answered line leaves the list (SL-D18). A tap on the tick is “got it” too. A long-press on any line is multi-select: checkboxes, nothing syncs, one Send at the end (SL-D8).

Try it, live

Three phones that share one pretend server. Swipe in the first and watch the other two: the second shopper's list marks it “Someone else got this” and the member's Shopping screen changes by itself. Press and hold a line for multi-select.

  1. Swipe a line right, another left, and watch the colour grow. Each line you answer leaves the list. The bar at the top says what is left and what Undo will take back.
  2. Tap Got something else on one, say what you got, press Enter. It syncs as a swap (120 characters at most).
  3. Type in Also got something that wasn't on the list? and press Got it too.
  4. Swipe a few lines left, then press I'll get these at the top: the other phone's list loses those lines, and the member's screen groups them under the link. I won't do it (only before the first swipe) kills the link.
  5. Won't be able to buy these closes every line still open. Undo brings them back. On the member's screen the lines nobody has move to the top with two choices.
  6. Press and hold a line: the list becomes checkboxes and the bottom button becomes Send, with the three counts first.

The requests, exactly for the build

Swipe mode sends one request per action and the server answers with the whole list's state. Multi-select and the no-script form send one request. All are form-encoded POSTs to <base>/list/<token>/… with no cookie and no session. A 404 is the one neutral answer for a link that has finished.

line
key (the line's id, stable while the list changes), state (got, notgot or open), swap (text, up to 120 characters; if it is not empty the line is swapped and the swap wins). One swipe, tap or saved swap is one request. Answers 409 with the current state if the line was already got or swapped on another link (SL-J4). A line answered here leaves the shopper's list; state=open (Undo) brings it back.
extra, extra/remove
text (up to 120 characters) adds an “also got” line; id removes one of this link's own.
unresolved
No fields. “Won't be able to buy these”: every line still open becomes notgot; answered lines, and lines answered on other links, are untouched.
undo
No fields. Takes back this link's own latest action (a whole unresolved or batch counts as one) and answers the state.
batch
Multi-select and the no-script form: got (repeated, one per ticked line), swap_<key>, also (repeated), confirm=1. Lines not named are notgot; lines already got on another link are skipped. Without confirm the server answers the confirm page.
GET events
The live stream (server-sent events). Each event is the whole state, so a missed one costs nothing: { rev, lines: { <key>: { state, words, by } }, extras, last, hours }. by is you, other or empty, never a name. Within about a second of any change on any link of the list.
  • GET <base>/list/<token> draws the page with data-state, data-by and the swap words already on each line, so it is right before the script runs. Every line is drawn, and each answered line carries data-sl-gone (the stylesheet hides it): a visit shows only what is left, and Undo only takes the mark off (SL-D18, SL-J28). When no line is open the server draws the end card unhidden and leaves the Send bar out. The batch request is unchanged: hidden lines' ticks and swap fields are still in the form.
  • List rows use the Shopping screen's own names (ul.rows.shop, li.shoprow[data-shop-row], .swrow, .swfg, .swbg, .tick, .rlink, .em, .smain, .sname, .want). Everything new starts with sl-.
  • One place the markup cannot match the app: the app's tick is button.tick[role=checkbox], which needs script. Here it is label.tick around a real checkbox, so the page works as a plain form. Same class, same look, one size up.
  • Pantry rules are server-side and not drawn: a got line adds its listed amount (1 where there is none, SL-J2); a swap and an also-got go through the same add-item matching as a hand-added item (SL-D11).

The owner's words voice, 11 October 2026
  • Swiping left, “couldn't find”, means the same as “I won't buy this”. Trim the list first (the wrong shop, say), then make a promise at the top on what is left: “I'll get these”. With nothing swiped it means all of them.
  • Two wholesale actions at the very top, only before any line is touched: “I'll get these” and “I won't do it” (answered 11 October: “I won't do it” is gone after the first swipe and after a promise). “I won't do it” kills the link at once and frees one of the three slots.
  • A promised line drops out of every other live link, so two people never buy the same line. A promise nobody acts on rides the link's own expiry and goes back to the pool.
  • On the household's list each line is needed, claimed or bought, and claimed lines sit grouped under the link that promised them, with no names. Lines nobody got come back with a call to action.
  • Each link has a view: time left once at the top, opened or not, promised or not, counts you can tap to see the names. Unopened is blank but for the countdown; a link its shopper turned down is grey and goes after a dismiss or a day.

Changes earlier decisions not overridden quietly: each is a call for the owner
  • SL-J4 (locking). It locked a line on other links only once it was got or swapped, and “didn't find” did not lock. Now a promised line is hidden from every other link too, and a write to it is refused (409) (SL-D26). And the owner has reversed the old not-found rule (SL-D37, 11 October 2026): a not-found line locks like got and swapped, drops off the other live links and refuses a write from them (409), until the household says “Still need it”.
  • SL-J21 (not-found lines do not come back). Still true for the shopper alone. But the household's “Still need it” puts the line back in the pool, so it returns to every live link, including the shopper's whose link left it not found (“back on your list”). The owner confirmed this on 11 October 2026 (SL-D36).
  • SL-D20 and SL-D7 (“Won't be able to buy these” stays as it is). The button, its words and its place are untouched. What it does is extended (SL-D24): untouched lines, including promised ones, become not bought and the household is nudged; it never kills the link.
  • Link cap and slots (SL-D16). A link turned down or cancelled frees its slot at once. A grey row (up to a day) does not count toward the 3. Unopened and unsent links still count.
  • Cancel and Withdraw (SL-D15, SL-J14). Unchanged, one tap, on the row and at the foot of the link view. “I won't do it” is the shopper's side of the same coin and leaves a grey row; Cancel leaves none.
  • 48-hour rules (SL-D13, SL-J13). No new timer for a promise: it ends when the link does. The new 24-hour clock is only for clearing grey rows from the screen, not for any link. “Opened” is still the page running (SL-J13), and it is what moves a link from unopened to active.
  • SL-D4 and SL-J12 (the result card). The card and the “Link is live” line become one row per link that opens a view of that link; the counts live in the view. The earlier frames in Parts A and B keep the earlier shape.
  • SL-D2 (a line not found stays where it is). It now moves to a group at the top, “Still needs sorting”, with choices.
  • SL-J7 and SL-D5 (no confirm). “I won't do it” asks once, because Undo cannot take it back (SL-J32).
  • SL-D23 and SL-J32 (“I won't do it” after a promise). The mockup allowed it after a promise with nothing swiped, and said it cleared the promise. The owner has ruled that out (SL-D34): it is gone for good after the first swipe or a promise, so a rejected link never had anything claimed.
  • SL-J41 and SL-J42 (a promised count). The link row and the link view no longer count promised lines (SL-D35); the counts are got, swapped and not found, and untouched promised lines are a group of their own.
  • Unchanged: the drag colours, Undo, answered lines leaving, “Got something else”, Also got, multi-select, what reaches the Pantry and when (SL-J2: only got and swapped lines write to it; promises and not-bought lines never do).

A grey row is not numbered (“Turned down 1 hour ago”), because its slot may already belong to a new link. Other way: letters, or the creation time. Cost: a number can be reused after a link ends.
  34. SL-J34. How a claim shows on the household’s list (SL-D25, SL-D29). The line leaves the main list and appears in its link’s group below it. In the group it is the same row as today with a dashed tick box, a coloured edge in the link’s colour, and the words “Someone’s getting this” with a bag. The group header is the link’s marker, “Link 2” and “4 still to get”, and “Details” (to the link’s view). Its tick is not tappable: a member who buys a promised line themselves has no quick way to say so (open question 5). Needed lines stay first, then the groups, so what the household has to do is what they see first. Other way: claimed lines stay in place with a tag (the owner ruled this out). Cost: a line moves when it is promised.
  35. SL-J35. How a promise shows on another shopper’s page (SL-D26). As soon as the snapshot says `claim: “other”`, the line says “Someone else is getting this” for 1.6 s and leaves like a line another shopper got (SL-J20); a message says “Someone else is getting Spinach.” or “... 5 of these.” If the promise is released (the other link ends, is cancelled or is undone) the line returns with “... back on your list”. A write to a promised line from another link is refused with 409, as for a got line. Other way: keep the line, locked. Cost: the same as SL-J20.
  36. SL-J36. A link its shopper turned down, as a row (SL-D32, SL-D33). Grey and dashed, “Turned down 1 hour ago”, “The shopper said no. The link is dead.”, Dismiss. It has no countdown, as the owner said, so the 24-hour clear is not shown as one; the link view says “It clears itself a day after it was turned down, or sooner when you dismiss it.” It does not count toward the 3 links (SL-D23 frees the slot), so a new link can be made at once. Other way: keep it counting until dismissed. Cost: up to 3 live links plus any grey rows on screen.
  37. SL-J37. What happens to a promised line when its link ends. Whichever way the link ends (the window runs out or a member cancels it; a link the shopper turns down never held a promise, SL-D34), every line it promised and nobody answered is released back to needed: no “not bought”, no nudge, because nobody said they couldn’t get it. Lines already answered stay as they landed. Expiry and Cancel leave one quiet, dismissible row (“Ended 20 minutes ago. 3 lines went back on the list.”) so a claimed group does not vanish without a word; it clears after 24 hours like a grey row. Cancel by the member who pressed it leaves no row (they know). Other way: no row. Cost: one more kind of row (open question 8).
  38. SL-J38. What lines that still need sorting look like and what the two choices do (SL-D28). A nudge row in the slot (“3 lines still need sorting. Nobody has them. Your call.”) that jumps to a group at the top of the list, outlined and headed “Still needs sorting”. Each line says “Not bought · nobody has it” with a mark and has two buttons: “Still need it” (back to needed, open to every live link, including the shopper who left it) and “Take it off the list” (the list’s usual take-off). The nudge is the in-app row plus one push when the shopper taps “Won’t be able to buy these” or the promise ends; single left swipes do not each push. Other way: a banner only. Cost: the push rule is a guess (open question 9).
  39. SL-J39. Cancel and Withdraw against “I won’t do it” (SL-D15, SL-D16, SL-J14). Cancel and Withdraw are unchanged: one tap, no question, on the link’s row and at the foot of its view. A member’s cancel releases the link’s untouched promises and leaves no grey row. The shopper’s “I won’t do it” leaves the grey row (it can only happen before a promise, SL-D34, so there is nothing to clear). Both free the slot at once. Unopened and made-not-sent links still count toward the 3 until one of these happens or they end.
  40. SL-J40. What a promise covers, and undoing it. “I’ll get these” claims every line still open and not promised elsewhere at that moment; lines already answered are untouched. A line the household adds afterwards is not part of the promise (it is open to every link). Undo takes the whole promise back, one step, and says “Undo takes back: Promised 9 lines”. A promised line is an ordinary open line for the shopper: they can still swipe it either way, and a left swipe on it makes it not bought. Promising is optional: a shopper who only swipes, as before, works as ever.
  41. SL-J41. The counts in a link’s view (SL-D30, SL-D31). Revised by SL-D35. Up to three, in this order, each hidden when zero: got, swapped, not found. Promised is not a count. The facts above them say whether it was opened and whether it has promised (yes or no, no number). The lines it touched follow: promised-and-untouched lines as a group of their own, then the answered ones. A shopper who never promised still shows their got, swapped and not-found counts. Tapping a count opens its names; tapping again, or another count, closes it. With no script every list is simply shown. Other way: always show the counts including zeros (the owner said no padding).

After a bail the link stays usable until its window ends (SL-D12) but has nothing left to show; the household’s “Still need it” brings a line back to it (SL-J21’s change, answered by the owner: SL-D36).

Performance note (DESIGN.md section 6)
  • Shopper, default path loads: the page (30 KB for 12 lines, about 4.5 KB compressed; about 2.1 KB a line, answered lines included, so roughly 420 KB at the 200-line cap, which should compress to about 20 KB; both figures are estimates. The swipe reveals and the swap field are on every line), shopper.css (16 KB, 5 KB compressed) and one deferred script, shopper.js (36 KB with its comments, 11 KB compressed; about 30 KB and 9 KB compressed without the comment lines). No app stylesheet, no header, no bottom bar, no scribble script. The page is usable before the script arrives, and without it (as the multi-select form).
  • Shopper, while shopping: one small request per swipe (a few hundred bytes, answered with the state, so about 2 KB for 12 lines), plus the one open event stream. Nothing polls. Without a swipe nothing is sent.
  • Deferred until chosen (phase two): receipt.js (8 KB, 3 KB compressed) and receipt.css (2 KB) are fetched only when the person taps “Snap the receipt”. In phase one the chooser itself is not in the page.
  • Sent and “That link has finished”: the page and its stylesheet only. No script, no list rows.
  • Member, default path: the Shopping screen as today plus the hand-off slot's markup (drawn on the server), about 6 KB of CSS (2 KB compressed) and about 5 KB of script folded into shopping.js. The live feed is the Shopping screen's own, opened only while a link is out; with no link out nothing extra loads.
  • Second round (SL-D21 on), shopper: the promise block and strip add about 2 KB of markup and about 1.5 KB of script and CSS to the page; there is one more request per promise (commit) and one for reject, and no new connection. Nothing else is deferred differently.
  • Second round, household: the link rows, groups and the link's view are drawn by the server from the Shopping screen's data; the only new script is the counts' open and close (a few hundred bytes), loaded with the Shopping screen. The link's view is a page of its own, fetched when a link is tapped, not on the Shopping screen. mock-member.js and mock.js are mockup only.
  • Why reasonable now: the shopper opens this on mobile data, possibly with a weak signal in a shop. Everything they need arrives in the first response, and a missed event costs nothing because every event is the whole state.
  • This fragment page is heavier than any screen it shows: it loads about 30 frames at once so every state can be compared. Mockup only. mock.js (the pretend server, its stream and a second shopper) and kitchie.css are also mockup only.
