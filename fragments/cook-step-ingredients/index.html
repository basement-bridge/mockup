Cooking: the ingredients this step uses

17 Oct 2026: the owner chose option A. Phone: A as revised. Desktop: chips in the step card with a dock for the lists. B and C stay as drawn below A for the record. Step text in the samples carries no quantities (decision CSI-21).

Owner ask, 16 Oct 2026 (voice): while cooking step by step it is a real inconvenience not to know which ingredients the current step involves. Each step should show the ingredients and the amount that step uses (2 of the 3 eggs, 1 cup of flour), highlighted, with the full list kept neutral. Three options below; A is chosen. Data shape and schema: analysis. Job note: cook-step-ingredients.md. Nothing is built.

A Callout under the stepchosen

A highlighted card right under the step words lists only this step's ingredients with this step's amounts. Below it the rest of the list stays plain. Revised 17 Oct 2026 (owner decisions, below): a tight callout with the name left and the quantity right, a chip for where the rest goes, "Still to come" ordered by what the next steps need and capped only when room is tight, and "Already used" folded away.

B Chips inside the step card

Smallest footprint: the amounts sit as chips inside the step card, so the words never move down. The full list is one tap away in a disclosure. Wraps at 360; long lists collapse after five.

C Dock above the buttons

A one-line dock sits just above the step buttons (thumb reach) and opens in place to the full callout. Step words keep the whole screen. Costs one tap on a long list; on desktop the dock is simply the open side panel.

Content rule, owner, 17 Oct 2026

Step text carries no quantities. "Add the flour", not "add 2 cups flour". Every amount on a step comes from its link and scales with Serves like the main list, so two numbers can never disagree. Existing recipes that already have quantities in their steps keep them; nothing is rewritten.

Option A, revised 17 Oct 2026 (owner decisions, voice)

  • Already used folds away. One quiet collapsed row, "Already used (3)", opens on tap. No space or attention by default.
  • Still to come follows the next steps. What the next step or two needs comes first. Three are shown with "+N more" only when room is tight (a step photo, long step text, a small screen); with room it shows up to six. Use the Room control to see both.
  • Desktop mixes the options. Chips inside the step card (no callout) and a dock for "Still to come" (accent) and "Already used" (dull grey), opening in place. Labels, icons and position differ as well as colour.
  • The callout is economical. Content-sized, no fixed height, tight rows; 44px only on what is tappable.
  • Name left in heavier weight, quantity right in lighter weight. No amount, nothing on the right: no dash, no filler words.
  • Split ingredient: this step's portion plus a chip to where the rest goes: "→ step 7", "→ 4, 7". The last use has no chip. This replaces the "1 left for step 7" sentence.

What every option does the same (proposals, see the job note)

  • Only stored links. The screen reads the links saved with the recipe and does arithmetic on them. Nothing is guessed when you open a step.
  • Split ingredient (B and C; A changed, above). Shows "1 of 2" with the rest named by step: "1 left for step 2". An earlier use reads "1 already used in step 1".
  • Amounts scale with the Serves control by the same rule the ingredients screen uses. Fractions round to quarters and thirds; nothing is adjusted to make parts add up.
  • Unknown amount (B and C; A shows nothing, above) shows a dash and "Amount not given". It is never filled in.
  • Step with no ingredients (saved as checked and empty) says so in one quiet line. A step that was never checked says nothing.
  • Not tagged yet: the step screen is exactly today's. No strip, no hint, no nag. Half tagged: strips only on tagged steps, and no still-to-come list (it could be wrong).
  • Not colour alone: a heading with a count, a left bar plus a tick glyph, and words for state ("Already used", "step 4"). No gesture hint text.

Open questions, and what a wrong guess costs

  1. Strip first, or step words first, on a long list? A shows the strip first (capped at five). Cost: more scrolling before the step words end on 360. C avoids it.
  2. Show the still-to-come list at all? Proposal yes, only on a fully tagged recipe; on option A it is capped at three only when room is tight. Cost: clutter for people who only want the strip.
  3. "Used in step 1" for an ingredient the cook never ticked off. Proposal: it describes the recipe, not what was done. Cost: a cook who skipped a step sees it as used.
