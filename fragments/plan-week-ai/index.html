Planning the week, AI-led

Fragment. One phone, several states, all live: tap a meal, drag it to another day, swipe it away, block a night, step through weeks. Sample data only. Nothing here is decided; every choice not spoken by the owner is marked Proposal below.

Owner's words (9 October 2026, voice)
  • Start phased: the first state is nothing. (Later the same day the owner dropped the permanent note in empty slots: decisions 14 and 15.)
  • Copy last week is something the assistant offers as an opening move, and (decision 16) a small icon beside the cog.
  • Meals are dragged between days, and that is the only way to move one (decision 6). Days can be blocked (night out); a Settings stencil sets the usual days, and the grid only ever overrides one week (decision 4).
  • Locking a meal ties it to a recipe: what is on hand can be held (a soft label, not stock maths); what is missing can go to the shopping list one item at a time, and the assistant is where a substitute is worked out (decisions 9, 11).
  • A day holds several meal cards (one person on leftovers, another cooking). A whole-household meal is sized by the assistant for everyone; otherwise cards stack, told apart by avatar. Competing claims on one ingredient are made visible, not auto-resolved.
  • Leftovers is a protected category. People can rename it to whatever they like, but the app always knows it as Leftovers for its mechanics (no recipe, no reservation, no shopping), and it cannot be deleted.
  • Nobody edits another person's plan. Anyone can view anyone's, or focus on their own. Blocking is per person.
  • A meal card shows how many ingredients are missing as a small count, enough to tap and sort out later (the card opens the split; the names are not on the card).
Design decisions, 9 October 2026 (owner, by voice) Applied to this mockup first; the live app follows once this is settled. Each is its own decision so it can be traced.
  1. Week limits. Scrolling back stops at last week (nothing earlier is reachable). Planning ahead stops four weeks out from the current week.
  2. Week header gestures. On the "Week of ..." header, a long-press jumps back to the current week and a tap steps forward one week. The left/right arrows stay alongside it.
  3. Not cooking, look. Keep "Not cooking" (the moon was dropped later: decision 24). Remove the diagonal-slash fill; it was distracting.
  4. Blocking from the grid is one week only. No "just this week or every Wednesday" choice on the grid. Repeating days are a Settings matter (the usual-days stencil).
  5. Open your assistant must open the app. Superseded by decision 17. (First said as a working deep link into the person's chosen assistant app, with no copy-to-clipboard fallback.)
  6. Moving a meal is drag only. "Move to another day" and the day picker are gone from the meal sheet, on desktop and on touch alike, with no button fallback.
  7. Three meal-source states, labelled apart. (a) An unmodified saved recipe: tap through to that recipe. (b) A saved recipe the assistant changed: labelled Modified by assistant; the substitution is locked to that plan entry, so on cook day the substitute is what is held, shown and shopped for. (c) A recipe invented on the fly with nothing saved behind it: labelled Made up by your assistant. 7(b) needs follow-up build work (the entry carries its own ingredient list).
  8. Card meta line. Just who and how many: "Everyone · 4 people". No "sized by your assistant" or "planned by you".
  9. Hold. In the meal sheet the full-width "Reserve for Tuesday" button becomes a small Hold button to the right of the On hand heading. The on-hand items and their quantities are indented a little.
  10. Quantities follow the people count. On-hand and missing quantities scale with the people count on the card. For assistant-made and modified entries the stored ingredient list must be per person (quantity per person) so it can scale later. Data-model follow-up for the build, not only a UI change.
  11. Missing ingredients. No Substitute button. A plain hint line under the list: "Talk to your assistant about substitutes" (not tappable; the person is usually already talking to their assistant). Each missing item gets a small icon-only shopping-cart button, styled like Hold, to add just that item to the shopping list.
  12. Remove. "Remove from the day" becomes "Remove"; the sheet already shows which meal and day.
  13. Swipe on a meal card. Swipe left = Remove (the day stays open for replanning). Swipe right = Remove and mark the day Not cooking. Two different outcomes.
  14. Empty days carry no copy. The permanent "speak to your assistant to lock something in" text is gone; the dashed outline is enough.
  15. Idle nudge. After a stretch of doing nothing on a week with an empty day, a "curtain" drops and says Plan with your AI assistant. Never right when the plan opens. The owner's number: about five seconds.
  16. Copy last week is an icon. A small persistent icon beside the settings cog, not a button in the main view. Pressing it again after it was applied clears what it copied.
  17. The assistant runs inside Kitchie. "Open your assistant" is not an external deep link to a separate chat app; it is driven from inside Kitchie as an in-app agent. Supersedes decision 5. Architecture note for the build, beyond the mockup.
  18. View switch order. Left to right: Mine, Everyone, then the named other person (Jane in the sample). Replaces Mine, Jane, Everyone.
  19. Nudge anchors below the switch. The curtain drops from just under the Mine / Everyone / person row, not from the top of the screen.
  20. The curtain pushes the grid down. It makes room for itself instead of covering the week.
  21. No jitter on the way out. When the curtain lifts, the grid does not move back up with it; the space settles later (here: when the view or week changes, or after a pause).
  22. Modern Copy last week icon. The old clipboard emoji ("1990s") is replaced with a clean line icon (two overlapping pages) drawn like the rest of the page's controls.
  23. Tap an empty night to mark it Not cooking. On a night with no meal, a plain tap on the dashed slot sets Not cooking for this week; no menu needed. The three dots stay only on nights that already have a meal, where the swipe-right gesture also stays as it is.
  24. No moon. The blocked night shows just the words "Not cooking", no icon. The swipe-right tint reads "Not cooking" (not "Remove and not cooking").
  25. Swipe left is Replan. The word on the swipe-left tint is "Replan" instead of "Remove". The owner agreed it is the wording; the effect (meal removed, night stays open) is unchanged. Open: should Replan also open the assistant?
  26. Source labels only in the detail. "Modified by assistant" and "Made up by your assistant" leave the card face and show only in the meal sheet; the card was too crowded.
  27. Symbolic pills. The pills on a card are an icon plus a number, not words: missing (warning icon, count), on the shopping list (cart icon, count), held (bookmark). Same colours as before, from the theme. Full words stay in the accessible labels and the meal sheet.
  28. Item and quantity are one pair. In On hand and Missing the quantity sits right after the item name, on the same line and baseline (no fixed column, which would jump around with name length). The vertical misalignment is fixed.
  29. Substitute hint under the Missing heading. "Talk to your assistant about substitutes" sits directly under the heading, above the list.
  30. A substitution shows where the item is. On a modified meal the substitute is the item on the row (on hand or missing), and what it replaced follows in brackets with a strike-through: carrots (mushrooms). This replaces the "mushrooms → carrots" line in the sheet. The quantity is the substitute's. The substitution is still locked to that plan entry (decision 7b).
  31. Last week is read-only. A meal planned last week can be opened and looked at, not edited, moved, removed or held. Empty days there stay empty, with no way to plan into them. Settles the earlier open question ("yes, editable").
  32. A hold moves with its meal; removing the meal releases it. Confirms what was drawn.
  33. A hold is a real claim. Once one meal holds an ingredient, a second meal cannot hold it too (no double counting of what is on hand). Reverses the earlier drawn answer ("nobody is blocked; both cards show the tag"). Shown as a switched-off Hold button with a line naming what is already held, and a "held for Mon" tag on the row.
  34. A stencil day opened by hand shows it is an exception. A usual day off that you cooked this week gets a small line, "Usual day off, cooking this week". The stencil is a default; a one-week override always wins and a planted card is never silently hidden. Reconfirmed.
  35. Three day states that cannot be mistaken for each other. An empty night stays a dashed outline. A night blocked by hand for this week only is a solid filled block in the accent tint. A usual day off from the Settings stencil stays a plain outline with no fill. (A usual day cooked by hand keeps its small override line, decision 34.)
  36. This week's one-off block has its own colour. It is filled, not dashed and not plain, so it reads at a glance as something done this week, not the recurring pattern.
  37. Edge cases kept. Tapping a blocked night cooks it this week only (usual day: back to the override line; one-off block: back to dashed empty). The stencil is a default and a planted card is never hidden. Last week shows the same states but cannot be changed.
  38. Replan only removes the meal. Swipe left removes the person's own meal and leaves the night open to plan again; it does not open the assistant. Other people's meals are locked and cannot be swiped.
  39. Modern settings icon. The settings cog emoji becomes a line icon, matching the copy icon.
  40. No automatic household resizing. A whole-household meal does not follow household size changes by itself. Changing who a meal is for is revised through the assistant (and the People stepper on the card), not automatically.
  41. Drag only is an accepted limitation. People who cannot drag cannot move a meal; the owner accepts this for now.
Follow-up work beyond the mockup (these touch the real Kitchie backend and app)
  • Decision 5 is superseded by 17. The deep-link fix is no longer the plan: the assistant is an in-app agent. Decision 17 is an architecture question for the build (the agent is driven from inside Kitchie), not a link fix.
  • Decision 7(b). A modified entry carries its own locked ingredient list; reservation and the shopping list read that list, not the recipe's.
  • Decision 10. Store assistant-made and modified ingredient lists per person, so quantities scale when the people count changes.
Proposals (owner silent)
  • Week shown Monday to Sunday, dinner only (as in the four-direction mockup).
  • Mine / Everyone / Jane switch at the top of the grid (order is the owner's, 9 Oct): Mine is where blocking and the idle nudge apply; Jane (or any other member) shows just that person's plan, view-only; Everyone stacks all of them.
  • Week header: the earlier fragment had no arrows, so small ‹ › buttons are drawn (greyed at the limits) and a small label under the title says This week, Next week, In 3 weeks. The sample plan exists only for this week; the Settings stencil applies to every week.
  • Gestures: moving up or down drags to another day (a short press first on touch, so scrolling still works); sideways swipes. A swipe commits past about 90 px and tints the card with what it will do.
  • Idle nudge: once per visit, only in Mine with an open night; it drops from under the switch and pushes the week down, any action dismisses it, tapping it opens the assistant. With reduced motion it simply appears.
  • With the banner gone, the assistant has one full-width "Open your assistant" button under the switch, plus the curtain nudge. Tapping an empty night now marks it Not cooking.
  • People count is a plain stepper in the meal sheet (owner earlier: editable per meal). The per-person quantities in the sample are invented.
  • Hold is one tap for all on-hand ingredients and taps back off. The state word on cards and rows is "Held" (the owner said Hold for the button; the state word is mine).
  • The indent applies to the Missing items too, so the two lists line up.
  • Copy last week again removes only the cards it added and that have not been touched since.
  • The conflict is shown on the on-hand row as an orange "held for Mon" tag, and Hold is switched off with a short line; no dialog. Holds count across weeks, and a Jane-planned meal holds the same way.
  • Last week's sample plan (Monday to Thursday) exists so the read-only state can be seen; Copy last week and Open your assistant are switched off there.
  • Blocked days still show others' cards in Everyone view, with a small "Sam is out" mark. Dropping a meal on a blocked day is refused with a short message; dropping on a day that already has a card stacks it.
  • Settings stencil: seven day buttons per person, "Usual days off cooking".
