Sign-in with five providers

Owner, typed, 26 October 2026: mock up the sign-in screen for the built WorkOS and Clerk sign-in (platform #134, merged into uat, issue #128). Updated 26 October 2026 (owner, typed): five providers. Google and Microsoft through WorkOS, Facebook, X and TikTok through Clerk. Apple is dropped: the owner will not pay the $99 a year Apple developer fee; Apple stays behind the port in Platform as a later option and is not drawn on any new frame. No email and password, no phone, minimal data. Mockup only. Nothing built, nothing approved; every decision below is proposed and none is confirmed.

Owner’s phone pick (proposed): option D

D = B’s two-across grid + C’s “Other ways”. First visit shows all five. From the second visit this browser remembers every provider used (browser only, nothing on the server) and those lead, two across; an odd last button is centred at one column width (no filler provider); the rest sit under “Other ways”, always one tap away. A provider whose keys are not set is not shown; if only direct Google is configured the page is today’s single Google button. Each button wears its provider’s official branding. See section 5 (D) first; A, B and C below are kept for comparison and now show five providers and official branding too.

Owner, typed, 26 October 2026 (SP-D9, proposed): if the last row is odd, just centre it and make it the same width as the other buttons. This supersedes the earlier “fill the odd slot with another provider” (SP-D3) and “an odd last one spans the row” rules. Scope: the remembered-providers part and the main grid, and the same inside “Other ways”.

New, 27 October 2026 (proposed, owner picks): option E (section 5c) gives D a desktop page of its own: heading first, pot under it, a narrower centred block in three widths, at least four providers first, extras joining the same grid, last used first and featured, the order Email, Facebook, then the rest. Option F (section 5d) adds a compact row of logo-only provider buttons under a plain “Continue with email” button (no email field on our page). Phones keep D. Decisions SP-D12 to SP-D19, questions SP-O11 to SP-O17.

1 · What exists today (read from code, not run, not seen on a screen)

Read on 26 October 2026 from platform uat (b53453f: pages.ts, server.ts, authn-*.ts) and kitchie uat (32f66eb: skin.ts, web-signin.ts, platform-config.ts). The built app has not been checked against this mockup. The two frames are drawn from that code.

  • The six buttons are on Platform’s page, not on Kitchie’s. Kitchie’s screen sends p=google (a fixed value, PLATFORM_PROVIDER) to Platform’s /auth/start. Platform’s own /signin page is only reached by typing its address. So today a person cannot choose Apple or Facebook from Kitchie. This mockup is Kitchie’s screen with the six buttons (SP-O1).
  • Order built: direct Google, then WorkOS (Google, Microsoft, Apple), then Clerk (Facebook, X, TikTok). Proposed: Google, Apple, Facebook, Microsoft, X, TikTok (SP-2).
  • Two “Google” buttons while the direct one stays on: the WorkOS one reads “Google (WorkOS)” (buildAuthn). They are two members (SP-O3).
  • A button is off when its key is not set. WorkOS needs two names, Clerk three; some-but-not-all stops start-up. Nothing else switches either on.
  • Errors: every failure goes back to Kitchie’s own /signin?signin_error=1 (“That sign-in didn’t work. Please try again.”). A signed-in account with no member goes to /not-a-member. There is no page for redirecting or returning: Platform answers with 303s only.
  • Not a member screen says “Use a different Google account”: Google-only wording (SP-10).
  • Own page or vendor page, as built: see section 8.
2 · Option A · one column

Five full-width buttons stacked, glyph and one word each, “Continue with” said once above them. Everything visible, nothing to learn. Cost: the column is about 290px tall on its own, so on a 360 by 640 phone the kitchen shrinks to a strip, and five bars read as a form rather than a welcome.

3 · Option B · two across recommended

Two columns, three rows. Google is first. The tick on a button means “last used on this device” (frame in section 6). On desktop the column stays 30rem wide, centred, with a taller kitchen above it.

4 · Option C · one button, the rest folded in place

One clear action: the first button, full width. “Other ways” opens the other four in place (a plain details, no script, DESIGN.md 6a). Best on the calm and on the words budget. Cost: Facebook, Microsoft, X and TikTok people always pay a second tap on a new phone, and a person who does not see their provider may think it is missing. Tap “Other ways” in the frames.

5 · Option D · remembered pairs, the rest under “Other ways” owner’s pick

The rule, in order. (1) A provider whose keys are not set is not shown at all. (2) First visit (nothing remembered in this browser): all configured providers, two equal columns, an odd last one centred at one column width. (3) From the second visit: every provider this browser has used leads, most recent first, two to a row; no filler provider is added. (4) An odd last button, in the main grid or inside Other ways, is centred at exactly one column width (SP-D9). (5) The rest sit under Other ways, a plain details, always reachable. (6) If only direct Google is configured, the page is today’s single full-width Google button and nothing else (resilience: the build must degrade to that when the others are off or their keys are missing). Memory is a browser-only store (localStorage, key kitchie.signin.providers, an array of provider ids, no names, no account); nothing is sent to or stored on the server.

Last used (SP-D10). On a returning browser the most recent provider leads, first in the grid, and wears one small tick (“Last used” for a screen reader and a long press). Nothing else changes: the order, pairs, centring and Other ways are as before. The screen is the same one a first-time browser sees until something is remembered, and logging out never clears the memory or changes the layout, so sign out then sign in again shows the same screen. Browser only (localStorage, key kitchie.signin.providers); nothing on the server.

Returning, 1, 2 and 3 remembered (odd, even, odd). Frames: Facebook remembered (odd: Facebook centred alone, four under Other ways); Facebook and TikTok remembered (even); Microsoft, X and Facebook remembered (odd: the third centred, two under Other ways).

Button polish (SP-D11, proposed; owner, typed, 26 October 2026, with a screenshot of uat.kitchie.co): “the login buttons aren’t looking good”. What the screenshot showed on the built page: a heavy gold focus box round the whole “Other ways” row with the buttons almost touching it; Facebook solid blue, four black buttons with a thin border and Microsoft a flat grey fill that read as disabled; tight rows; and a wide, low-contrast grid on desktop. Now one treatment for every button: the surface fill, a border one step stronger than the theme border (a mix of --fg and --border), --fg text, --r-ctl corners, 56px high, one 28px icon column, 12px gaps. Hover raises the gold border and a faint tint; keyboard focus is a 2px gold ring with a 2px gap (a pill the size of the words on “Other ways”, not a box round the row). Brand colour lives in the mark only: Google and Microsoft draw their own colours, Facebook’s “f” is its blue (a white f vanishes on a light button), X, TikTok and Email follow the text colour. Nothing but a failing vendor (dashed, dimmed, alert shape, SP-D7) looks unavailable. Last used is now small words with the tick under the name (“Last used”, 12px) on the first remembered button only, instead of a bare tick at the edge. “Other ways” has 12px of air above the opened buttons. On desktop the group is capped at 25rem so the buttons stop stretching. Unchanged: option D, SP-D9 centring, remembered order, only-Google single button, browser-only memory, which provider is where. Email is drawn as the sixth provider (built since platform #144), last in the fixed order, shown in option D frames only.
