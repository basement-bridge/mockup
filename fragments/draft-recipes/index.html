Draft recipes: cook first, keep it after

Fragment for recipe #123. The owner's design, 15 October 2026 (voice): a person starts by cooking — often improvising, talking to the assistant — and only afterwards is offered the chance to keep it as a recipe. An improvised dish is a recipe from the first moment, carrying a draft status; saving is the reward for it turning out well, never the price of entry. Sample data. Use the Theme button for light and dark. Notes: draft-recipes.md.

This fragment proposes; it does not decide. Where there is a genuine fork it shows the options side by side, one marked lean with the reason. Nothing here is built into Kitchie or Recipe.

1. Where a draft lives in the library

Three ways a draft can appear among 42 kept recipes. The question underneath: is a draft a kind of recipe or a separate tray?

A — its own group, pinned abovelean

A section at the top of Recipes, only present when drafts exist. Disappears when the tray is empty, so a tidy library stays tidy.

B — a chip among the filters

"Not kept" joins the existing chips. Nothing changes on the default view, so a draft is invisible unless you look for it — and that is the risk: it expires unseen.

C — inline, in date order

Drafts sit in the one list with everything else, marked. Most honest to "a draft is just a recipe" — and the most cluttered, because a dish you may not keep sits beside dishes you cook weekly.

Why A: a draft has a deadline, and nothing else in this library does. A thing with a deadline has to be somewhere you pass without looking for it, which rules out B. And it should not sit permanently among things with no deadline, which rules out C. A section that appears only when there is something in it costs nothing when there is not. On the label: "Not kept yet" reads better than "Pending save" — it is what a person would say, and it implies the action rather than naming a system state. The owner's "pending save" is indicative; this is the proposal.

2. The offer to keep it

The one rule: it is offered, never required, and it never blocks cooking. So no modal, and nothing before the food is done.

A — when the cook endslean

The card appears under the ingredients once they stop cooking. One question, two taps, and "Not this one" is as easy as "Keep it".

B — available throughout, asked never

A quiet dashed card sits below the ingredients the whole time. Nothing ever interrupts; the person keeps it when they feel like it. Costs: most people will not notice it, and the draft expires.

Why A, with B's card underneath: they are not exclusive. Show B's quiet card during the cook (so the option exists the whole time, and the assistant can offer it mid-cook if the person says "that's good"), and turn it into A's direct question once cooking ends. Asking once, at the end, is not nagging — it is the only moment the person has the information. Proposal: if they tap neither, nothing is asked again; the draft simply sits in the library with its clock.

3. Keeping it: the one place structure is imposed

A draft accepts whatever the person gave — ingredients with no steps, steps with no ingredients, a blob of neither. Keeping it is the single point where the questions get asked, with the person there to answer. Amounts stay optional; what is firm is that an ingredient is an ingredient and a step is a step.

4. How the clock is shown

A draft is wiped if it is not kept within a day. That has to be visible without becoming a nag. Three readings, from loudest to quietest.

A — say the time in wordslean

"Keeps until 7pm tomorrow", turning to "Goes in 2 hours" near the end. Unambiguous, and readable by anyone.

🍲Garlic soy noodlescooked tonightKeeps until 7pm tomorrow
🥣Left-over soupcooked yesterdayGoes in 2 hours

B — a ring that empties

A 28px ring beside the row, draining over the day and turning warm at the end. Pretty, and silent — but a ring is not a time, and a person cannot tell 3 hours from 8.

🍲Garlic soy noodlescooked tonight
🥣Left-over soupcooked yesterday

Would need the words anyway, for screen readers — at which point A is doing the work.

C — silent until the last hours

No clock at all while there is plenty of time; a line appears only near the end. Quietest, and the one that surprises people: a dish vanishes that they never saw a deadline on.

🍲Garlic soy noodlescooked tonightNot kept yet
🥣Left-over soupcooked yesterdayGoes in 2 hours

Why A: this is destructive and automatic, so the deadline should be stated plainly from the first moment, not revealed late. A is also the only option that needs no second mechanism for accessibility. Proposal on wording: an absolute time while it is far off ("until 7pm tomorrow"), switching to a relative countdown under about four hours ("goes in 2 hours"), because that is when the number starts to matter. A wrong guess here costs a little clumsiness, nothing more — the threshold is one constant.

5. The assistant changes the dish, mid-cook

The person is cooking and talking: "no soy, use fish sauce — and I doubled it." That reaches the same object the screen is showing, so the screen changes in place (DESIGN.md section 6a). No reload, no navigation, no losing the ticks already made.

Proposals here, all unprompted: changed lines carry an outline for a few seconds rather than animating, because the person's hands are busy and a moving list is hard to re-find. The removed ingredient stays struck through until the next change instead of disappearing, so a mis-heard instruction is visible and correctable. And the screen echoes what it heard, not what it did — "you said: no soy, fish sauce instead" — because that is the sentence a person can check against their own memory. A wrong guess costs a confusing moment mid-cook; all three are small CSS and copy choices.

Desktop: the same thing in the rail layout

Drafts need no new desktop pattern. The group sits at the top of the column; selecting one opens the usual side panel, where Keep is the panel's primary action.

What this screen loads

Default path: nothing new. The draft group is part of the Recipes list already being fetched, and a draft row carries the same fields as a kept row plus an expiry timestamp. The keep sheet is fetched when the person taps Keep, not with the list (DESIGN.md section 6, load on engagement). The clock is computed in the client from one timestamp — no polling, no timer requests; the row re-reads it when the tab regains focus, which the desktop Pantry already does. The mid-cook update arrives on the connection the cooking screen already holds and is applied in place (section 6a), so no new request at all.

Decisions this fragment records

D1 — an improvised dish is a recipe from the first moment

Owner, 15 October 2026 (voice): "you, in fact, might have to think about the random ones as recipes you add with a different status."

Not a separate lightweight object that later has to be converted. Same object, same identity, from the moment cooking starts; only the status differs. Settled by the owner.

D2 — cooking is the entry point; keeping is offered afterwards

Owner, 15 October 2026 (voice): the person "might just start making a random recipe by just talking to AI… if they find that it's useful, they should be able to convert that into a recipe".

Saving is never a precondition for cooking or for logging a cook. Settled by the owner.

D3 — a draft expires in one day if not kept

Owner, 15 October 2026 (voice): "if they don't confirm it within an hour, sorry, a day, you just wipe it."

Settled as a day. Open: a day from what — the cook starting, or the cook finishing? This mockup assumes finishing, because that is when the person last touched it. Shown in the copy as a wall-clock time rather than "24 hours left", which is only possible once that is decided.

D4 — drafts are visible in the library, in their own group

Owner, 15 October 2026 (voice): "you'd want to have a category, uh, awaiting to save or something… pending save. Or or something like that. You come up with what's the more sensible thing."

Group, not a chip and not inline: section 1 above. Proposal on the label: "Not kept yet" rather than "Pending save". Owner to confirm the word.

D5 — loose on how much, strict on what kind

Owner, 15 October 2026 (voice): "there's still the concept of like you don't put ingredients as part of method. And you don't mix it up… you need to control that at the point of MCP… I'm happy for you to enforce that as a contract." And: "you must have ingredients and ingredients with quantity, um, quantity optional."

A draft accepts any subset — ingredients with no steps, steps with no ingredients, free text that is neither — and never validates for completeness. It does enforce typing: an ingredient is a typed ingredient (name required, amount optional), a step is a separate typed thing, and the two never share a field. Enforced at the MCP boundary. Structure is imposed only at keeping: section 3. Settled by the owner.

D6 — assistant edits mid-cook land on the screen in place

Owner, 15 October 2026 (voice): "they might then start uh, giving you… MCP instructions to fiddle with it. Which then again should go and update what's on the screen."

The MCP and the screen act on one object; the screen updates where the person is looking. Consistent with DESIGN.md section 6a. Settled by the owner. How the changed lines are marked is a proposal: section 5.

Open questions, and what a wrong guess costs

  1. When a draft expires, what happens to the cook-log entry that pointed at it? The owner's lean is that history must stay true — the log keeps a frozen snapshot of what was cooked even after the draft is gone. Not decided. Cost of guessing wrong: the person loses "what did I actually cook last Tuesday", silently, and there is no way back. This is the one question in #123 that a naive delete would corrupt. Nothing in this fragment depends on the answer; the library is the same either way, but the cook log's own screens are not, so they are not drawn here.
  2. Does a draft count as a recipe anywhere else? Search, the chips, what_can_i_cook, the week plan. If a draft is a recipe with a status, it is eligible everywhere unless excluded. Proposal: excluded from planning and from "what can I cook" (you cannot plan next Tuesday around something that evaporates tomorrow), included in search so a person can find it. Cost: a plan built on a dish that disappears.
  3. Is the expiry a household thing or a personal thing? If two people cook, whose draft is it, and can the other keep it? Cost: one member silently losing another's dish, or a draft nobody feels responsible for.
  4. Can a kept recipe go back to being a draft? Recipe's rule is that nothing a person made is deleted — put away replaces deletion. A draft that evaporates sits against that rule. It probably reconciles as "a draft is not yet something the person made", but that is a judgement the builder should state rather than assume. Cost: a rule quietly broken, and a precedent for deleting other things.
  5. What does the assistant say when a draft is about to go? Nothing in this fragment notifies outside the app. A push or a chat message would be the obvious place and is not drawn, deliberately: it crosses into the assistant's behaviour rather than the screen's. Cost: drafts expiring while the person is nowhere near the Recipes tab — which is most of the time.
