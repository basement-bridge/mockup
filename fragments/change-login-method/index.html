Change login method

Mockup only. Nothing built, nothing approved. Owner (Arjan), from the voice and text hand-off log, 27 October 2026: “Change login method” is a swap, not an add. A signed-in person taps it in their profile, signs in with the new provider, and Kitchie attaches the new identity. The old method stays valid and is retired only after the new one has worked 5 times. This page draws the six screens; every choice the owner did not state is marked Proposal. Section 7 adds recovery by admin Re-invite (owner, voice, 27 October). Builds on the sign-in fragment (sign-in-providers, SP-D numbers).

The rule behind every screen

Failures are ours, never the person’s. A swap starts only after the new provider has signed the person in. A failure never counts, never retires anything, and the old login keeps working. Every message blames us. Nothing on the sign-in page shows that two methods exist.

1 · The profile, with the Change login method row

Where it lives is a proposal (CL-D12): the Me page, in a small “How you sign in” card, because it is about one person, not the household. The row is a plain 56px link, no warning colour. The card names the login the person uses now. Seen on a screen? No. UAT answers 401 to a signed-out reader, so the frames are drawn from the mockup’s own profile menu and Profile window (fragments/profile-about, fragments/desktop-profile), not from what UAT shows today.

2 · The provider picker

Opens when the row is tapped: a bottom sheet on a phone, a dialog on desktop. It lists only providers that are switched on and healthy right now (CL-D9). The current one is marked “Current” with a tick (shape, not colour) and cannot be tapped. A provider that is off or down is simply absent, not greyed and not explained. Tapping a provider goes straight to that provider to sign in; nothing is saved until it has signed the person in.

3 · Mid-swap line, and the finish

A plain line in the profile, exactly the owner’s wording: Switching to Microsoft: 2 of 5 sign-ins done. Under it five markers (filled with a tick for done, hollow for to do; the line carries the meaning, the markers are decoration and hidden from screen readers) and one sentence that says the old login still works. The proving sign-in does not count, so a new swap shows 0 of 5 (CL-D15). On the 5th success the old login is retired in the same step and the card shows the finish. After that a quiet line keeps the history. “Stop switching” is a proposal (CL-D20).

4 · What the sign-in page says

Nothing on the sign-in page shows that two methods exist (owner): during the swap it is the ordinary page. The nudges sit where they cannot give anything away. During the trial, after a person signs in with the old login, a note appears on the first screen they land on (not a blocking screen; “Got it” hides it until the next old-login sign-in). After retirement, the old login no longer signs anyone in; the person lands back on the sign-in page with a note that names where they moved, and the new provider leads with the accent ring (as “Last used” does in option E). If we no longer know where they moved, the note is plain and names nothing.

5 · Failure messages

Failures are ours, never the person’s. Every message says first what happened to us, never what the person did, says nothing changed, and says the old login still works. A cancel is not called a failure. None of these counts toward 5, retires anything, or touches the old login. They appear in the profile, because a swap starts from there (signed in already). The paused message is the fallback proposal (CL-D19).

6 · Support and admin view of one person

Read only (CL-D21). For each person: current method, the swap if any, N of 5, what is pending retirement, and the last failure and when. It also shows how many old-login sign-ins happened during the trial, to make clear they cost nothing. Provider names and times only: no email, no provider ids. Where it lives, and whether support may pause or cancel a swap, are open (CL-O4, CL-O5).

7 · Recovery: Re-invite (admin only)

Owner, voice, 27 October 2026

Nobody signs up without an invite, so a signed-out person trying a different provider is refused and no empty account is made. Change login method works only while signed in; someone who lost their old login cannot use it. Recovery is a re-invite, admin only: on the person’s existing row in the members list an admin taps Re-invite. Kitchie makes a one-time invite tied to that member; the person opens it, signs in with whatever login they have now, and that identity attaches to the same member (history, meals, notes stay). One tap, no passwords or resets, admins never see the email. Never merge, never link by email, refuse plainly if the identity belongs to another member. The old login is replaced the moment the new one attaches, atomically.

Six screens follow (7a to 7f). Everything the owner did not state is marked Proposal in the caption or in the decisions (CL-D30 on). Nothing is built, and the schema is reserved for the owner’s explicit go.

7a · The members list

The admin sees Re-invite on every other person’s row (not their own, CL-D33). A non-admin sees the same list with no button at all: it is not drawn, not greyed. The button is a 44px target; the row keeps the person’s role under the name.

7b · Admin: confirm, then share

One tap on Re-invite opens a short confirm that says what will happen. Create invite then shows the link with Share and Copy. Kitchie holds no email for the person, so the admin sends the link themselves (CL-D34, Proposal). The share step says plainly that anyone who opens it can become Jo (CL-O10).

7c · What Jo sees: the invite landing page

Opened from the link, signed out. It greets Jo by name and offers the providers that are on and healthy now (same list as the sign-in page). Whatever Jo picks signs in and attaches to Jo’s own member. Errors never blame Jo; an invite that cannot be used says what to do next.

A different provider, no invite (CL-D23, owner). A signed-out person who presses a provider Kitchie does not know, with no invite, is refused. No account is made. The message points them to the admin Re-invite, which is how a person who lost their login gets back.

7d · Pending invite: Withdraw and Re-send

While an invite is waiting, the person’s row says so in words with an envelope (shape, not colour), and offers Re-send and Withdraw. Re-send makes a new link and the old link stops (CL-D31). Withdraw is one tap and the row goes back to normal. An invite nobody opened turns into “expired” with a fresh Re-invite. Only admins see any of this.

7e · Done

Jo sees a plain welcome that says the old login has been replaced. The admin’s row is an ordinary row again (with a quiet “Reconnected today” for a day). History gets one line with the note Re-invited by Priya (CL-D32, Proposal). The attach and the replacement happen in one step, so Jo is never left with two logins or none.

Decisions

Numbered CL-D1 and on, continuing a new series. “Owner” means stated by the owner; “Proposal” means the mockup chose where the owner was silent. None is confirmed.

  • CL-D1 Owner Change login method is a swap, not an add, like changing an email address.
  • CL-D2 Owner Flow: signed-in person taps Change login method in their profile, signs in with the new provider (proves control), Kitchie attaches the new identity.
  • CL-D3 Owner The old method is not retired yet. It stays valid, and nothing on the sign-in surface shows both exist.
  • CL-D4 Owner The old method is retired only after the new one has been used successfully 5 times. Five is a configuration value. Only successful new-method sign-ins count; the old method signing in never counts and never costs anything.
  • CL-D5 Owner On the 5th success the old method is retired in the same step. Only ever one previous method at a time.
  • CL-D6 Owner During the trial, signing in with the old method shows a nudge toward the new one. After retirement, trying the old method on the sign-in page shows a nudge: “you moved from <old> to <new>”, naming it if known.
  • CL-D7 Owner Attach and retire are atomic. If the new identity already belongs to another member: refuse and say so. Never merge two people. Never link by email.
  • CL-D8 Owner A provider is offered only if switched on and healthy now. A swap starts only after the new provider has already signed the person in.
  • CL-D9 Owner Failures never count toward 5, never retire the old method, and the old method keeps working. Failure messages blame us, never the person.
  • CL-D10 Owner State is visible: a plain line in the profile (“Switching to Microsoft: 2 of 5 sign-ins done”) and a support/admin view per person (current method, pending retirement, N of 5, last failure and when).
  • CL-D11 Proposal The number 5 is read from configuration, never typed into the screens: the line is built as “N of {limit}” and the help text uses the same value.
  • CL-D12 Proposal The row lives on the Me page in a “How you sign in” card (desktop: the Profile pane), not in Settings or Household.
  • CL-D13 Proposal Picker: sheet on phone, dialog on desktop; current provider marked with a tick and the word Current, not tappable; absent providers are not shown and not explained; empty list gets one plain line that blames us. Order is the fixed order of the sign-in fragment.
  • CL-D14 Proposal The status line is one sentence in the card with five decorative markers (done = filled with a tick, to do = hollow) and one help sentence that says the old login still works. Paused swaps use dashed markers and the word “paused”.
  • CL-D15 Proposal The proving sign-in (the one that attaches the new identity) does not count: a fresh swap reads 0 of 5. This follows the owner asking for a 0 of 5 screen; it is stated here so it can be corrected.
  • CL-D16 Proposal Trial nudge: a dismissible note on the first screen after an old-login sign-in, with a link to the profile line. It never blocks, and it shows again on the next old-login sign-in. The sign-in page itself is unchanged during the trial.
  • CL-D17 Proposal After retirement the old identity is remembered only as “moved to {provider}” (a provider name, no email, no account id) so the nudge can name it. If that is gone, the plain no-name note is used.
  • CL-D18 Proposal Failure copy as drawn (five messages). A cancel is “No change made” and is not called a failure. The “belongs to another member” message never says who.
  • CL-D19 Proposal, with a recommendation Safe fallback for a repeatedly failing new provider: after 3 failed new-method sign-ins in a row (a configuration value) the swap pauses by itself; the old login is kept as is; the count is kept; the profile says so in plain words and blames us; the swap resumes by itself when the provider is healthy again. Recommended over leaving it counting (a failing provider could strand the person) and over cancelling (they lose their progress).
  • CL-D20 Proposal “Stop switching” in the profile ends a swap that has not finished: it detaches the not-yet-primary new identity and clears the count. The old login is untouched. Needs a confirm step in the build (not drawn).
  • CL-D21 Proposal Support view is read only, shows provider names and times only (no email, no provider ids), and includes the number of old-login sign-ins during the trial.
  • CL-D22 Proposal Layout and look: the app’s existing tokens, 56px rows, 44px controls, the SP-D11 button treatment for the picker, a shape (tick, dash, pause) wherever colour could carry meaning.
  • CL-D23 Owner Nobody signs up without an invite. A signed-out person who tries a different provider with no invite is refused, and no new empty account is created.
  • CL-D24 Owner Change login method works only while signed in (CL-D2 stands). A person who lost their old login cannot use it.
  • CL-D25 Owner Recovery is a Re-invite, admin only: on the person’s existing row in the household members list an admin taps Re-invite.
  • CL-D26 Owner Kitchie creates a one-time invite tied to that member. The person opens it, signs in with whatever login they have now, and that identity attaches to the same member: history, meals and notes stay.
  • CL-D27 Owner Not admin-heavy: one tap, no passwords and no resets (Kitchie holds none), and admins never see the person’s email.
  • CL-D28 Owner Never merge two people, never link by email. If the identity already belongs to another member, refuse plainly; it is never the person’s fault.
  • CL-D29 Owner The old login is replaced at the moment the new one attaches, atomically. No trial, no count: this is recovery, not a swap.
  • CL-D30 Proposal Invite expiry is 7 days, read from configuration (never typed into a screen; the landing and row text use the same value). The invite works once.
  • CL-D31 Proposal One pending re-invite per member. Re-invite while one is pending is not offered; Re-send makes a new link and the old one stops working; Withdraw ends it and the row returns to normal.
  • CL-D32 Proposal History gets one line when the person reconnects, with the note “Re-invited by <admin>” (admin name only; no provider id, no email). Creating, withdrawing or expiring an invite leaves no History line.
  • CL-D33 Proposal The Re-invite button is a 44px text button on other people’s rows. It is not rendered for non-admins (not greyed), and not on the admin’s own row (they use Change login method).
  • CL-D34 Proposal Confirm then share: one confirm that says what will happen, then a share step with Share and Copy link. Kitchie sends nothing itself, because the admin never has the person’s email; the admin passes the link on.
  • CL-D35 Proposal Landing page: “Welcome back, <first name>” and “Sign in to reconnect your kitchen.” Providers are the on and healthy ones. Errors: used, expired, and one message for withdrawn, replaced or unknown links (so a link never tells a stranger which). “Belongs to someone else” does not use up the invite.
  • CL-D36 Proposal The pending row says Re-invite sent. Expires in N days. with an envelope; expired says Re-invite expired. with a clock. Words and shapes, not colour alone.
  • CL-D37 Proposal Completion: Jo sees “You’re back” and that the old login has been replaced, then opens the kitchen. The admin sees an ordinary row with a quiet “Reconnected today” for one day. No push or email to anyone.
  • CL-D38 Proposal A Re-invite works even if Jo’s old login still works (the admin cannot tell). If a Change login method swap is in progress, the attach ends it: the new identity is the only login and the count is cleared.
  • CL-D39 Proposal The unknown-login refusal on the sign-in page names the way back: “ask a household admin to Re-invite you”, and offers Try another login. It creates nothing and does not say whether the account exists anywhere.

Open questions (owner picks)

  • CL-O1 Is the Me page the right place for the row, or Settings?
    If wrong: A wrong guess moves one row.
  • CL-O2 Does the proving sign-in count as the first of the 5?
    If wrong: Drawn as no (0 of 5). If yes, the swap finishes one sign-in sooner.
  • CL-O3 Pause rule: is 3 failures in a row right, does the count stay while paused (proposed), and should it resume by itself (proposed) or wait for the person?
    If wrong: Too low pauses on a blip; too high strands someone.
  • CL-O4 Where does the support view live: Platform admin, or Household admin for each household?
    If wrong: Decides who can see another person’s sign-in state.
  • CL-O5 May support pause, cancel or force-retire a swap, or is the view read only for now?
    If wrong: Drawn read only.
  • CL-O6 Can a person stop a swap (CL-D20), and does the new identity then stay attached or leave?
    If wrong: Proposed: it leaves.
  • CL-O7 How long do we remember “moved from X to Y” after retirement, and may it be shown to someone who signs in with the old identity (it proves they once had that account)?
    If wrong: A privacy and tidiness call; forever is the worst case.
  • CL-O8 Is there a time limit on a swap that never reaches 5?
    If wrong: Proposed: none; the old login just keeps working. A stale swap may sit forever.
  • CL-O9 Which sign-ins count: only interactive ones, or also a session that renews?
    If wrong: Drawn as interactive only.
  • CL-O10 The invite link is a secret: anyone who opens it first becomes Jo. Do we add a check, such as a short word the admin tells Jo aloud, or accept one-time plus 7 days plus Withdraw?
    If wrong: Drawn without a check. A leaked link means someone takes over a member, history and all.
  • CL-O11 May an admin re-invite another admin? An admin could use their own link to become another member.
    If wrong: Drawn: yes for any other member, never on one’s own row. Wrong means a member can be taken over by an admin, with no trace beyond the History line.
  • CL-O12 Does the admin pass the link on (drawn), or may Kitchie send it somewhere? Kitchie would need the person’s contact, which admins must never see.
    If wrong: Wrong costs a feature: either a sending path or a share sheet.
  • CL-O13 Is the 7-day expiry right, and may a household set it?
    If wrong: Drawn as one config value for all.
  • CL-O14 Does Jo, or the old login, get any notice when an attach replaces the old login (CL-D37 says none)?
    If wrong: Wrong means a quiet replacement nobody sees.
  • CL-O15 Link opened while signed in as someone else: sign out first, or refuse?
    If wrong: Not drawn. Wrong could attach a login to the wrong member; the build needs a rule.
  • CL-O16 Should withdrawn and expired invites leave a History line (CL-D32 says no) and for how long is History kept?
    If wrong: Wrong means the audit trail misses attempts.
  • CL-O17 Removed members use Restore (removed-members). Is Re-invite hidden for them?
    If wrong: Drawn: hidden; a removed person is not on the members list.
