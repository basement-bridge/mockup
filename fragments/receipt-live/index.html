Live receipt scan, phone

Fragment, mockup only, phone only, no app build. This shows what Kitchie v0.103.0 (kitchie PR #695) already does, so the owner (Arjan) can see it before it goes to production. Open the camera from the basket bar, point at a receipt, watch the boxes and prompts, and let it take the photo. Sample data, an invented receipt, no camera is ever opened. Use the Theme button (bottom right) for light and dark.

Notes: what the default path loads, and what is deferred
  • This page loads the shared theme.css, shared.css, theme.js and two small files of its own (cam.css, cam.js, under 20 KB together). No images, no fonts beyond the theme, no libraries. It draws everything with markup and inline icons. It never calls getUserMedia, never reads a file and never touches the network.
  • In the build nothing new loads on page load. The scan code arrives only after a shop has begun, as in v0.91.0. The camera stream, the looking and the reader's worker start on engagement: the person taps Scan. On a slow or metered connection with the engine not yet downloaded, the viewfinder opens as a plain camera (shutter only, no looking, nothing downloaded) and the existing download question comes after the photo.
  • Deferred here, on purpose: desktop and tablet (the build shares one viewfinder, a webcam on desktop; not drawn here), the shopper-link version, and the existing "Done shopping" flow behind the other button.
  • Mockup and build stay close. Markup is shaped like the build's: a Basket region holding the buttons, a status line with role=status, a progressbar for steadiness, three counted chips, tokens only. Class names here use a cs- prefix for the page; the build keeps its own names. Not copied from the build's source, so differences are not listed yet.

1. Try it

Tap Scan in the phone. With Receipt in view it plays the happy path: looking, found, steady for 1.2 s, photo, review. The other conditions hold one state still so you can look at it; the shutter and Choose a photo work in all of them. Nothing is saved.

2. The three overlay treatments

Colour is never the only cue. Each treatment has its own icon, its own word and its own line style, and the three counted chips under the picture repeat all three.

Milk

On your list. Tick, the list item's name, solid thick filled box. The strongest. Direct match to the shopping list.

Swap?

Usual swap. Arrows, "Swap for Butter?", dashed box with a light fill. A known stand-in, such as margarine for butter. It is a guess and writes nothing.

Other

Other things bought. Plus, "Other", dotted hairline box, no fill. Not on the list, not a swap. Lines that are not items (totals, dates) get a hairline only.

3. Every state, side by side

Same phone frame, one state each, in the order a scan goes. On a wide screen they sit in a grid.

Decisions recorded (as built in kitchie PR #695, v0.103.0; owner voice, 12 October 2026: "best effort, sensible decisions, I'll check before productionization")
  1. CS-D1 Entry is a camera button in the shopping basket bar, next to Done shopping.
  2. CS-D2 A live viewfinder (rear camera preferred) reads the receipt as you hold it. Boxes on read lines: on your list (solid thick filled, tick, list name), usual swap (dashed, arrows, "Swap for X?"), other things bought (dotted, plus, "Other"), non-item lines (hairline).
  3. CS-D3 Three counted chips repeat the signs and words, so colour is never the only cue.
  4. CS-D4 Prompts: "Hold steady, the image is blurry." (confident); "Can't find a receipt (best guess): ..." (softer, for very little text, a flat view or text that is not items and prices); "Receipt found. Hold steady..." with a progress bar; "Steady. Taking the photo...". A negative prompt needs two looks in a row.
  5. CS-D5 Auto-capture when steady (last 4 looks read as a receipt, same words in nearly the same places, none blurry, over at least 1.2 s). The manual shutter is always there.
  6. CS-D6 Haptics: 15 ms the first time a list line is a direct match two looks running, once per line per viewfinder; 30 ms on the photo.
  7. CS-D7 The review is the existing one, unchanged: matches ticked, doubtful ones need a Confirm, leftovers must be answered (add as bought, substitute, skip) before the single write.
  8. CS-D8 Fallbacks: no camera API gives the old one-tap picker; a refused or missing camera gives a screen with "Take or choose a photo"; Choose a photo is on the viewfinder at all times.
  9. CS-D9 The camera and looking stop after 150 s without a photo, until Resume. The stream is released on Done, Escape, a photo, an error or that rest.

Proposal (this page's own choices, the owner was silent): the sample receipt and list; where the status line, steadiness bar and chips sit in the frame; the exact words on the refused screen and the Review buttons ("Back", "Save to the list"); the hint pill for the haptic tick; the resting overlay's look; the sample photo picker. The build's pixels may differ; the page is for seeing how it behaves.

Not verified without a real phone
  • How the camera really behaves: focus, exposure, noise, rotation, frame rate, iOS Safari playsinline and autoplay.
  • How the blur number and the steadiness tolerance feel in a shaking hand, on a real printed receipt (only a synthetic one has been used).
  • Speed on a phone's CPU, and battery and heat over a long scan. Live reading ran at roughly 0.3 to 1 look a second on a loaded sandbox, below the 2 to 4 the ceiling allows.
  • The vibration motor (some phones and browsers have none; the tick is optional) and the real permission prompt.
  • The usual-swap list on real receipts.
  • Everything on this page: the animation timing here is a stand-in for the real thing.
Still open
  • Blur threshold. Set at 0.10 on a synthetic receipt at a 720 px frame (crisp reads 0.5 to 1.5, 3 px blur 0.01). It depends on text size and camera noise, so it needs a real phone before it is right.
  • Steady tolerance. 4 looks over 1.2 s, with 75% of boxes within 0.6 of a line height. Too tight and it never fires; too loose and it takes a smear.
  • Swap list. A built-in short guess (butter, margarine, spread; milks; mince; chicken, turkey, duck; cheeses; oils; bread; pasta; rice; fruit; greens; fish; cereal; coffee, tea). The household's own substitution rules are not used. Wiring them in means the server sending them to the page. Who owns the list, and whether the household can edit it, is the owner's call.
  • Low-confidence matches. The overlay does not claim a "low" match as a direct hit; it draws a swap if the item is in a family, else "other". The review still says Check.
  • Haptics. Direct matches only, or swaps too? Off by a setting?
  • Prefill. A live swap is not carried into the review's "Instead of..." answer; it stays the person's own choice. Should it be offered as the first choice?
  • Long receipts. One frame holds one screenful. Nothing here scans a long receipt in several parts.
