# Photo Enhance, option C: AUTO-ON

Open `index.html` (served: `python3 -m http.server`, then `fragments/photo-enhance/c/`). Phone first, 360 and 390, light and dark. Uses the shared engine (`../engine/enhance.js`) on the real samples, plus "Try your own photo" (pure client side). The small "Mockup" button (bottom right) jumps to every state: each sample, banner / step / more / cooked / after cooking, and the edge states.

## The option in one paragraph
The picture opens already enhanced at the default level (Balanced). No extra step, no extra screen: Enhance lives on the existing review screen of Add / Replace. A small pill on the photo says **Enhanced**; tap it to toggle Original, tap its chevron for strength (Light / Balanced / Full). Press and hold the photo itself to see the original. One line under the photo says in plain words what was done ("A little brighter, colours a touch richer, edges a touch crisper") with a one-tap **Undo**. "What changed" opens the engine's notes. One button: **Use photo**. Undo is the skip, so there is no separate "Skip" button.

## Decisions (Proposals: the owner made none of these; the owner's words are in the PR)
- **PE-C-O1 Auto-on at Balanced. Proposal.** The picture arrives enhanced; the person undoes, not opts in. Why: no step to learn, nothing to find, the most people get the better picture. Cost: see PE-C-O5.
- **PE-C-O2 Where in the flow: on the existing review screen, on the whole picture, before the 240 px square is cut. Proposal.** The analysis sees the whole scene, so a crop can never change how the photo looks. The square is cut from the SAME enhanced result (engine `encodeSet({square:240})`), so it is never enhanced twice. The Crop and rotate sheet for already stored photos does not offer Enhance (the stored photo is already enhanced; enhancing again would stack). Open question below.
- **PE-C-O3 What persists between photos: strength and on/off, for this visit only. Proposal.** A person who undid Enhance on one photo is not auto-enhanced on the next one in that visit (nothing is more annoying than "I said no"); a new visit starts auto-on again. Built as `sessionStorage` (mockup key `peC`). Wrong guess costs: someone who undid once and wanted it back on the next photo taps the pill once; someone who wanted it always off gets it back next visit.
- **PE-C-O4 The control: one split pill, top left of the photo.** Left half = state in words and the toggle (`aria-pressed`), right half = strength menu (`aria-haspopup`). Both halves are 44 px tall. While the original is held the pill reads "Original" with a dashed border. The line below carries the one-tap Undo (same effect as the pill toggle, but it says "Undo", which people know, and it carries the "what happened" words that build trust). Hold-to-compare is on the photo itself; the pill toggle is the way for anyone who cannot hold.
- **PE-C-O5 Cost of a wrong guess.** Auto-on means every uploaded photo is changed unless noticed. Mitigations in the design: the engine is conservative (clamped, skips when nothing is clearly wrong), the original is showing the instant the picture is picked and the enhanced one swaps in, the line says what changed, hold shows the original at once, Undo is one tap. Cost that remains: a photo nobody looked at is stored enhanced, and the server keeps no originals (PE-C-O13). An enhanced-wrongly photo can be replaced from the phone's library, not reverted.
- **PE-C-O6 Accessibility.** Hold target is a real `<button>` ("Your photo. Hold to see the original.", described by a hidden hint that also names the toggle). Space or Enter held on it = original, release = back; blur releases. Pill toggle is the non-hold way, `aria-pressed`, with the label spelling out the action ("Enhanced. Tap to show the original."). The strength menu is `menuitemradio`, arrow keys, Escape returns focus. The line under the photo is `aria-live="polite"` so a level change or Undo is spoken. No long-press callout or selection (`-webkit-touch-callout:none`, `user-select:none`, `touch-action:pan-y` so the page still scrolls and a vertical drag cancels the hold, pointer capture, `contextmenu` blocked). `prefers-reduced-motion`: the pulse on "Enhancing…", the chevron turn and the progress sweep stop. Tap targets are 44 px; colour is never the only signal (words and icons change).
- **PE-C-O7 First paint and "Preparing".** The picked file shows at once as a plain `<img>` (no engine code needed), then the engine's `render("off")` takes over, then the enhanced version swaps in. Until it does the pill says "Enhancing…" and the original is showing (that is the whole "Preparing" state; there is no spinner screen). A level that is already cached swaps with no busy frame in between (`session.ready()`); the other levels are prefetched in the background after the first one arrives. Measured here (headless Chromium, software rendered, a phone is 2 to 4 times slower): original on screen in 0.12 to 0.19 s, enhanced in about 1.0 s (dark), 1.8 s (17 MP large), 2.0 s (noisy); confirm to result 0.9 s, 1.4 s, 3.4 s.
- **PE-C-O8 Confirm: the only moment anything is encoded.** "Use photo" calls `session.encodeSet(level, {maxEdge: 1600 | 960, square: 240})`; the screen shows "Making the 1600 px copy" with the engine's real progress; then the result card shows the long edge and KB of the picture and the square and "was N KB" for the original. `PhotoEnhance.stats.encodeCalls` is 0 until then (checked by the tool). Then the existing queue takes over (tile chip `.pf>.pq` and floating `.pqpill`, same words as Kitchie's `photo-queue.js`: Waiting, Uploading 40%, Photo is being prepared, Done). Cancel never encodes anything.
- **PE-C-O9 Level off at confirm** (Undo tapped) takes today's path exactly: the original shrunk and squared, no enhancement, same sizes and WebP. So Undo IS "upload the original as today".
- **PE-C-O10 Cook-mode path ("add a photo after cooking").** Same screen, same one button; auto-on is the point there: hands are busy, nobody wants to choose. The aim line says "What you made tonight". Proposal: nothing extra in cook mode, no level picking by default (the chevron is still there). Jump: Mockup > "After cooking".
- **PE-C-O11 Edge states and thresholds. Proposals.** Engine unsupported: no pill, no line, no notes, the screen is today's screen, silent (the pill can show "Enhancing…" for the 100 to 300 ms until the engine answers; accepted). Small photo (long edge under 600 px, Proposal; the engine itself says "small" under 480): no Enhance, one line "Small photo (420 px). It goes as it is." Big photo (12 MP and up, Proposal): the line under the photo says so, the preview stays at 1280 px, the full copy is made on Use photo with a progress bar ("This is the one slow moment, a few seconds"). Already good (engine `good`): the pill says "Looks good", the line says "Looks good already. Balanced barely changes it." and Undo stays; we do not pretend it was improved. HEIC the browser cannot open: placeholder, "This phone could not open that kind of photo, so it goes as it is" (existing behaviour, server prepares it). Encode fails: "That one would not polish. Send your original as it is instead?" with one button.
- **PE-C-O12 Banner vs step vs more.** The aim line says which kind and the output size (banner 1600 px, others 960 px, plus a 240 px square). The result card shows both sizes.
- **PE-C-O13 After confirming. Open question, with a Proposal.** The stored photo is the enhanced one and the original is not kept ("no originals"), so removing the enhancement later is impossible. The done screen says so plainly in a collapsed "Can I get the original back?" (open when the photo was enhanced). Proposal: that is enough. The phone's own library still has the original (pick it again with Replace); the existing 5-minute Undo removes the photo. Wrong guess costs: someone enhances a photo they cannot reproduce (a screenshot, a forwarded picture) and wants it back; fix would be storing originals, which reverses a locked decision.

## Mockup-to-build differences (for the builder)
- Markup is the existing sheet vocabulary (`.top .body .act .btn .aim .frame .shrink .pundo .mini .pf .pq .pqpill`); new classes are `.pe-*` only (`c.css`). In the app prefix them `.rtab ` like `recipes-edit-photo.css`. `c.js` is the mockup's screen script; its engine calls (`open`, `render`, `ready`, `prefetch`, `notesFor`, `encodeSet`, `close`) are the contract and lift as they are. Picker, sample list, "Mockup" panel and the faked queue timeline are mockup only.
- In the app this screen is a sheet (as the Crop and rotate screen is), not a full phone page; the stage and pill markup does not change.

## What loads, what is deferred (DESIGN.md section 6)
- Default path (opening the recipe or the Add photo entry): nothing for Enhance. No engine, no worker, no CSS for it.
- On the first photo picked: `enhance.js` (13.8 KB) is added by `<script>`; the worker (`enhance-worker.js`, 15.3 KB) is created inside `open()`. The `.pe-*` styles (about 9 KB, 2 KB gzip) and the screen script (about 30 KB mockup, less in the app) load with the Add photo sheet as the app already does for `recipes-edit-photo.js`.
- Deferred until Use photo: encoding. Deferred until the person opens the strength menu: nothing extra is fetched (the other levels are prefetched in the worker as soon as the first is shown, because instant switching and hold-to-compare need them; that is background CPU, not network).
- Cost in the auto-on option specifically: the analysis and one preview render run for EVERY picked photo, even when the person would not have touched Enhance. About 0.3 s analysis + 0.2 to 1 s render on this VM, on a worker thread. This is the price of auto-on and the main thing options A, B and D avoid.

## What I could not verify
- Real phones (iOS Safari 16.4 and older, low-memory Android): only headless Chromium here. Timings above are a floor.
- Real photographs: the samples are illustrations with synthetic flaws (see the engine's notes); the look of auto-on on real food photos, skin tones and meat is unproven.
- The hold gesture with a real finger (iOS long-press callout) is configured as the brief says but only tested with mouse, keyboard and emulated pointer events.
- Screen reader behaviour is by construction, not tested with VoiceOver or TalkBack.
