Plan it: a month behind Another day

Live, clickable mockup for Kitchie issue #826 (the follow-up to #757 option F). Mockup only, nothing is saved or sent. It is the desktop Home page with the Mandarin Mango Yogurt Bowl open on the right. Press Add to plan (or P), then Another day under the seven days. The sample today is Saturday 17 October 2026. Updated 17 October 2026: Monday to Sunday now runs across, and Another day can take two flyouts’ width.

Owner, 17 October 2026 (typed)

“Monday to Sunday as a vertical down, it’s not how humans work. Let’s go back to Monday to Sunday horizontally, left to right. Also when people click another day, this is where I want to change the behavior a little bit. When they go for plan, I want that Monday to Sunday, if there’s enough space, to take two flyouts worth of space. I’ll talk about what if there isn’t space. Actually, you come up with a plan for that. And do a few more visuals as to if you were to take two flyouts worth of space, how will this be a better experience for the user?”

Owner, earlier the same day (voice)

The open day must stand out as the main call to action (“I’m going to make use of that open slot”). All days off should look the same, “just a day off no matter what kind”. Open is now the only solid, filled state; planned and day off recede. The single grey look for a day off is awaiting the owner’s confirmation.

The wide layout needs a window at least 1360px wide. In a narrower window the pages below fall back to the single width on purpose; open them full size, or use the previews on this page (each is the live page, scaled).

1. Two flyouts’ width: three layouts to compare

Pressing Another day widens the Plan flyout to 800px (two flyouts) when the window has room. It takes the recipe pane’s lane and the This week lane; the Home column is never covered (from 1624px it keeps its full width, between 1360 and 1623px it gives up width down to 472px). The recipe pane steps back while the month is open and returns when you go back or close. All three keep the same dates, the same states, the legend, the hover or keyboard detail, the 3-second Planned hold with Undo, and the owner’s rule that open is the call to action: a solid green cell that says Plan here.

A. Roomy calendar agent recommends

Each date is a big cell: the day number, its state icon, and the meal’s name (or Plan here when open, Day off when off). A left column names the week. Hover or keyboard focus opens the read-only detail on the date itself.

Trade-off: the simplest and most like a calendar people already know; whose meal is on a date still needs a hover. Open full size

B. Calendar and a side card

The week keeps its captions above each row and gives 244px to a card that follows the date you point at or focus: the meal being planned, the date, what is planned and whose, what picking it does, and a Plan button (solid green for an open date). No floating popover.

Trade-off: the most detail without a hover, and the recipe stays visible; cells are about 60px so meal names clamp to two short lines, and the card’s button is a second way to plan (the date itself still plans at once). Open full size

C. Open nights first

A solid green button above the week plans the first open night (Plan the next open night Fri 23 Oct, with how many are open), and each week row ends with its open count (“2 open”, “none open”).

Trade-off: pushes hardest toward open nights, which is the stated job; the extra count column costs a little cell width, and the button helps someone who wants an open night, not someone replacing a planned one. Open full size

Recommendation: A. It is what was asked (names in the cells, nothing to learn). C’s shortcut button is a cheap add-on to A later if people want it; B is the pick only if seeing whose meal is on a date without a hover matters more than bigger cells.

How wide is better than the single width

Single flyout width (today’s fallback)

Number and icon only: to know what is on a date you hover each one.

Two flyouts wide (A)

The whole month reads at a glance, with no hovering.

  • You see the plan without hovering. The meal is in the cell, so one look says which nights are taken, by what, and which are free.
  • Open nights are a real button. About 85 by 70px, solid, labelled Plan here, against a 44px square with a plus.
  • A week is a row, left to right, with its name and dates at the left, the way people read a week.
  • The hover is for detail only (whose meal, what is held), not for finding out what is there.
  • Cost: the recipe pane steps back while the month is open (the recipe name and, in B, a recipe card stay in view); it needs a window at least 1360px wide.
2. When there is not enough space: the plan

Rule for every width: Monday to Sunday always runs across, never a cell under 44px, never a sideways scroll. The wide layout is an upgrade; everything below it still works with the same states and rules. The buckets are DESIGN.md section 7’s; each form factor has its own file.

Recommendation: this ladder. The compact fallback is the same markup as the wide layout with the names hidden, so there is one grid, not two. Considered and dropped:

  • Grow the flyout over the Home column on a narrow desktop (a modal over what you were looking at, and the column jumps). Dropped for the compact in-lane grid.
  • A one-week-at-a-time pager on the phone. Hides the open nights in later weeks, five taps to see a month. Dropped for all five weeks in one screen.
  • Vertical weekdays on narrow screens. The owner rejected it.
  • A horizontally scrolling grid. Hides dates, and a scroll inside a scroll. Dropped.
States (a colour plus a shape or word each, so colour is never the only cue)
  • Open (the call to action): solid green, bold plus, and Plan here where there is room. The only filled state; it lifts on hover.
  • Planned: quiet amber tint, plate icon, the meal name (yours, someone else’s and the household’s are one state; hover says whose). 2+ plans: stack icon and the count.
  • Day off: one muted grey state with a moon and the words Day off, whatever the kind (a usual day off or off this week only), still pickable, not locked single look awaiting the owner’s confirmation. The kind is said in the hover detail and the screen-reader label. A day off that is being cooked this week looks planned with a small moon.
  • Past: hatched, struck number, not pickable. Today: ring and underlined number. Hard avoid: a triangle where it asks first; a stop sign (“Not for Jo”) on every date when the meal is not for that person.
  • Hover or keyboard focus shows what is planned, read only; a line under the grid says what picking the date does. Keys: ← → a day, ↑ ↓ a week, Home today, End the last day, Enter plans, Esc back. The month step: a heavy rule where the month changes and “1 Nov” on its first cell.
Rules kept from #757 (owner, 16 October 2026)
  • A pick plans at once; a 3-second Planned or Replaced hold with an inline Undo, then the flyout slides out. Two steps only for a stacked night (and, on touch, for a planned night or a day off, because there is no hover). A usual day off is not locked. Replace applies to the person’s own meal. The Hard avoid guard is asked live per date. This week and up to four weeks ahead.

Loads (DESIGN.md sections 6, 6a and 7)

The default path is the flyout as F. On the first press of Another day the flyout fetches month.js and month.css; a window at least 1360px wide also fetches rich.css, wide.css and wide.js; a narrower window never fetches them. The tablet and phone pages load their own small files and never the wide ones. The month fills the flyout’s slot in place, and widens or folds back in place if the window crosses 1360px (no reload). No images, no fonts. The previews on this page are lazy iframes: they load only when scrolled to.
