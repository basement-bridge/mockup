It is the desktop Home page with the Mandarin Mango Yogurt Bowl open on the right. Press Add to plan (or P), then Another day under the seven days. The sample today is Saturday 17 October 2026. The wide layout needs a window at least 1360px wide. In a narrower window the pages below fall back to the single width on purpose; open them full size, or use the previews on this page (each is the live page, scaled).

1. Two flyouts’ width: three layouts to compare

Pressing Another day widens the Plan flyout to 800px (two flyouts) when the window has room. It takes the recipe pane’s lane and the This week lane; the Home column is never covered (from 1624px it keeps its full width, between 1360 and 1623px it gives up width down to 472px). The recipe pane steps back while the month is open and returns when you go back or close. All three keep the same dates, the same states, the legend, the hover or keyboard detail, the 3-second Planned hold with Undo, and the owner’s rule that open is the call to action: a solid green cell with a plus and no words.

A. Roomy calendar not chosen, historical

Each date is a big cell: the day number, its state icon, and the meal’s name (a plus and no words when open, Day off when off). No week labels (the dates speak for themselves). Hover or keyboard focus opens the read-only detail on the date itself.

Trade-off: the simplest and most like a calendar people already know; whose meal is on a date still needs a hover. Open full size

B. Calendar and a side card owner chose B (voice, 17 Oct 2026)

The grid gives 244px to a card that follows the date you point at or focus: the meal being planned, the date, what is planned and whose, what picking it does, and a Plan button (solid green for an open date). No floating popover.

Trade-off: the most detail without a hover, and the recipe stays visible; cells are about 60px so meal names clamp to two short lines, and the card’s button is a second way to plan (the date itself still plans at once). Open full size

C. Open nights first

A solid green button above the week plans the first open night (Plan the next open night Fri 23 Oct, with how many are open), and each week row ends with its open count (“2 open”, “none open”).

Trade-off: pushes hardest toward open nights, which is the stated job; the extra count column costs a little cell width, and the button helps someone who wants an open night, not someone replacing a planned one. Open full size

Recommendation: A. It is what was asked (names in the cells, nothing to learn). C’s shortcut button is a cheap add-on to A later if people want it; B is the pick only if seeing whose meal is on a date without a hover matters more than bigger cells.

How wide is better than the single width

Single flyout width (today’s fallback)

Number and icon only: to know what is on a date you hover each one.

Two flyouts wide (A)

The whole month reads at a glance, with no hovering.

  • You see the plan without hovering. The meal is in the cell, so one look says which nights are taken, by what, and which are free.
  • Open nights are a real button. About 85 by 70px, solid, a plus and no words, against a 44px square with a plus.
  • A week is a row, left to right, the way people read a week. There are no week labels: the dates are enough.
  • The hover is for detail only (whose meal, what is held), not for finding out what is there.
  • Cost: the recipe pane steps back while the month is open (the recipe name and, in B, a recipe card stay in view); it needs a window at least 1360px wide.

Recommendation: this ladder. The compact fallback is the same markup as the wide layout with the names hidden, so there is one grid, not two. Considered and dropped:

  • Grow the flyout over the Home column on a narrow desktop (a modal over what you were looking at, and the column jumps). Dropped for the compact in-lane grid.
  • A one-week-at-a-time pager on the phone. Hides the open nights in later weeks, five taps to see a month. Dropped for all five weeks in one screen.

  • A horizontally scrolling grid. Hides dates, and a scroll inside a scroll. Dropped.
States, in rank order (owner-stated, voice, 17 Oct 2026, and the shapes are agent defaults so colour is never the only cue)
  • 1. Open, the primary action (“this is where you can drop it”). Boldest: a 2px green outline, a green tint, a heavy plus, no words and no caption at all. It has the transient halo below, so it does not need the heaviest fill.
  • 2. Day off, second (overriding it is allowed). Medium: a 1.5px grey outline, a grey fill, a moon. Day off is per person: it shows only for the person the Who pill selects, and under Everyone it shows none. Still clickable, not locked.
  • 3. Planned, the quietest. A 1px neutral hairline, a barely-there tint, and not bold. One dark-grey mark for a night with one plan, three dark-grey marks for a stacked night (the household plan plus someone’s own). Same grey, quantity only; the count of marks is the cue. Red is never used for planned. Under Everyone the cell shows the household plan, with three marks when anyone has their own plan too; under a person it shows that person’s plans.
  • Outside the scale: Past is hatched and not clickable. Hard avoid is a plain exclamation mark (not a triangle), always red in light and dark and a step larger than the other marks: a small one in the corner where it asks first, and the stop (“Not for Jo”) on every date when the meal is not for that person. It shows only when the recipe being planned conflicts with a Hard avoid of the people the Who pill selects; a recipe with no conflict shows no mark at all.
  • No today state. Today is just the first cell of the grid and reads like any other date; no badge, label or ring (a faint cue was considered and not added: the grid always starts at today’s week and past days are hatched, so the first live date is plain to see).
  • No week labels (owner-stated): the “this week, next week” column is gone. The month shows where it changes by the heavy step-rule and by spelling the month on the cell: “1 Nov” and the grid’s first cell “12 Oct” (agent default; the earlier month band is dropped).
  • Hover or keyboard focus shows what is planned, read only (and the Hard avoid line, with the same exclamation mark); a line under the grid says what picking the date does. Keys: ← → a day, ↑ ↓ a week, Home the first day, End the last day, Enter plans, Esc back.
  • Legend (bottom of the flyout): Open, Planned (one grey mark), Planned several (three grey marks), Day off (moon), Hard avoid (exclamation), Past (blank).

Recommendation: A. The state is what the person is choosing between, so the state mark gets the centre and the size, and the date is where a calendar reader expects it (a corner) and small, so the number never competes. B is the quietest grid but loses the green fill that says “drop it here” at a glance; open then rests on the plus and the halo alone. C keeps the old reading order but the number still sits above the mark, so the grid stays busier than A.

Loads (DESIGN.md sections 6, 6a and 7)

The default path is the flyout as F. On the first press of Another day the flyout fetches month.js and month.css; a window at least 1360px wide also fetches rich.css, wide.css and wide.js; a narrower window never fetches them. The tablet and phone pages load their own small files and never the wide ones. The month fills the flyout’s slot in place, and widens or folds back in place if the window crosses 1360px (no reload). No images, no fonts. The previews on this page are lazy iframes: they load only when scrolled to.
