Share a recipe: create the link

Fragment, slice “sheet” of the recipe share feature. The job: send a recipe you are happy with to someone outside the household, with a link that expires and needs no sign-in. This slice is only the create flow, opened from the recipe's Share button. Where the button sits, the manage and cancel panel, and the recipient's page are other slices. Mockup only: nothing is created and every link is a placeholder (kitchie.example).

The owner's words voice, 13 October 2026
  • A recipe he is happy with can be shared with people outside the household through an expiring signed link, in spirit like the shopping-list link.
  • Recipients need no sign-in to view, and it works the same if they are signed in. Links are multi-use, not single-use, and they expire. The intent is growth.
  • Links can be renamed: give the name at creation and rename it later, like the shopping link.
Recommendation: A. One short sheet, three choices, one button. Name (the recipe's title, already filled in), how long it works (24 hours, 7 days, 30 days; 7 days is on), and one switch for whether they get today's copy or your later edits (today's copy is on). Then the link, Copy and Share. B is quicker still but makes a link every time the sheet opens; C is the most exact but is a form where a share should be one tap away. Reasoning, proposed decisions and open questions are in notes.md in this folder.

Each option below is the real screen in a frame, so you can tap through it: the sheet, the expiry choices, the switch, Create, Copy, Share, Done and Esc all work. Phone is under 1024px (bottom sheet); desktop is 1024px and up (the Pantry's flyout). The Share button on the page behind is a stand-in so the sheet can be opened again; its real place is another slice.

AOne screen, three choices Recommended

Name, how long, today's copy or later edits, then Create. The created sheet is the link, Copy, Share and Done.

  • Every control earns its place: the name is the rename the owner asked for, the three chips are the expiry, the switch is the pinned-or-follows question. No custom date, no version picker.
  • Version is the one on screen (With peas, the household's usual), shown as text, not a control.
  • Costs one decision screen before the link exists.

BThe link already exists

Opening Share makes the link at once with the defaults. Name and expiry become two rows you can change after.

  • Fastest for the common case: Share, Copy, Done.
  • Costs a link every time the sheet opens, even if it is never copied, so the manage list collects strays and any cap on links fills up. A real build would need to discard an unused link.
  • Changing the expiry of a link that already exists is a new rule to decide.

CPick exactly what they get

A preview of what the recipient sees, a version list, a pinned-or-follows choice, expiry with a custom date, then the name.

  • Most control, and the clearest answer to “what will they see”.
  • Taller than the phone sheet: it scrolls, with Create held at the bottom. Five groups to read before sending a recipe to a cousin.
  • The custom date is here to show its cost, not because it earns the space.

States, all three options

Open any option from its links: Default, Created, Copied (the button reads Copied and a line says so; it resets after a few seconds when live), Error (Create failed, nothing was made, Try again), After Done (the sheet closed, with a short confirmation). B's default is the moment after opening, while the link is being made. The last link on each option makes the first Create fail, so Try again can be seen.

Performance

The recipe page loads nothing for sharing: in the build the sheet's code loads when Share is tapped. In this mockup phone and desktop each load only their own pair (phone.css and phone.js, or desktop.css and desktop.js). The link is made in place and the result lands in the same sheet, with no navigation (DESIGN.md section 6a).
