Shared recipe: what the recipient sees

Fragment, mockup only, nothing is built. Owner (Arjan), voice, 13 October 2026. This is the recipient side only; the Share button, the create sheet and the manage or cancel panel are drawn by other slices. Full notes: notes.md. The page itself, with every state: page.html (parameters at the top of the file).

The owner's words
  • A recipe he is happy with can be shared with people outside the household through an expiring signed link. No sign-in to view; the same if they are signed in. Multi-use, expires after a set time.
  • "The intent of this is growth hacking": a good read on its own that invites people into Kitchie without getting in the way of the recipe.
  • Nothing hinted or upsold inside the signed-in app for things the person is not entitled to. This page is outside the app, so a gentle invitation is fine.
What the page never shows
  • Stock or what is missing, reactions, cook counts, member names, who cooked what, versions history. Notes appear without names. A signed-in viewer sees the same page.
  • Default path: one HTML page, one small stylesheet and script, text only. Step photos load next, the banner last (the decided photo order). The desktop stylesheet is fetched from 1024px up only; no framework, no fonts, no tracking script in the mockup.

1. The growth invitation: three options

Same recipe, same top strip and footer. Only the invitation differs. Phone (390) and desktop (1280, scaled down).

AQuiet top strip, then one card at the endRecommended

The top strip is identity only (logo, Sign in) and scrolls away. The one invitation is a card after the last note, when the person has finished reading. Nothing covers or moves the recipe.

Weight and trade-off. Lowest interruption, reads as part of the page, works printed or shared on. Trade-off: people who never scroll to the end never see it, so it converts the least of the three.

BSlim bottom bar, after reading startsAlternative

A one-line bar fixed to the bottom, with Try Kitchie and a close button. It appears only once the person has scrolled past about a third of the page, and a dismiss lasts the visit. On desktop it becomes a small card, bottom right.

Weight and trade-off. Always in reach once someone is reading, so it is seen more than A. Trade-off: it permanently takes about 56px of a phone screen while someone may be cooking from it, and fixed bars are the thing people close without reading.

CBanner above the recipeAlternative

A soft banner above the photo: one line and Take a look, dismissible. Seen first by everyone.

Weight and trade-off. Highest visibility. Trade-off: it is the first thing on the page, ahead of the recipe, and pushes the dish photo and title down by about 150px on a phone. That is the one placement that breaks the readable-first rule most, shown so the owner can see the weight.

3. A link that carries an invite

The owner's words (voice and typed, 13 October 2026)
  • A share link can optionally carry an invite. It is single-purpose: if the link is passed around, only the first person to activate it claims the invite. It sets up a new household and never joins an existing one. Sending invites respects the member's maximum-invites limit.

Placement follows the recommended option A (the card after the last note). Claiming is a separate file fetched only on tap, and replaces the recipe in place. Proposed wording is in the notes.
