Recipe share links: manage

Fragment, mockup only: nothing is sent or saved, every link is a placeholder at kitchie.example. One slice of sharing a recipe: seeing, renaming and cancelling the links a recipe has out. Where the Share button sits, the create sheet and the recipient's page are other slices.

Owner, 13 October 2026: option B, the panel from the Share button, is the main view; A and C stay drawn, marked not chosen. Earlier, by voice: "Somewhere in the recipe there should be information about the pending URLs that people can go and cancel." Links can be renamed, like the shopping link. No sign-in for the recipient, multi-use, expiring; the intent is growth, so show that a link is being used. Full words, rationale and open questions in notes.md.

A · A Shared section in the scroll Not chosen

Where: a section after Photos in the continuous scroll, with a "Shared 3" chip in the jump row. Both appear only when the recipe has a link to show; with none, the page is exactly as it is today.

Why: it is literally "somewhere in the recipe", it costs the cooking job nothing (it is at the end, and absent for most recipes), and the list is a plain page section that needs no new container on phone or desktop. The chip is the way in when links exist.

Trade-off: you scroll or tap the chip to reach it, and nothing on the page top says links are out (the chip count is the only cue).

B · A panel from the Share button Chosen

Chosen by the owner, 13 October 2026. On the desktop recipe summary flyout the panel is the same lane. Where: the Share button carries a count badge; tapping it opens a bottom sheet on phone and a side panel on desktop (the Pantry item panel's lane), with "New link" and the list. The Share button would then be the one door for making and managing.

Why: zero cost on the page, one place for create and manage, and the only option with a real empty state ("Not shared with anyone yet").

Trade-off: one tap deeper, and the information is not in the recipe until you open it; on desktop the panel takes the width the column was using.

C · A card in the recipe's details Not chosen

Where: a card between the title block and the ingredients: "Shared outside the household, 3 links out", opening in place into the list. Absent when nothing is shared.

Why: always seen, so the sender notices a link is about to end without looking for it.

Trade-off: it pushes Ingredients down in the cooking job even when closed, for a feature used once in a while. This is the one that fights DESIGN.md section 1.

What is drawn

Every control is live in the frame. Tap a name (pencil) to rename: Enter or Save keeps it, Escape or Keep leaves it. Copy link copies the placeholder. Cancel link is one tap, no confirm; the row becomes an Undo line for 5 minutes (the repo's photo removal pattern, P-D17), then the link stays cancelled and leaves the list. Ended links are greyed: Renew gives the same link another 7 days; Remove from view hides it, with the same Undo. Nothing is ever hard deleted.

Invites on a link (owner, 13 October 2026)

A link can carry an invite to start a new household (opted in on the create sheet). Each row shows its state: Invite waiting (with a quiet Withdraw), Invite claimed with the date and nothing about who, Invite ended with the link, Invite withdrawn. No allowance is drawn here. An invite on a link counts in the usual place, My invites (at most three pending at once, INH-D12); while a link carries an invite the panel shows one small line pointing there. Cancel link on a link with an unclaimed invite withdraws it; a claimed invite stays claimed (the Undo line says which). A version tag shows only when the link points at something other than the usual. States to try: Invites, Cancel invite, Invite ended. Details in notes.md.

Default path and deferred

Default path loads the theme, shared.css, manage.css and manage.js, no images, no network. Only the form factor on screen is fetched: phone.js or desktop.js is requested on first show, the other never (DESIGN.md sections 6 and 7). A cancel, rename or renew changes the row in place; the page never reloads (section 6a).
