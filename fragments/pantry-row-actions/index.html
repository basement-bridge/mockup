Pantry row actions on desktop

Desktop. Decided 17 Oct 2026: A + B + C together. Kitchie #866. This page is the mockup of the decision; the build follows it.

Decided: A + B + C togetherEarlier options (superseded, context)
The owner’s decision (voice, 17 Oct 2026, relayed, lightly cleaned). “I really like this. If somebody presses Command or Control and clicks, I want that multi-action idea (option C), in addition to the other option I want. When I do multi-select, you cannot do Use one; the only options are going to be Add to shopping list or Used up. The quantities in the shopping list are not a concern here. In addition: if the person does not hover over the right side where the buttons show when they hover, I want them to be able to right click and get the context menu (option B), but I do not want the three dots. If they are hovering over the right side of the row where the buttons show, I am happy for them to drive it from there (option A). So I am going for all three options, with specific drive modes.”

One Pantry row, three ways in, and the pointer decides which: over the buttons at the right it is A, anywhere else on the row a right click is B, and Ctrl or Cmd click is C. The phone and its swipe are unchanged. Drawn on the settled desktop Pantry (rail, column, side panel) at 1024, 1280 and 1440 wide, light and dark.

A: buttons on the right of the row

Unchanged from the recommendation the owner liked: three small buttons with key caps over a soft fade, shown on hover, focus and the current row; the use-by words step aside; a button opens its label under the pointer; a long name ends before the buttons. The open item’s panel gets Use one beside Add to shopping list and Used up, each with its key cap. A mouse drag no longer swipes on desktop.

B: right click opens the menu, no three dots

Right click on a row anywhere except over the buttons. The menu offers Use one (only on a counted row), Add to shopping list, Used up (not on an Out row), then Open, each with its key cap. Nothing is greyed: an entry that cannot apply is not there. The three-dot trigger is gone from every view. Right click over the buttons opens nothing of ours, so the browser’s own menu is the person’s fallback.

C: Ctrl or Cmd click to select several

The selection bar takes the header row’s place (the same 48px, so nothing in the column moves) and shows the count with Add to shopping list and Used up only, plus Clear. The hint line says why Use one is missing. One counted row picked may still Use one; two or more may not. Used up for the batch is one action with one Undo and one sentence that names how many were done.

Empty and edge states (all as in the earlier views, on the decided one)

Touch laptop and tablet landscape. No hover, so a tap makes the row current and its buttons show at 44px (the condition is the hover capability, not width); swipe still works for a finger. A finger has no Ctrl or Cmd, so there is no multi-select by touch alone; the rest of the three modes works one row at a time. The row’s right click is the browser’s own event, so a long press opens the menu only where the browser sends it, which this design does not rely on.

Build impact
No migration. No schema, no stored data, no new setting. Three slices in this order; the first two are routine, the third is guarded.
  • 1. A, the panel’s Use one and no mouse drag: routine. desktop-pantry.js (the Add to list button and key caps on each row, desktop only, fetched only from 64rem), desktop-css/pantry.ts (the strip; the fade is a pseudo-element that takes no clicks), the desktop item panel (one button), swipe.js (pointer type, one line, input not viewport, the only shared-file edit), the swipe test and a desktop browser journey. No deny-list path, no new route: act() and shop() have several callers and are not edited.
  • 2. The right-click menu, no three dots: routine. A contextmenu handler and one menu element in desktop-pantry.js, made on first use; it does nothing over the strip. Cost is not a build risk: Ctrl or Cmd click and right click on a row link stop giving the browser’s link menu and new-tab click (DA-P17).
  • 3. Ctrl or Cmd click multi-select: guarded. The bar and the picks are client-side, but a batch Used up needs a batch route (a new write path in web-edit.ts and the store, on the deny list): looping the single call trips the third-in-five-minutes guardrail and the per-member write limit, and one Undo for the batch needs the same path. A batch Add to list can loop today’s add call but shares the write limit and the one-Undo need. It needs a risk package, and the route is where DA-Q4 is decided (one guardrail stamp per batch is the recommended position).
  • The single-row in-place guardrail confirm (DA-P10): guarded, separate slice, unchanged. The reduce route accepts exactly one field today.
What this loads (DESIGN.md section 6)

The default path is desktop.html, rows.css, rows.js, act.js and sample.js on top of theme.css, then only the decided view’s two files, opt-all.css and opt-all.js; the earlier options’ files are never requested unless that view is opened (checked). Nothing is deferred in the mockup beyond that. In the app nothing new is fetched: the strip, the menu and the selection bar are drawn by desktop-pantry.js, which a phone never downloads (DA-D3); the menu element and the bar are made on first use, and actions change rows in place with the Undo bar, not by reloading (section 6a). Checked at 1024, 1280 and 1440 wide, light and dark, with a mouse and a touch laptop, by tools/pantry-row-actions-check.mjs.

Stress test: what the owner could push back on, and the answer
  • “Hidden until hover means nobody finds it.” A shows on hover, on focus and on the current row, with key caps, so moving the mouse over a list is enough, and a tap or J / K keeps them up. D has no hidden part; its keys line is always there. If the owner still doubts A, the cheap fix is the label on the first row for the first visits (client-side, like the phone’s nudge).
  • “Screen space is precious.” A, B and C add nothing at rest and change no row, header or list height. D adds one 36px line, which the cues switch hides.
  • “Used up is one click next to the cart.” It is the far-right button, as the tick is today, 6px from the cart, red only on hover, with the 5 second Undo bar, Z, and the server’s third-in-five-minutes guardrail, which A to D now ask about in place instead of pointing at the item.
  • “Two code paths, one for the phone and one for the desktop.” No. Every option calls the swipe’s own act() and shop(), as the keys and the item sheet already do. Only the control that triggers it differs, and it lives in desktop-pantry.js, which a phone never downloads (DA-D3). The one shared-file edit is that a mouse pointer stops starting a swipe.
  • “Right click is the browser’s.” The strongest case against B. The row is a real link, so B hides “Open in new tab” and “Copy link” (Shift right click gives them back).
  • “Who selects five pantry items?” Unknown; there is no evidence yet of a bulk job. C is the only option that is not a thin trigger on an existing call: one used-up is fine, three or more would trip the removal guardrail and the per-member write limit if looped.
  • “An iPad in landscape, a touch laptop.” They get the desktop layout and have no hover. Each option shows its controls on the tapped row at 44px, and swipe still works for a finger. Covered by the touch checks.
  • “Tab through forty rows.” A adds a third stop to each row (today two). The keys are the fast path; the buttons stay real buttons for screen readers. Left as it is (DA-Q11).
  • “A used-up item vanishes.” It goes to Out, stays findable by search and the Out chip, and Undo brings it straight back.
