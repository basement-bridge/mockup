Receipt scan: bulk add to the Pantry

Today the Pantry adds one item at a time. Scan a receipt, read on the device, and add many at once, matching each line to what you already have. Mockup only: nothing is built, nothing is saved, no real receipt is read (the sample is invented). 12 October 2026. Four directions, phone and desktop. Every decision below is a proposal, none confirmed.

Open any link, then move the pointer to the top edge or press the backtick key to pick a window size and theme, or add &vp=390, 768, 1024, 1280, 1440, 1920.

The owner's ask (voice, 12 Oct 2026, tidied)
  • Match each scanned line to an existing Pantry item exactly first.
  • Exact: a clear cue, and it must say add as new or just revise the quantity.
  • Close, not exact: a confidence shown visually (he floated one, three, five dots; compact). The person confirms or overrides; an override becomes a new item.
  • Not recognised: flagged distinctly; the person must confirm it as new, "like a visual scan where they cross it out".
  • Client-side OCR, no server round trip. Same look and feel as Kitchie.
Four directions

A. Receipt order (recommended default)

The receipt as one list, in print order. A glyph on the left says what will happen, dots on the right say how sure.

  • Entry: a two-way switch at the top of the Add flyout, One item | From a receipt.
  • Review: one row per printed line; filter chips (All, Check, New?, Ready, Not added). Open rows carry their one-tap buttons.
  • Confidence: five dots, 5 / 3 / 1 filled.
  • Phone: a row opens inline. Desktop: the row opens in the right lane.
  • Good: closest to the Pantry list, least new UI, the person sees every line. Poor: the lines that need you are mixed in with the ones that do not (the chips help).

B. Sort the pile

The review sorts itself by what needs you: Check these, Not recognised, then Ready. Groups shrink as you decide.

  • Entry: a dashed "Scan a receipt" tile at the top of the Add flyout.
  • Review: three bold groups with counts and a progress bar; Ready and Not added are folded; a decided line sinks into Ready.
  • Confidence: three rising bars.
  • Phone: a card's detail is a sheet. Desktop: the right lane.
  • Good: attention goes to the doubtful lines first; best when many lines are unsure. Poor: loses receipt order, so matching a line to the paper is harder.

C. The paper page

The receipt drawn as paper. A circle in the margin per line: tick it to say yes, cross it out to say not an item. The pencilled note is the suggestion.

  • Entry: the Add button gets a caret; its menu has Add one item and Add from a receipt (no extra control on the Pantry header).
  • Review: print on paper, dotted leaders, tick circles. A tick you make is scribbled across the line with the Shopping list's own five scribbles; a cross strikes it through.
  • Confidence: a ring beside the pencilled note.
  • Phone: tap the note for a sheet. Desktop: the right lane.
  • Good: the "go through the page and cross it off" feeling the owner described, ties in with Shopping. Poor: the most to build; tick and cross are small targets on a dense page.

D. One at a time

Exact matches fold into one card; each doubtful line is a big card. Swipe or press a key.

  • Entry: a camera button inside the item box (and, on desktop, drop or paste a photo anywhere).
  • Review: one card per doubtful line, a dot per line above it, decided lines in a folded list with Change. Phone: swipe right yes, left new item. Desktop: Y, N, X, with the queue in the right lane.
  • Confidence: five large dots with the word beside them.
  • Good: fastest when the read is good; very little reading. Poor: hides the exact matches behind one card (a trust gap early on) and a long deck is slow.

Recommendation (a proposal): A as the default, because it is the Pantry's own row with one new idea and the shortest build. Take B's "Check these first" ordering as a later view for long, doubtful receipts. D is the fastest path when the read is good; C is the most playful and the most to build.

Proposed decisions RS-1 to RS-28 (none confirmed)
  1. RS-1Entry lives inside Add. No new control on the Pantry header (screen space on the list is precious). A: switch in the Add flyout. B: tile. C: caret menu on the Add button. D: camera in the item box, plus drop or paste on desktop.
  2. RS-2Read on the device, photo thrown away. Camera or photo picker on phone (the phone's own camera, no live preview); drop, paste or choose on desktop. A long receipt takes up to three photos ("Add the other half"). Nothing is uploaded or stored (owner, 11 Oct 2026; edge-ocr OCR-D1 to OCR-D4).
  3. RS-3Exact first. Each line is matched to the Pantry in this order: exact name (or a learned alias), then close, then none. The first that hits decides the state.
  4. RS-4Five line states, one chip each. Top up (exact, revises an existing item), New item (exact, kept as its own item), Check (close, needs you), New? (no match, needs you), Not added. Every state has a glyph, a word and a colour, so colour is never the only signal.
  5. RS-5An exact match says which it will do. Default is Top up. If the existing item has a use-by, the default is New item, because a new carton has its own date and the old one keeps its. One tap flips it (the Top up | New item switch, key T). The line always says the result: "5 kg to 10 kg" or "keeps eggs as it is".
  6. RS-6Exact match to a used-up item is Back in stock. Tuna at 0 becomes 3 tin: the same item, its place and minimum kept.
  7. RS-7Exact lines are ready without a tap. Nothing is written until the commit step, so the person is not asked to confirm what is certain. They can still open and change any line.
  8. RS-8A close match is never pre-confirmed. The person says Yes (the suggestion), or Not this one, which makes a new item (owner). Proposal: a "Pick another item" path is also offered, since the second and third candidates cost nothing.
  9. RS-9A confirmed close match follows decision 5. It becomes Top up or New item by the same use-by rule.
  10. RS-10No match is flagged distinctly and never added by default. Dashed outline, a question mark, the danger tone, the chip "New?". The person must add it as new (a tick, or "Add as new") or cross it out ("Not an item"). Lines left unchecked are not added, and the summary says how many.
  11. RS-11A new item's name starts as the cleaned receipt text. Editable on the spot ("Smoked salmon"). When the text is unclear it starts empty with the prompt "What is it? The text was unclear".
  12. RS-12One confidence per line, shown as a band. The recogniser's score times the match score, drawn as three bands (85 and up, 60 to 84, under 60), never a percentage. Default: five dots with 5, 3 or 1 filled (the owner's idea). Rising bars and a ring are drawn as alternatives (B, C) and any option takes any style with ?conf=pips|bars|ring. The word (Very likely, Likely, Weak guess) shows when a line is open.
  13. RS-13Confidence is never drawn like the battery. The Pantry battery means stock level. Dots, bars of rising height and a ring cannot be read as it. Same band colours as the app (sage, amber, red-tone).
  14. RS-14Discount and non-food lines are never added. They sit under Not added with the reason (Discount: takes $3.00 off the line above; Not food: a bag charge). "This is an item" rescues one. A discount is attached to the line above it, not treated as an item.
  15. RS-15Header and totals are ignored but counted. "6 total and header lines ignored" is on every review, and the lines are listed, so a wrongly dropped item can be found.
  16. RS-16Duplicate lines are combined by default. Two MILK 1L lines become one row "2 lines", 2 L, with "Keep separate" to undo.
  17. RS-17Quantity is parsed and shown. "4 @ 1.10" is 4 units; "400G" next to it is the tin size and is dropped; a weight line under an item belongs to it; kg and L convert to the Pantry's g and mL. The line says how it was read, and the amount is a stepper the person can change.
  18. RS-18Price is shown to help check, not saved. Open question: does Kitchie want to store price at all.
  19. RS-19Bad reads have their own states. Low quality: a banner on the review and a retake link, unclear lines flagged. Dark photo: a warning before reading. Nothing found: an empty state with try again and add by hand. Camera blocked: choose a photo instead.
  20. RS-20First use on a metered connection asks before downloading about 16 MB. Offline with no reader saved: says so and offers add by hand. (edge-ocr section 3.2; Choice 3 there.)
  21. RS-21Commit is a summary step. New items, topped up, left out; the lists; a reminder of any unchecked lines with a way back; one button "Add N to the Pantry". Then the Pantry, with the changes marked.
  22. RS-22Undo. A toast with Undo for 9 seconds after commit; Undo puts quantities back and removes the new items, with Redo. In the review, every decision has Change. Proposal: also reachable from the Pantry history for 5 minutes (the photos rule). Open question: the window.
  23. RS-23Where new items go. A new batch of an existing item takes that item's place. A brand new item lands under "Where do these go?" with no place, to be set later. Open question: does the app have this state.
  24. RS-24Phone and tablet layout. The review is a full page with a sticky bar ("10 ready, 4 to check, Review and add 10"); sheets for the Add flyout, capture, summary and (B, C) a line's detail. Tablet is the same, centred and wider.
  25. RS-25Desktop layout. Rail, the 736px column, the 400px right lane. The lane is the Add flyout, then the line inspector, then the summary; with nothing picked it is the summary, never empty (plan-desktop 52).
  26. RS-26Desktop keys. J or Down, K or Up move; Y yes or add as new; N new item; X not an item; T top up or new item; 1 to 3 pick a candidate; Z undo a line; Shift+Enter add to the Pantry; R scan from the Add flyout. Close is Ctrl or Cmd Enter and the x; Esc only cancels a menu (Pantry rule).
  27. RS-27Viewport code in its own files. rs.phone.js and rs.desktop.js, phone.css, tablet.css, desktop.css. Shared files carry no size rules.
  28. RS-28Provenance and writes. Source is the existing closed value receipt, by is the person, writes go through the same service methods as chat (standing rule). No new field.
Open questions
  • Which option? Nothing is built, so a wrong guess costs nothing yet.
  • Does Kitchie hold separate batches of an item? RS-5 assumes a new carton can be its own item. If not, every exact match is Top up and the switch goes.
  • Is price stored? RS-18 shows it and does not save it.
  • Where do no-match new items go? RS-23 leaves them without a place; is there such a state in the Pantry?
  • Undo window after commit (RS-22): 9 seconds on screen, 5 minutes in history.
  • Dots, bars or ring (RS-12), and whether the word shows only when a line is open.
  • Combine duplicate lines by default (RS-16)?
  • Override (RS-8): the owner said it becomes a new item; this also offers "Pick another item". Keep or drop?
