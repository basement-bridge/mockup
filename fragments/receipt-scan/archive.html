One language with Shopping: three boxes become five states

Shopping draws three things. The Pantry has one more case (revise the quantity) and its grey needs an answer, so each Shopping drawing is kept and extended by sign, word and edge, never by a new colour. Colour is never the only cue.

Rice +5 kg

Top up. Green, solid, thick, filled: Shopping's "On your list". Up arrow and the amount. Exact match that revises an existing item.

New eggs

New item. Same green, plus a doubled edge: a new one beside the old one. Exact match kept as its own item (it has a use-by), or a confirmed new item.

Cheddar?

Check. Amber, dashed, light fill: Shopping's "Usual swap" slot. Wave sign, the suggestion, the confidence. Close match; pulses until you answer.

New? Salmon

New? Grey, dotted (2 px, not Shopping's 1 px), question mark. No match. Pulses. Never added unless you say so.

Discount

Not added. Hairline, minus, the reason in one word. Discount, bag, non-food; or crossed off by you. Never matched.

What the person sees besides the box: a small tick in a circle at the corner of any line they answered (read and decided are different things); four counted chips under the picture that repeat the signs and words (Ready with "n top up, n new", Check, New?, Not added), tappable, counting everything read so far; and Add N in the control row's empty right slot. The prompt, the steadiness bar and the shutter ring, the flash and the haptics are Shopping's, unchanged.

Deviations from Shopping, and why. (1) A fourth chip and a split of green: Shopping has no "revise quantity" and no excluded-with-reason list. (2) Dotted is 2 px, not 1 px, and pulses: in Shopping "Other" is optional, here "New?" blocks nothing but is never added unasked, so it must not read as background. (3) Chips are buttons: counts open the Last look. (4) The right control slot is Add N, not empty. (5) The tag sits at the right end of the line, as built in #697, not straddling the top edge as the mockup drew it. (6) A tick-in-circle for answered lines: Shopping has no per-line answer on the picture.

Recommended default (a proposal, RS-43): A. It is Shopping's own boxes made into the controls, so the answer lands where the eye already is and it is the least new vocabulary. The Last look (B's walk) is its safety net, so nothing is left unanswered. If a real phone shows that tapping a paper held in one hand is too fiddly, swap to C. B and D stay as drawn.

Direction A: Tap the paper (recommended default)

The boxes are the controls

Tap a box on the live picture. The frame holds, the line comes up to the top of the picture, its card opens at the bottom. Answer it and the camera carries on.

  • Boxes: the five drawings above. Ready lines need nothing; Check and New? pulse until answered.
  • Confidence: five dots (5, 3, 1) in the Check tag, on an opaque pill so they survive glare and motion. The card repeats them with the word.
  • Confirm or override: the card says Yes, it's Cheddar or Not this one, new item; Pick another item chips stay beside it (open question 8); on an exact line a Top up | New item switch (T) and an amount stepper.
  • No match: flagged live (dotted, pulsing, counted). It can be answered live from its card; the Last look (from the chip or the summary) walks every one left, so none is forgotten.
  • Auto-capture: unchanged. It banks each screenful and the receipt slides on, so a long receipt works. It waits while a card is open.

Commit, undo, leave

  • Add N (right of the shutter) opens the summary: new items, topped up, left out, anything still unanswered with a Last look link, one button.
  • Then the Pantry with the changes marked and a toast: Undo with a 9 second countdown; the Pantry history keeps it for 5 minutes (RS-22, the window is open).
  • Done closes the camera and drops the read. Nothing was written.

Desktop

Rail, the 736 px column with the webcam (or a dropped photo), the 400 px lane. Click a box or press J K: the lane shows its card; with nothing picked the lane is the summary. Keys: Y N X T 1 to 3 Z, Space takes it now, L Last look, Shift+Enter adds. Escape lets go of a held line; it never closes the scan.

Direction B: Freeze and walk

Live to read, still to answer

The picture only reads: boxes and counts, nothing to tap. When it holds steady it is captured and the frame stays on screen as a held photo. The open lines are walked one at a time, the rest dimmed.

  • Boxes: same drawings; on the held photo the current line has a ring and the others fade.
  • Confidence: three rising bars (the second look from RS-12). Walk card repeats it with the word.
  • Confirm or override: Yes / New on the card, More for Pick another item and the amount; Next, Back.
  • No match: crossed out on the paper itself: the scribble is drawn over the printed line, on the photo and on the card. This is the closest reading of "cross each one off". Add as new is the tick.
  • Auto-capture: unchanged; a capture freezes. Scan the rest goes back to live for the other half.

Why it is here

It is the nearest to what Shopping did before, and the easiest with a shaky hand or a receipt held in the other hand: you never tap a moving target. It is also the least "live". The same walk is the Last look in A, C and D.

Desktop

The column keeps the held photo and the shutter; the lane is the walk card. Keys as in A, with J K moving through the queue.

Direction C: Cards under the camera

The camera never stops

Nothing on the picture has to be tapped. Lines that need you drop into a dock of cards between the picture and the shutter, in the thumb's reach, and leave it when answered.

  • Boxes: same drawings, read-only; tapping a box finds its card.
  • Confidence: a ring (thirds) in the tag and on the card.
  • Confirm or override: Yes / New on the card; More opens Pick another and the amount in place.
  • No match: a card with the printed line on it: tick to add as new, swipe across the line or Not an item to cross off. Last look is the dock made full size.
  • Auto-capture: banks and slides on, as A. The dock keeps its cards across screenfuls.

Cost

The dock takes about 200 px of the picture, so the stage is smaller than A's. More on screen at once. It is the best for one-handed use. Desktop: the lane is the queue of cards, ready lines open in the lane when tapped.

Direction D: Camera and list

The paper printing out below the lens

The top half is the picture. The bottom half is a live list: one row per line, in receipt order, appearing as lines are read. The list is where decisions are made.

  • Boxes: same drawings on the picture; tapping a box finds its row.
  • Confidence: five dots and the word on the row.
  • Confirm or override: open rows carry the quick answer (Yes / New, Add as new / Not an item); tap a row for the full card.
  • No match: a dotted row with its two quick buttons; the list is the queue, so there is no separate Last look.
  • Auto-capture: banks and slides on. Nothing needs the picture to hold still.

Desktop is where it shines

Webcam in the column, the list is the whole lane, with the summary under it. On a phone the picture is small (262 px), so the boxes are small. It is revision 1's "Receipt order" with a camera above it.

Confidence over live video: dots, bars or ring (RS-12, RS-13, RS-37)

Same three bands as before (85 and up, 60 to 84, under 60), never a percentage, never drawn like the Pantry's battery. What is new is the video: marks sit on an opaque pill so they survive glare and a busy background, they are small (5 px dots, 4 px bars, an 18 px ring), and a band changes only after two looks agree so it does not flicker. While the frame is blurry the boxes freeze with their last band. In low light the score is lowered 15%, so a borderline line reads lower, never wrongly higher. On the card it is bigger, with its word. Not decided: dots, bars or ring, and how it reads over live video against a static card (open question 6). Each direction defaults to a different look so they can be compared; any direction takes any look with ?conf=pips|bars|ring or the panel.

Edge states (shared by every direction; shown on A)

Shopping's prompts and tones are reused: a confident, outlined warning for blur; a softer, dashed, italic "best guess" for not-a-receipt, which looks different on purpose. A negative prompt needs two looks in a row. New here: too dark (with a light button when the phone has one), glare (a hatched patch over the washed-out lines), cut-off lines (a dashed open box and an arrow toward the edge; not matched until whole), "seen again" (a line read twice is counted once), and the desktop's dropped photo. Counts of lines already read are kept when the camera sees a wall.

Decisions RS-29 to RS-54 (proposed, none confirmed). RS-1 to RS-28 stand unless listed as superseded below.
  1. RS-29The matching logic stands. Exact, close, none; exact says Top up or New item (RS-5); a close match is never pre-confirmed (RS-8); no match is never added unasked (RS-10); discount, bag and non-food lines are left out with a reason and never matched (RS-14, reconfirmed by the owner 14 Oct). The mockup's rule is the same function as revision 1's. Only where and when it is answered moves.
  2. RS-30Live boxes keep Shopping's drawings and extend them by sign, word and edge. Top up: green, up arrow, amount. New item: the same green, layers sign, a doubled edge. Check: amber dashed, wave, suggestion, confidence. New?: grey dotted 2 px, question mark, pulses. Not added: hairline, minus, a one-word reason. A tick in a circle marks a line the person answered.
  3. RS-31Four counted chips, tappable. Ready (n top up, n new), Check, New?, Not added. They count everything read on this scan, not only what is in view. Check, New? and Not added open the Last look on those lines; Ready opens the summary.
  4. RS-32Add N takes the control row's empty right slot (dashed and disabled at 0 ready). The commit is where the thumb already is.
  5. RS-33No match is flagged live and answered actively. "Cross each one off" needs a still page, so: live, a pulsing dotted "?" box and the New? chip; the answer is a tick (Add as new) or a cross-off (Not an item) from the line's card, live in A, C and D, and always available in the Last look, which walks every line left on a held frame. In B the cross-off is drawn on the held photo itself. Unanswered lines are never added; the summary says how many. A wrong guess costs a redraw of where the answer lives.
  6. RS-34Auto-capture and steadiness are Shopping's, unchanged (4 looks, 1.2 s, 75% within 0.6 of a line height, none blurry; the ring, the bar, the flash; haptics 15 and 30 ms). What a capture does changes: it banks the screenful (A, C, D) or freezes the frame (B). The manual shutter does the same. In A it waits while a card is open.
  7. RS-35A long receipt is read in screenfuls, each line counted once. A line is tracked by its words and place; a line seen again after the paper slides is "Seen again, not counted twice". Two real identical lines combine into one box "x2" (RS-16 stands; Keep separate on the card). This answers Shopping's open "long receipts in several parts" for the Pantry.
  8. RS-36Cut-off lines are not read. A line cut by the frame edge gets a dashed open box and "Cut off" with an arrow; it is not matched, counted or tagged until whole. A soft prompt says so.
  9. RS-37Confidence on a live box. On Check boxes only (exact lines are certain, no-match has nothing to rate). The three bands of RS-12 on an opaque pill; a band changes after two looks agree; frozen while blurry; lowered 15% in the dark. The card shows the same mark with its word.
  10. RS-38The card is the line detail, unchanged. The printed text and price (shown to check, RS-18), how the amount was read (RS-17), Yes or Not this one, Pick another item, the Top up | New item switch, the amount stepper, Combined from 2 lines. Used as a sheet, a lane, a dock card, a walk step or an open list row.
  11. RS-39Direction A. Tap a box: the frame holds, the line comes up to the top third of the picture, the card opens (a sheet on a phone, the lane on a desktop). Answering an open line closes it and the camera carries on. Auto-capture waits while a card is open.
  12. RS-40Direction B. Live boxes are read-only. A capture freezes the frame (a held photo, a solid outline). The open lines are walked one at a time, the others dimmed. A no-match line is crossed out on the paper by drawing Shopping's scribble over it (danger tone, the five hand-drawn paths). Scan the rest returns to live.
  13. RS-41Direction C. The camera never stops. Open lines are cards in a dock above the shutter, Check first. An answered card leaves. Tapping a box finds its card; tapping a Ready box opens a sheet. The Last look is the dock made full size.
  14. RS-42Direction D. A live list in receipt order under a shorter picture. Open rows have quick answers; a row opens to the card; tapping a box finds its row. The list is the queue; on desktop it is the lane.
  15. RS-43Recommended default: A (a proposal). Fewest new ideas; the answer is where the eye is. Swap to C if a real phone says tapping is too fiddly.
  16. RS-44Desktop layout and keys. Rail 88, column 736 with the webcam, lane 400 (RS-25 amended: the lane is per direction). Keys as RS-26 plus Space (take it now), L (Last look), Shift+Enter (add). Escape lets go of a held line only; Ctrl or Cmd+Enter or Done closes (the Pantry's rule). Viewport code in lv.desktop.js and lv.desktop.css.
  17. RS-45On a desktop a photo is a first-class path. Webcams are poor at receipts. Drop, paste or choose draws the same boxes on a still (no steadiness ring; a "Photo" read) and a second photo adds the other half (RS-2's rule, kept for photos).
  18. RS-46Edge prompts. Blur (confident outline, boxes freeze at the last good read, no capture); not a receipt (soft, dashed, italic; counts kept); too dark (outline, a light button if the phone has one); glare (outline, a hatched patch); cut off (soft); resting after 150 s (Resume); camera refused (one screen, Take or choose a photo). Shopping's wording and tones are kept; a negative prompt needs two looks.
  19. RS-47Light button. Appears in the top bar only when it is dark and the phone exposes a torch; otherwise the prompt says what to do.
  20. RS-48Commit, summary, undo. Add N opens the RS-21 summary as a sheet (phone) or the lane (desktop), with the unanswered reminder and a Last look link. Add N to the Pantry releases the camera and shows the Pantry with the changes marked and a toast: Undo with a 9 second countdown, 5 minutes in history (RS-22 stands, window open).
  21. RS-49Done leaves without adding. It closes the camera and drops the read. Proposal: ask first if any line was answered; the mockup does not.
  22. RS-50Entry is inside Add (RS-1 stands). The live revision draws the tile ("Scan a receipt"). Choosing it opens the camera at once; there is no capture sheet between. Revision 1's other entries (switch, caret, camera in the item box) are untouched and still open as presentation.
  23. RS-51Haptics. 15 ms the first time a line becomes Ready two looks running (once per line); 30 ms on a capture; 10 ms on an answer. Nothing when a Check or New? line appears (it would buzz all the time).
  24. RS-52Tag position as built. At the right end of the line, centred on it, as in #697; the box edge is drawn outside the text so a tight line is not covered.
  25. RS-53Viewport code in its own files (RS-27 extended): lv.phone.js and lv.phone.css, lv.tablet.css, lv.desktop.js and lv.desktop.css; the shared lv.css, lv-core.js, lv-run.js carry no size rules. The Pantry shell (phone.css, tablet.css, desktop.css) is revision 1's, reused.
  26. RS-54For the build. Reuse the existing worker (the frame message from #695) and the browser's matching.ts; no new engine. Match a tracked line once and again only if its text changes (cost). Class names: Shopping's rl-* for the shared drawings, lv-* for the Pantry's own. The mockup's LV.out equals revision 1's RS.out. --cue here is --added in Kitchie's stylesheet.
