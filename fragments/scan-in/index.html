Scanning things in, four directions

Fragment. The job: getting things from the physical world into the app. These are four ways to look when someone opens it. Each phone shows the first moment, then what the person sees after the first scan or snap.

Sample data only: a milk, penne and spinach shop. What the camera shows is a stand-in; how things get recognised is deliberately not shown or decided here.

Open
  • How we know someone is scanning (versus cooking or shopping)? Open, not modelled here. See the job file.
  • How anything gets recognised is left open on purpose: the job file does not say, and this mockup does not guess.
  • Which direction leads? 1 and 4 combine easily: shelf chosen up top, viewfinder under it.
  • One at a time or a pile? 2 holds everything until you confirm, 1 and 3 add as you go.
  • When something is not recognised, does it ask on the spot (3), flag it at the end (2) or add it blank to fix later?
  • Does a receipt count as a scan here, or is it its own path?
  • Where does it land by default when the person does not pick a place?
Performance note (DESIGN.md section 6)
  • Default path loads: this page, the shared theme and the small inline icon set. Nothing else; the viewfinder shapes here are plain CSS, no images.
  • Deferred until engaged: in the real app, the camera and anything that reads what it sees load only when the person taps the shutter, a mode chip or the mic. Opening the screen does not ask for the camera.
  • Also deferred: the item pictures and place list that appear after a scan arrive with the result, not with the page. Other themes are separate files fetched on choice (theme.js).
  • Why reasonable now: the common start is to look, not to scan, so the heavy parts wait for the first action.
