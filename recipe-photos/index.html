Recipe photos

One slice of the Recipe tab (gap G7). Two halves: the architecture decisions (who owns the bytes, where they sit, sizes, who shrinks, uploads from the app and from an assistant, limits, privacy, caching, removal, backups, loading) and a clickable phone mockup of what a person sees. Everything not marked Locked is a Proposal until the owner says yay, nay or combine.

Options and what the owner chose

Thirteen options decided by the owner (typed, 10 October 2026). Each keeps a one-line "Not chosen" for the options he did not pick, and the downside of what he picked. N1 is still open.

P1Who owns the photos (P-D1)

A Recipe owns them. Bytes and their records live with Recipe's data; Kitchie only shows them by id.

Locked A (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B a shared photo service in Platform (a new service everyone depends on); C Kitchie stores them (Kitchie would own Recipe's data).

Downside of A: Recipe, which today serves only JSON, gains file routes and a growing store. If Kitchie later wants item photos it reuses the port (P-D2), not Recipe's store.

P2Where the bytes sit (P-D2)

A Files in a folder next to Recipe's data, behind a blob-storage port: Recipe's code talks only to the port; the files adapter is the one built now. Online object storage is option C, as a future adapter: named, not built.

Locked A + port (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B inside SQLite as blobs (bloats the database and every backup). C as the store today (metered; it stays a future adapter behind the port).

Downside: a port is one more layer to write and test, and two things to keep in step (the row and the blob). Handled by writing the blob first, then the row, and a nightly sweep for strays (RP1).

P3What is kept per photo (P-D6)

A, varied by the owner: the banner keeps large (1600px) + square (240px). Every other photo (step, cook, gallery) keeps medium (960px) + square. No originals. Colour and a 24px preview in the row.

Locked A, varied (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B A plus the original (3 to 6 MB each); C original only, resized on request (CPU on every view).

Downside: a step or cook photo can never be shown bigger than 960px, and the banner never has a medium (the large size serves the top of the page, heavier than before: about 250 KB against 75).

P4Who shrinks the photo (P-D7)

C Both, the owner's way: the phone shrinks first (the default path). When a photo arrives unshrunk (an assistant upload, a phone that could not), the server derives the sizes in a background, low-priority queue that backs off under load. Banner square first; then every other square; larger sizes later. When the system grows, that work moves to a third-party service through a seam (named, not built). Until a photo's sizes exist the UI says "Photo is being prepared".

Locked C (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: A phone only (an assistant's upload could never be shrunk); B server only (CPU spikes on every upload).

Downside: the server now has an image library (a native dependency, a bigger container) and a queue to run, and a photo can sit "being prepared" for a while on a busy day.

P5How an assistant adds a photo (P-D12)

A one-time upload, refined: the assistant's MCP call obtains it (the person is not involved). Not a blocking step: the assistant carries on and reports the photo as pending. Recipe's acknowledgement carries verification (hash, bytes, dimensions, the sizes it will keep). Up to 3 attempts (a setting), each smaller: 2000, 1400, 1000 px. After the third, stop and tell the person.

Locked A, refined (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B picture data in the tool call (base64: huge in tokens); C a web address (already removed by L4).

Downside: an assistant that cannot send a file over HTTP falls back to giving the person a Kitchie link (drawn as scene 4); the assistant now sends picture bytes, so Recipe must check them strictly.

P6What a photo is attached to (P-D9)

A A version or a cook, never a revision. Managed downside: anyone can remove a version's photos and upload replacements (viewer: Replace, Remove, 5-minute undo).

Locked A (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B the recipe as a whole (shows the wrong dish for a version that changed a lot); C a revision (a typo fix would lose its photos).

Downside: a new version that looks different shows its parent's photos (with the icon) until someone adds its own.

P7When nothing has a banner (P-D10)

B Only recipe photos can be banners. A cooked photo never leads. A version showing its parent's photo carries a small icon (with a title) until it has its own.

Locked B (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: A parent's cover then the newest cooked photo (a messy dinner photo could lead); C no banners, newest photo of any kind.

Downside: a recipe that has only cooked photos (Ragù in the mockup) has no banner and keeps the stock picture in lists.

P8Who can see a photo (P-D15)

A Same-site, household only, checked against the Platform sign-in on every request, plus an MCP read tool, get_photo, so an assistant can see a household photo, signed in by the same SSO as the person on Kitchie. No tokens in addresses.

Locked A + MCP read (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B signed links that expire (leak through copy and paste, defeat the cache); C loaded by script with the token (defeats lazy loading).

Downside: nobody can share a photo outside the household yet. Named future: expiring public share links, not designed now.

P9Limits (P-D8)

The owner's set: one banner per version; one photo per step and at most 10 step photos per version (a step need not have one); 3 per cook; 2 GB per household; plus a simple search-and-remove flow for photos of versions not used. Kept from A where they do not conflict: up to 3 gallery photos per version, 8 MB per upload, 60 uploads an hour per member.

Locked (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: A as written (6 recipe photos per version, 1 MB per upload); B roomy (20, 10, 5 GB); C only a household total.

Downside: 2 GB holds about 8,000 to 23,000 photos depending on the mix; the free-space flow is how a household stays under it. All caps are settings.

P10Removing a photo (P-D17)

B Gone after an undo window of 5 minutes. Hidden at once; Undo for 5 minutes (the row counts down); then the blobs are deleted.

Locked B, 5 minutes (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: A hidden with a 30-day restore (files linger); C never deleted (photos can show people and homes).

Downside: a removal noticed after 5 minutes cannot be undone; only a backup could bring it back.

P11Backups (P-D18)

B Database as today; photos copied as new files only (a blob never changes, so only new ones are copied).

Locked B (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: A the photo folder copied whole with every backup (grows fast); C no photo backups.

Downside: both copies still sit on the same server; an off-server copy is infra's open question. A photo removed after 5 minutes stays in older backups until they roll over.

P12Top of the recipe page (P-D20)

A Full-width banner, with the owner's loading order: text first, then step photos, then the banner last. Other photos only on demand. The banner's box is there from the first paint, so nothing jumps.

Locked A (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B a small square beside the title; C no photo at the top.

Downside: the banner, the biggest picture on the page, is the last to appear; on a slow line the top shows its colour and blurred preview for a moment longer.

P13Photos in the list (P-D21)

The owner's order: a stock picture first so recipe content is prioritised; then the planned recipes' photos if this week's plan exists; then rows on screen plus 5 more in the category; everything else on demand. The default frame is lazy-fetched after the core of the page completes; Home is never affected.

Locked (owner, typed, 10 Oct 2026)

Not chosen: B no photos in lists; C a smaller 40px thumbnail and slimmer rows. A's tile (56px, the banner's square) is kept as the place the picture loads into.

Downside: for a moment every row shows the same stock picture; scrolling fast past row 13 shows stock tiles until they load.

N1Where a note lives (P-D26)

Settled underneath by the owner (typed, 10 Oct 2026; V-D31, versions spec 9.4), refining R-D24: a note is a field of the version, inherited down the lineage: it shows on the version it was written on and every version made from it, and a version stores a notes change only where its notes differ. N1 is only about the end of a cook:

A No question. The note goes on the version being cooked (inherited as above), for the household. Written at the end of a cook it carries that cook as a tag (P-D29).

B Ask at the end of a cook: "This cook only" (shows on that night's row in Cooked) or "For next time" (on the version, as A). From the recipe page it is always A.

Recommended A

Downside of A: a note that only made sense that night ("ran out of soy, used fish sauce") sits among the lasting notes; the cook tag and the cook's own one-line comment (P-D11) soften that. The blob-storage port (P-D2) Locked: port + files adapter

Recipe's photo code never touches a path or an SDK. It talks to one interface. Keys are content hashes, so the same bytes are one blob, a blob never changes, and backup and cache rules fall out of that.

interface BlobStore {
  // key = "sha256:" + 64 hex chars of the bytes; the caller computes it, the store re-checks it on put
  put(key, bytes | stream, {contentType, owner: householdId}) → {key, bytes, created: bool}   // idempotent: an existing key is a no-op
  get(key) → bytes                                    // small blobs (squares)
  read(key, {start?, end?}) → ReadableStream          // streaming read, with ranges; what the photo route pipes out
  exists(key) → bool
  stat(key) → {bytes, contentType, owner, createdAt} | null
  delete(key) → void                                  // only after the row says nothing points at it
  listByOwner(householdId, {after?, limit}) → {keys: [{key, bytes}], next?}   // sweep, household leave, quota checks
}
  • Files adapter (built now). Root: Recipe's data folder, beside recipes.db. Layout: blobs/<household>/<aa>/<bb>/<64-hex> (two levels of fan-out by the hash's first four hex chars) plus uploads/<slot id> for unshrunk uploads waiting for the derive queue. put writes to blobs/.tmp/, fsyncs, checks the hash, then renames into place (atomic on one filesystem). Owner and content type live in the photo row; stat reads size and time from the file. listByOwner walks blobs/<household>/. Files are mode 0640, owned by the container's app user (1000:1000).
  • Host folder. The server's Docker is the snap package, which can bind-mount only under /home. So the data folder is a bind mount such as /home/data/recipe → /data in the container (as Kitchie's /home/data/kitchie), or stays the named recipe-data volume it is today; either way the blobs live next to recipes.db. Never a path outside /home. Moving from the volume to a bind mount is infra's step and carries blobs/ with it.
  • Future adapter: online object storage (old option C; for example the cloud provider's object store). Named, not implemented. It would map key to an object name <household>/<64-hex>, read to a ranged GET, listByOwner to a prefix listing. It slots in by configuration (RECIPE_BLOBS=files|object) with no schema change: rows hold keys, never paths or URLs. Migration is a copy of every key (listByOwner then put) followed by a switch; bytes are still served by Recipe's photo route (P-D15), never by public object URLs.
  • Conformance tests run against every adapter: put twice is one blob; a wrong hash is refused; a partial write never becomes visible; delete then exists is false; a range read returns the right bytes.
The background derive queue (P-D7 C) Locked: phone first, server when free
  • When it runs. Only for uploads that arrived unshrunk (an assistant upload; a phone that could not shrink). A phone-made photo goes straight to ready.
  • Jobs and order. A job is (photo id, size) in a SQLite table, worked one at a time by a single worker inside Recipe's process. Priority, highest first: (1) a banner's square; (2) every other photo's square; (3) the banner's large; (4) every other medium. Within a priority, oldest first. The photo is ready for lists once its square exists and for the page once its big size exists.
  • Only when resources are free. The worker runs at the lowest CPU priority (nice 19), one image at a time, with the image library limited to one thread and about 256 MB. Before each job it checks load: it waits (backs off 30 s, doubling to 10 minutes) while the 1-minute load average is above 1.0 on the 2 vCPU server, or while Recipe has served a request in the last 2 seconds. It never runs inside a request.
  • Done. Each made size is put through the port, its hash recorded, then the upload is deleted once every kept size exists. A job that fails three times marks the photo failed; the UI then says "This photo could not be prepared" with Remove.
  • The seam for later. The worker calls a Deriver interface: derive(uploadKey, {size, crop?}) → {bytes, width, height}. Built now: a local adapter (sharp/libvips). Named for later, not built: a third-party image service adapter, chosen when the system scales. The queue, priorities and rows do not change.
MCP tools for photos (P-D12, P-D15) Proposal

request_photo_upload In: {target: {version_id, kind: "banner"|"step"|"more"} | {cook_id | cook: "tonight", version_id}, step_id?, filename, content_type, width, height, bytes, sha256, replace_photo_id?}. Out: {slot_id, upload: {method: "PUT", url, header}, expires_in: 900, max_bytes, attempts_left, blocking: false, retry: [{long_edge: 2000, quality: 85}, {long_edge: 1400, quality: 80}, {long_edge: 1000, quality: 75}], instructions}. Errors: limit (with which: banner, step_10, cook_3, household_2gb), invalid, not_found.

Description for the model: "Save a photo the person gave you to a recipe version, a step or a cook. You get an upload slot yourself; do not ask the person to do anything. This is NOT a blocking step: carry on with the recipe work and tell the person the photo is on its way. Send the picture with one HTTP PUT to upload.url with upload.header. First attempt: long edge 2000 px, JPEG or WebP quality 85. If it fails, try again smaller (1400 px, quality 80), then smaller again (1000 px, quality 75). Use at most attempts_left attempts. After the last failure stop, tell the person the photo was not saved and offer to try later or to add it in Kitchie. When the PUT succeeds, check the acknowledgement: received.sha256 and received.bytes must equal what you sent (matches: true). Tell the person in one line that it was saved. Never send a web address; Recipe never fetches one."

The acknowledgement (the PUT's 201 body, also returned by get_upload {slot_id}): {photo_id, received: {sha256, bytes, width, height, content_type}, matches: bool, will_keep: [{size, width, height}], derive: "queued"|"ready", attached_to, attempts_left}. A mismatch is 422 mismatch with both hashes, and nothing is stored.

get_photo In: {photo_id, size?: "t"|"m"|"l"} (default the square; a size the photo does not keep falls back to the nearest kept). Out: MCP image content plus {photo_id, kind, size, width, height, bytes, state, attached_to, added_by, added_at}. Same household check as the app, through the same Platform sign-in as the person's Kitchie session; pending returns state only. Description: "See a photo of this household's recipe or cook. Use the square to identify it; ask for the big size only if the person wants detail."

Example (assistant.html, scene 1): the person shows tonight's ragù → request_photo_upload (blocking false) → record_cook with the pending slot → the assistant says "the photo is on its way" → PUT 2000 px → 201 with matches: true → "Photo saved; Recipe received exactly what I sent". Scene 2 shows three smaller failures and the stop. Scene 3 shows get_photo.

How a photo travels
1Pick. Phone camera or library in Kitchie's add screen (its own route), or the person shows it to their assistant.
2Shrink on the phone (the default): banner to 1600px + square, anything else to 960px + square; WebP; location gone. An assistant instead sends one picture at 2000px or less.
3Send to Recipe with the sign-in (app) or the one-time slot (assistant). Recipe checks household, limits, type, dimensions and the hash.
4Store through the port. Blobs first (by hash), then the row. Phone-made: ready. Unshrunk: pending, two derive jobs queued, square first.
5Show. Replies carry ids, colour and state. Text paints first; photos load by phase (below); a pending photo shows "Photo is being prepared".
