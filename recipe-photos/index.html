Recipe photos

Draft for feedback, one slice of the Recipe tab (gap G7). Two halves: the architecture decisions to lock before anything is built (where the bytes live, sizes, uploads from the app and from an assistant, limits, versions, privacy, caching, deletion, backups), and a clickable phone mockup of the experience. Storage is not drawn: only what a person sees. Everything is a Proposal until the owner says yay, nay or combine, except what the owner locked below.

Owner's locked decisions applied (voice, 10 October 2026) Locked
  • L4 (R-O4): recipe content comes only through the person's own AI assistant over Recipe's MCP tools. No link paste, no scraping, no reading a photo or a link. The app's job is exactly two things: adding extra photos, especially "when they've made their meal", and notes ("notes is fine"). So: after-cook photos lead the add flow (P-D25), notes get their own screen (P-D26 to P-D29), and everything about fetching a photo from a web address is gone (P-D12's later part, P-D24, option P5 C, RP7, the web scene on the assistant screen, two open questions).
  • L5 (R-O5): no follower-versus-cook distinction; every member has the same controls. Anyone adds photos and notes, and removes their own (P-D17, P-D22). "Can someone who only follows the plan add cooked photos?" is closed: yes.
  • L8 (R-D31): putting away needs no approval. Nothing in this slice asks anyone's approval either.

Not locked, kept exactly as they were: storage options P1 to P13 (every option, recommendation and downside; only P5 C is gone, by L4), the tab bar, the editing screen, who draws the tab.

Reconciled 10 October 2026 with the other Recipe tab slices: the versions spec now carries photo_ids on a cook and cover_photo_id on a version, as P-D4 and P-D13 say; the remove rule (P-D17) is settled for this slice by L5 (equal roles). Build handoff: recipe-tab-handoff.md. Nothing has been built or filed.

Options for yay, nay or combine

Where the choice is real, each option is written with its downside. The recommendation is marked; the downside of the recommendation is said plainly.

P1Who owns the photos (P-D1)

A Recipe owns them. Bytes and their records live with Recipe's data; Kitchie only shows them by id. Matches "each app keeps its own persistence".

B A shared photo service in Platform for every product.

C Kitchie stores them, since Kitchie is the only screen.

Recommended A

Downside of A: Recipe, which today serves only JSON, gains a route that serves files and a folder that grows. If Kitchie later wants item photos it builds the same thing again for itself (B avoids that, at the cost of a new shared service everyone depends on).

P2Where the bytes sit on the server (P-D2)

A Files in a photos/ folder next to recipes.db, inside Recipe's existing data folder; the database holds one small row per photo.

B Inside SQLite as blobs in recipes.db. One file, always consistent.

C Tencent object storage (COS). Unlimited, off the server.

Recommended A

Downside of A: two things to keep in step (a row and its files). Handled by writing files first and the row second, and a nightly sweep for strays. B makes every database backup copy every photo again and bloats the database the app reads all day; C is a metered service, which breaks "pays for nothing".

P3What is kept per photo (P-D6)

A Three WebP sizes, no original: large 1600px (viewer), medium 960px (top of the page), square 240px (lists and galleries), plus a colour and a 24px blurred preview stored in the row.

B A plus the phone's original (3 to 6 MB each).

C Original only, resized on request.

Recommended A

Downside of A: the full-quality original is gone; a print or a much bigger screen later cannot get more detail than 1600px. B costs about 15 times the disk; C makes the 2 vCPU server resize on every view.

P4Who shrinks the photo (P-D7)

A The phone. Kitchie's add screen makes the three sizes in the browser before sending; Recipe checks them and stores. No image library on the server.

B The server with an image library (sharp/libvips).

C Both: the phone shrinks to 1600px, the server makes the other two.

Recommended A

Downside of A: the server has to trust bytes it did not make, so it checks strictly (real WebP, sizes and dimensions within caps) and cannot fetch and shrink a photo from a web link by itself (option 5C needs B or C). Changed by owner decision L4, 10 Oct 2026: that part of the downside no longer applies, since nothing fetches a web photo. B adds a native dependency, a bigger container and CPU spikes on a small server.

P5How an assistant adds a photo (P-D12)

A A one-time add link. The assistant calls request_photo_upload; the person taps the link and picks the photo in Kitchie, already aimed at the right recipe and cook. No picture goes through the chat.

B Picture data in the tool call (base64).

C A web address: the assistant passes an image URL (e.g. from a recipe page) and Recipe fetches it. Changed by owner decision L4, 10 Oct 2026: removed. Nothing fetches or reads a link or a photo.

Recommended A now; C later, for drafts only

Downside of A: one more tap for the person, and the assistant never sees the photo it "added". B is huge in tokens, capped by every assistant, and lets an assistant store any image without a person looking. C is a server that fetches addresses it is told to, which must be fenced off from the private network, and needs option 4B or 4C.

P6What a photo is attached to (P-D9)

A A version (its photos and one cover), or a cook (cooked-it photos). A version with no cover shows its parent's, labelled "Photo of With peas".

B The recipe as a whole: one gallery and one cover for every version.

C A revision: every fix keeps its own photos.

Recommended A

Downside of A: a new version that looks quite different still shows its parent's photo until someone adds one (the label says so). B shows the wrong dish for a version that changed a lot; C makes a photo vanish after a typo fix.

P7When nothing has a cover (P-D10)

A The parent's cover, then the newest cooked photo, labelled with its date; any photo can be made the cover with one tap.

B Only recipe photos can be covers; a cooked photo never leads.

C No covers: the newest photo of any kind leads.

Recommended A

Downside of A: a dark, messy dinner-table photo can become the face of the recipe without anyone choosing it. The date label tells everyone it is "what we made", not the ideal.

P8Who can see a photo (P-D15)

A Same-site, signed-in only. Images are plain addresses under /recipe/photos/… on the same site; Recipe checks the Platform sign-in cookie and the household on every request. No public links, no token in any address.

B Signed links that expire after an hour.

C Loaded by script with the token, shown as in-memory images.

Recommended A

Downside of A: it only works while Kitchie and Recipe are on one site (true today: one edge, path prefixes). An assistant cannot fetch a photo; nobody can share a photo link outside the household (by design). B links leak through copy and paste until they expire and change every hour, which defeats the browser cache; C defeats lazy loading and the cache.

P9Limits (P-D8)

A Tight: 6 recipe photos per version, 3 per cook, 1 MB per upload after shrinking, 2 GB per household, 60 uploads an hour per member.

B Roomy: 20 per version, 10 per cook, 5 GB per household.

C Only a household total.

Recommended A

Downside of A: someone who photographs every stage of a cook hits 3 quickly (step photos are a later piece). The caps are settings, so raising one is a config change, not a migration.

P10Removing a photo (P-D17)

A Hidden at once, gone for good after 30 days, with Undo and a "Removed photos" list; plus "Delete it for good now" for a photo that should never have been there.

B Gone at once, Undo for a few seconds only.

C Never deleted, like recipes.

Recommended A

Downside of A: the file stays on the server for 30 days, and in backups until they roll over, even though nobody can see it. C is not acceptable for photos: they can show people and homes.

P11Backups (P-D18)

A Same backup as the database: the photo folder is inside Recipe's data folder, so today's backup already includes it.

B Database as today, photos copied as new files only (a photo's file never changes, so only new ones are copied).

C No photo backups.

Recommended A now, B past about 500 MB

Downside of A: every backup copies every photo again, so backups grow quickly. Both A and B still sit on the same server: a lost disk loses both. Getting a copy off the server is an open question for infra, not this slice.

P12Photo at the top of the recipe page (P-D20)

A Full width, 195px tall, swipe for the version's other photos. Only the cover loads; the rest on first swipe.

B Small square beside the title (72px). Ingredients start 180px higher.

C None at the top; photos only in the Photos section.

Recommended A

Downside of A: ingredients start lower, and it is the biggest download on the page (one medium photo, about 60 to 90 KB). B is the pick if the page is used more to cook from than to choose.

ABC

P13Photos in the list (P-D21)

A The cover in the existing 56px tile, instead of the emoji. No row grows.

B No photos in lists: emoji only, nothing downloaded.

C A smaller 40px thumbnail and slimmer rows: more rows per screen.

Recommended A

Downside of A: each row on screen downloads a small thumbnail (about 10 to 15 KB). B is the zero-cost choice if the list is used mostly by searching.

ABC

N1Where a note lives (P-D26)

A Household note, always. Every note is the household's, on the recipe, shown on every version (R-D24). Written at the end of a cook it carries that cook as a tag (P-D29). No question asked.

B Ask at the end of a cook: "This cook only" (shows on that night's row in Cooked) or "For next time" (the household's, as A). From the recipe page it is always A.

Recommended A

Downside of A: a note that only made sense that night ("ran out of soy, used fish sauce") sits among the lasting notes; the cook tag and the cook's own one-line comment (P-D11) soften that. B adds a question to the quickest moment, and two kinds of note to explain. Added for L4, 10 Oct 2026: notes are the second UI job.

How a photo travels
1Pick. Phone camera or library, in Kitchie's add screen (a route of its own).
2Shrink on the phone. 1600, 960 and a 240 square, WebP; location data gone. A 4 MB photo becomes about 350 KB in all.
3Send to Recipe on the same site with the sign-in cookie. Recipe checks household, limits and that the files are real WebP of the right size.
4Store. Files into photos/<household>/, then the row. Colour and preview kept in the row.
5Show. Lists get the id and colour; the page gets the cover's preview too. The browser fetches the square or medium size only when it is about to be seen, and keeps it.
